# Milestone 1: The question, the rules, the scaffold
*2026-10-07 · stages: plan, pass-1 harvest, R0 · commits `c738dd9`, `ab115bb`*

## The question
Vox Day argues that population genetics rules out natural selection as the explanation for the human–chimp divergence. The argument is made in *Probability Zero* (2025), in a series of Zenodo papers built around a formula Day calls MITTENS, and in years of blog posts. The headline formula is:

```
F_max = (t_div × d) / (g_len × G_f)
```

It divides the generations available since the split by a measured "generations per fixation" taken from Lenski's E. coli long-term evolution experiment (LTEE). It then compares the result with the number of fixed differences between humans and chimps. Depending on the version, Day reports a shortfall of 220,000× to over 1,000,000×.

Critics, mostly writers on Substack, blogs and YouTube, say the maths is wrong. Their main points:
- The neutral substitution rate equals the mutation rate.
- Fixations run in parallel.
- Ancestral polymorphism inflates divergence.
- The bacterial rate does not transfer to humans.

Neither side has published in a peer-reviewed venue. Both mostly trade assertions.

The project's long-term goal is a simulator whose controls are the parameters this dispute turns on. Before writing any simulator code, the maintainer chose to run a research phase that maps and checks every claim on both sides.

## Decisions made at the start
- **Scope:** Day's full body of work from 2019 to the present, every critic who could be found, and the allies (Hössjer, Dembski, Tipler, Davis, Duffy).
- **The repo is the record:** markdown and check scripts. Status summaries are published alongside it.
- **Checks may be throwaway scripts,** but each must pass review before its result counts.
- **Equal depth and equal scrutiny** for both sides.

## The pass-1 survey
Three agents surveyed the territory read-only, working through summarizing web fetches. They found:
- About 120 blog posts and 39 Zenodo records under Day's name, all co-authored with "Claude Athos". 25 of the records are on population genetics.
- About 20 critic sources.
- 15 primary-literature works cited by Day.

From this survey came a first argument tree with eight branches, A to H:

| Branch | Claim |
|---|---|
| A | MITTENS rate limit |
| B | Neutral theory is insufficient |
| C | Ancient DNA |
| D | Sequence space |
| E | LTEE and punctuated equilibrium |
| F | Kimura's fixation-time point |
| G | The "Bernoulli barrier" |
| H | Cost of selection |

The survey also produced a first list of suspected citation problems on both sides.

**A deliberate limit:** none of the pass-1 numbers counted as recorded. Each had to be re-checked against the raw text in R1. The plan notes that the pass-1 assessments leaned toward the mainstream view. A balance ledger was set up to counter that lean.

## The rules
These were written before any result existed:
1. **Three verdicts per claim:**
   - *Internal:* does the conclusion follow from the author's premises?
   - *Fidelity:* does the cited source say that?
   - *External:* are the premises realistic?

   Simulations can settle the first two. Only data settles the third.
2. **Pinned definitions.** Most of the dispute turns on words that each side uses differently: fixation vs fixed difference, SNV vs base pair, N vs Nₑ, throughput vs latency.
3. **Numeric provenance.** Every number comes from `parameters.yaml`, a cited quote, or a stated derivation. A lint enforces this.
4. **No question-begging.**
   - Forward simulations decide disputed points; the coalescent is used only for baselines.
   - Scaling shortcuts are validated before they are used.
   - Exact Markov chains are used where the diffusion approximation is in doubt.
5. **Pre-registered predictions** for each side's model, written before any check runs.
6. **No full texts in git.** No Wayback snapshots, no contact with authors, and no posting without the maintainer's approval.

## R0: the scaffold
The R0 commit created:
- the `docs/research/` layout, with claims, opponents, ledgers, sources, a glossary, `parameters.yaml` and `hierarchy.yaml`;
- a claim template;
- a gitignored `sources/raw/`;
- a Python environment with numpy, scipy, sympy, msprime and tskit. fwdpy11 was used later for one cross-check; it is installed separately and is not in `requirements.txt`.
