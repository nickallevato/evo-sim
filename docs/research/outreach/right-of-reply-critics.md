## Template letter (edit; one per recipient)

**Subject:** A public audit of the mathematics in the Probability Zero debate: your claims, and a request for corrections

Dear [NAME],

I am writing on behalf of [OWNER NAME], who runs an open audit of the mathematical arguments in the debate over *Probability Zero* and the replies to it. I would like to give you the chance to correct what the audit says about you before it is finalised.

**What this is.** The audit is carried out by AI models (Claude instances) under the direction of one human owner. It reads the published arguments of both sides, tags each claim as argument or rhetoric, recomputes the numbers, and gives each claim three verdicts: whether it follows from its own premises (internal), whether it reads its sources correctly (fidelity), and whether it matches the outside evidence (external). The same rule is used for every author. It is not a peer review, no human expert has reviewed it, and it may be wrong. Its limits are set out here: [LINK: docs/research/criticality/METHODS-AND-LIMITATIONS.md]. All of it is public: [REPO LINK].

**What it says about your claims.** [N] claims of yours are in the audit. Findings in your favour and against you are listed below with their locators and the verdict on each. [PASTE the recipient's row and claim list; for each unfavourable verdict, name the quote, the locator, and the recomputation.]

**What I am asking.** Please tell us where the audit has misquoted you, misread you, used a number you did not use, chosen a reading of your words that is not the one you meant, or missed a point you made elsewhere. A short reply is enough. Corrections with a locator are the most useful.

**What will happen to your reply.**
- You have 21 days from [DATE SENT] to reply. Replies after that are still read and will be added, but the 21-day set is the one used for the first finalised version.
- Your reply will be published verbatim, next to the verdict it concerns, unless you ask us to withhold a specific private detail.
- A reply can change a verdict. If it does, the change and the reason will be recorded with the date, in the open.
- Silence is not treated as agreement. A claim with no reply is marked "no reply received" and its verdict stands as written, with the limits stated above.
- Nothing in your reply will be used in a way you were not told about, and you may withdraw a reply before publication.

You are under no obligation to respond. Thank you for your time.

[OWNER NAME] / [CONTACT PLACEHOLDER chosen by the owner]

## Reading the table

- Every claim of each person is listed, not only the ones with an unfavourable verdict, so that a recipient sees favourable and unfavourable findings together. The unfavourable ones to flag in each letter are those with internal `arithmetic-error` or `non-sequitur`, fidelity `misread`, or external `contradicted`. At the time of writing these are: Hancock/Gutsick Gibbon B5c (internal `non-sequitur`), Hössjer H5 (internal `non-sequitur`, load-bearing). External `contradicted` is not used for any critic claim in the list above; many critic claims are external `contested`, which means the audit found the question open or dependent on a premise Day disputes, not that the critic was wrong.
- Rhetoric tags (rule RH) change no verdict. Entries tagged this way (for example Hancock RH-15, Myers RH-20, Mansfield RH-18, as the status board records them) are listed in `ledgers/slips.md`; mention them in a letter as tags, not as errors.
- Hössjer, Dembski, Davis, Keen, Uncle John's Band and Tree of Woe are allies of Day in the audit's vocabulary (people on his side of the debate who are not him). Whether to write to them is the owner's choice; the rule treats allies separately from critics and never merges them.
- Felsenstein and Rosenhouse appear through a transcription of their material by Day or a third party (`secondhand`); say so in the letter, because the book has not been read.
