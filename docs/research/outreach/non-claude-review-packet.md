## PASTE FROM HERE

You are asked to do an independent, blind review of three computational checks of mathematical claims from a public debate about the mathematics of evolution (a book called *Probability Zero* by Vox Day, and replies to it). You have no access to any repository and should not search for one. You are not told what anyone concluded. Work from the text below only; if something you need is missing, say so and say what you would need. You may do arithmetic and write small code to verify numbers; anything you cannot verify, state as unverified.

**For each of the three checks, return the following form.**

1. *Correctness.* Any error in the method, the arithmetic, the use of the sources, or the reading of the results. Label each: BLOCKER (the result cannot stand), MAJOR (changes a number or a conclusion by more than a quarter), MINOR (does not).
2. *Steelman for the claim's author.* The strongest case that the quoted claim holds, or that the check does not touch it.
3. *Steelman for the critics of the claim.* The strongest case that the claim fails, or that the check understates how badly.
4. *Verdicts under the rule below,* each with reasons and a confidence (low, medium, high): internal; fidelity; external. If the verdict depends on how a quote is read, give every plausible reading and its verdict.
5. *What you could not determine.*

### The verdict rule (condensed)

Each claim gets three independent verdicts.
- **Internal:** does the stated conclusion follow from the author's own stated premises and model, ignoring whether the premises are true? Values: `holds`, `arithmetic-error`, `non-sequitur`, `pending`, `n/a`.
- **Fidelity:** are borrowed inputs cited and read correctly? `accurate` (cited and read correctly), `partial` (cited, read with a flagged omission or error), `misread` (the source text contradicts the reading), `unverifiable` (an empirical input stated without citation that is not standard, or cited but not retrievable), `pending`, `n/a` (no external input borrowed). Standard values need no citation: generation time 20 to 30 years, genome 3.1 to 3.2 Gb, human mutation rate 1.1 to 1.5e-8 per site per generation, about 60 to 100 de novo mutations per newborn. Human effective population size about 1e4 is exempt from citation but is flagged where the conclusion depends on it, because the author contests it; the flag goes in the external verdict, never in the internal one.
- **External:** does the claim match outside evidence and standard theory? `supported`, `contested`, `contradicted`, `untestable`, `pending`, `n/a`.

Clauses.
- **C, charity.** Before scoring a slip or mismatch, try the most charitable reading of each ambiguous referent (parts versus totals, per lineage versus pairwise, additive versus log scale, per haploid versus per diploid, spoken number versus caption). Record the reading tried. "Most charitable" means the reading under which the quoted statement is most defensible, i.e. closest to literally true.
- **R1, materiality.** Recompute the number or comparison with the correction, on the author's own basis (their unit, model, count). The slip is an error only if the correction moves the stated conclusion by more than 25%, or flips it, or moves a downstream number in the same source by more than 25%. Otherwise it is a minor slip with no verdict effect; 5% or less is rounding. A printed-number slip that is an error is `arithmetic-error`; an omitted term that is an error is `non-sequitur`. For steep outputs (tail probabilities, exponentials) an input stated as "about X" may not be loosened by 25% to rescue a number.
- **N, non-sequitur.** The conclusion is a `non-sequitur` if, with the author's stated premises and model: it fails when recomputed from the author's own table or equation; or it needs a premise the author's own text contradicts or excludes; or it rests on a circular match (fitted to the target); or it is stronger than the premises support. A disagreement about whether a premise is true is external, not a non-sequitur. A flagged hypothetical or illustration is scored on its arithmetic only.
- **S, scope.** Only the verbatim quoted statement and its derivation are scored; slips elsewhere in the same source are recorded but do not change the verdict.
- **RH, rhetoric.** First tag each quoted statement `dialectic` (a truth claim offered as an argument: a number, a derivation, an empirical assertion that carries a conclusion) or `rhetoric` (persuasion aimed at an audience: boast, hyperbole, taunt, irony, analogy used for effect). Rhetoric is not fact-checked literally and changes no verdict. Where a statement is unclear, ask whether it is remotely close to true (within a factor of 3, fixed in advance) under the most charitable reading when worked out, and report all plausible readings.
- **Pre-registered predictions:** the checks below state predictions made before the run. A prediction that is missed is reported, not hidden.

## CHECK 1 (of 3): waiting time for coordinated regulatory changes (id D15)

**Claim (verbatim; the author is a mathematical statistician, writing a review of a position paper, 2026-09-14, p.8).** "Although this mathematical model has not yet been applied to humans and chimps, my prediction is that the waiting time for several genes to change expression (so that their expressions match that of humans rather than chimps) far exceeds 9 million years." Stated premise, same page: "the intermediate steps of the evolutionary trajectory towards such an irreducibly complex (or close to irreducibly complex) target would most likely have low fitness." The window of 9 million years is 450,000 generations at 20 years per generation (the author's basis) or 360,000 at 25.

**The model the claim cites** (Hössjer, Bechly and Gauger 2021, J Theor Biol 524:110657). A haploid Moran population of N individuals and m genes. Each gene has a regulatory region of L nucleotides, and counts as done when a binding site of length W (K target words, up to d_max mismatches) is present. Mutation probability mu per nucleotide per generation; fitness depends on how many genes are done; back mutation allowed; fixed-state approximation; T_m = time until all m are done. The author's default (his Table 3): N = 1e4, L = 1000, m = 1, mu = 1e-8, W = 6, K = 1, d_max = 0, neutral. His Table 16 uses W = 10, K = 3, m = 5, "valley" intermediates of fitness 0.9999.

**The check.** A forward Wright-Fisher simulation (haploid, 2N_e copies, N_e = 1e4 or 1e5), validated against the author's tables and the fixed-state chain before use. Per gene: forward rate kmult x 1.458e-8 per genome per generation (kmult = 1 reproduces his default), back rate 5.30e-8. kmult is a redundancy axis: 1/12 (W = 8), 1 (default, W = 6 specific), 3 and 10 (any of 3 or 10 equivalent sites), 15 (one mismatch tolerated), 30 and 100 (many weak sites). Fitness depends on the number j of genes done: N (neutral); V4, V3, V2 (intermediates 1 - 1e-4, 1e-3, 1e-2; final fitness 1); Fin2, Fin3 (only the final state is beneficial, +1% or +0.1%); S2, S3 (stepping stones, each gene +1% or +0.1%; these are the check's own grid values, not from any participant). Recombination: none (rho = 0) or free (rho = 1). The start is all genes absent (a new feature, no standing variation). Grid: 714 cells, 60 replicates each, m = 1 to 4. Baselines reproduced before the sweep: the author's Tables 4, 5 and 9 to within 1 to 2%, and Durrett and Schmidt 2008's theorem (human case 8.66e6 generations) with the simulation ratio within tolerance. Statistic: p9 = fraction of replicates finishing within 9 My; p90 within 90 My. 28 cells (all N_e = 1e4) hit a wall-time guard, but their censoring clocks exceeded 90 My, so p9 and p90 are exact everywhere. Every N_e = 1e5 cell relies on a value of N_e that the claim's author disputes.

**Scoring convention used in the tables below.** "Exceeds 9 My" is read as p9 <= 0.05 and "far exceeds" as p90 <= 0.05. The check's pre-registered predictions are in its script docstring and are not reproduced here.

**Results.**
- In-scope set (m >= 2, kmult <= 1, neutral or valley intermediates; both N_e, both rho): 96 cells. p9 = 0 in all 96; maximum p90 = 0.017. Neutral and valley cells at kmult = 1 are mostly unfinished at 900 My (p900 = 0.48 at m = 1, 0.18 at m = 2, 0 at m = 3, 4 for N_e = 1e4).
- The other 618 cells relax one premise. Counts of cells with p9 <= 0.05 / p9 >= 0.5 / p90 <= 0.05 (25 y; N_e 1e4 / 1e5): neutral 42 / 47, 0 / 0, 20 / 27 of 49 each; V4 40 / 42, 0 / 0, 27 / 41 of 42 each; V3 and V2 all 42 each; Fin2 28 / 20, 13 / 21, 13 / 11 of 49 each; Fin3 31 / 27, 7 / 16, 14 / 12 of 49 each; S2 6 / 0, 36 / 39, 0 / 0 of 42 each; S3 13 / 6, 24 / 36, 6 / 0 of 42 each. Totals over 714: 470 with p9 <= 0.05, 192 with p9 >= 0.5.
- Stepping stones at kmult = 1: S2 p9 = 0.77 / 0.70 / 0.73 (m = 2, 3, 4; rho = 0; N_e = 1e4), 1.00 at N_e = 1e5. Final-only benefit with m = 1, kmult = 1: Fin2 p9 = 0.87 (N_e 1e4), 1.00 (1e5). Redundancy: Fin2 m = 2, N_e = 1e4, p9 = 0.05 / 0.12 / 0.33 / 0.68 / 1.00 at kmult 3 / 10 / 15 / 30 / 100. S2 at kmult >= 3: p9 = 1.00 everywhere.
- Stationary probability a site is already present per gene, from the author's formula: 0.022 (kmult 1/12), 0.216 (1), 0.45 (3), 0.73 (10), 0.81 (15), 0.89 (30), 0.97 (100).
- The author's own Table 5, W = 6, d_max = 1: E(T_1) = 4.5271e4 and E(T_2) = 9.0758e4 generations (against 5.3813e7 and 2.0518e8 at d_max = 0); reproduced to 0.2%. From a stationary start a matching site is present at t = 0 with probability 0.99 at d_max = 1. From an all-absent start the same chain gives E[T_1] = 4.57e6 generations (114 My at 25 y; P(T <= 9 My) = 0.076) and E[T_2] = 6.88e6 (172 My; P = 0.006).
- "Which genes": the grid requires one particular set of m genes. Fixed-state chain, W = 6, kmult = 1, neutral, all-absent start, origination times only: m = 2, specific set: 2.28e8 generations, P(<= 9 My) = 3e-5; any 2 of M = 100: 1.4e6 gens, 0.10; M = 1,000: 1.4e5, 0.97; M = 20,000: 6.9e3, 1.00. m = 4: specific 2.43e9, 7e-10; any 4 of 100: 2.95e6 (74 My), 0.002; of 1,000: 2.76e5 (6.9 My), 0.76; of 20,000: 1.37e4, 1.00. Under the author's own disjoint-subset formula, M = 20,000: m = 2 P = 0.24, m = 4 P = 4e-6, m = 5 P = 2e-8.
- Neutral fixation takes about 4N_e generations after origination (4e4 at N_e = 1e4; 4e5 at 1e5, longer than the 9 My window). The simulation includes it; the chain does not.
- Caveats the check lists: the grid stops at m = 4 and W <= 8, easier than the author's own Table 16 case (m = 5, W = 10, K = 3); the fitness convention depends only on the number done; the start condition is all-absent.

## CHECK 2 (of 3): transferring the LTEE fixation rate to humans (id A2e)

**Claim (verbatim; the author is the book's author; two papers, 2026).** "Whatever number the LTEE produces, it is the empirical ceiling on what evolution can accomplish when every tool in its kit is deployed simultaneously under ideal conditions." And: "The LTEE rate is therefore not an estimate for complex organisms. It is an unattainable ceiling, the absolute best-case scenario, the performance of a Formula One car used to benchmark a horse-drawn cart." (The second sentence's analogy is to be tagged under rule RH; "unattainable ceiling, the absolute best-case scenario" is the argument.) The author states the comparison per generation ("The objection implicitly claims that humans can fix mutations faster per generation than bacteria under ideal conditions"). A critic had replied that using the bacterial value is generous only per unit of time, not per generation (the critic's wording: it is "very generous for the estimation of X2 to use the A1 value as an estimate for A2 even though A1 isn't why 1/X1 is high"; units: per year versus per generation).

**Setup.** LTEE = a long-term experiment on E. coli. G_f = generations per fixation. The author's value: 1,322 (3.0 version) or 1,587 (2nd edition); 2,890 = the adaptive-only reading after subtracting the neutral expectation 4.1e-4 per genome per generation. Rate_human <= rate_LTEE = 1/G_f per generation is the claim. Transfer factor F = k_human / (1/G_f); the claim holds iff F <= 1.

**Method.** A clonal-class process (no recombination) is calibrated by simulation so the clonal rate equals 1/G_f at each (LTEE N_e, s, G_f); the clonal-interference factor R_int = (1/G_f) / (independent-sites rate). Then the human rate is computed with recombination (Weissman and Barton asymptote, W&B Eq. 1 and 8) as the beneficial supply is scaled by kappa = U_b,human / U_b,LTEE per haploid genome. Swept inputs: LTEE N_e (haploid copies) 3.3e6, 1e7, 3.3e7, 1e8 (3.3e7 = N0 log2(100), unsourced); s = 0.003, 0.01, 0.03; G_f 1,322 / 1,587 / 2,890; kappa = 1 (no scaling), 125 (mutation rate only, 1.25e-8 / 1e-10), 652 (genome length only, 3e9 / 4.6e6), 81,500 (both), 93,659 (the ratio from the author's stated factors: 3.2e9 x 1.2e-8 = 38.4 per haploid human genome against 4.1e-4 per LTEE genome; the author himself does not apply supply scaling); human N_e 3,300 or 1e4; recombination: clonal, map 1.5 M, map 36.8 M, free. Per year versus per generation also reported. Pre-registered at the script's first commit; nothing in the main run is post hoc.

**Results.**
- Adaptive-only model, uncapped: with kappa = 1, F <= 0.104 in every recombination mode. With kappa = 125: F = 0.04 to 13, below 1 at s >= 0.01 (0.19 to 0.58 at s = 0.01; 0.04 to 0.11 at s = 0.03) and above 1 at s = 0.003 (4.3 to 13). At kappa = 93,659: F = 7.3 to 8,970, every cell above 1, including humans treated as clonal (7.3 to 167).
- Capped by the author's own cost-of-selection figures (post hoc analysis of stored calibrations, closed forms): at kappa = 93,659, F = 1.98 (cap 1/667), 4.41 (Haldane 1/300), 6.4 to 7.1 (mean-field ln R / D at R = 1.111), up to 33.9 (cap 1/39). The caps do not move the flip kappa*.
- Flip kappa* (F = 1) on the coupled grid (same s in both systems): 3.9 to 13,050, central 217 (s = 0.003 / 0.01 / 0.03: 9.63 / 217 / 1,110 at the central calibration). With human N_e = 3,300, kappa* is 658 (3.03 times higher). The upper corner (13,050) needs G = 2,887 and N_e,human = 3,300. Closed form: kappa* is about (M_LTEE / 2N_h) x R_int,LTEE x (u_L / u_h), 220 against 217 at central. If the human beneficial effect is smaller than the LTEE's (s_L = 0.03 with s_h = 0.001), kappa* = 32,260 at central; 6 of 96 decoupled cells, and 2 of 144 on a wider grid, put kappa* above 93,659. kappa* is the same for the 1.5 M map, 36.8 M map and free recombination.
- Total-throughput basis (the author: "MITTENS measures total throughput"): taking the human total substitution rate as k = mu = 38.4 per haploid genome per generation against 1/1,322 gives F = 50,765 per generation (60,941 at 1,587). This needs no kappa or s, only whether the human neutral substitution rate is mu (separately disputed, and not tested here).
- Per year: under the recombining model F_yr <= 0.50; the total-throughput F_yr = 0.72 to 1.05 (1.047 / 0.837 / 0.722 at 20 / 25 / 29 y generation time). The author states the comparison per generation.
- Other pre-registered predictions: 1/R_int spans 107x across s (predicted >= 20x; met); LTEE N_e moves 1/R_int 2.81x at s = 0.01 but 8.07x at s = 0.003 (predicted <= 3x; missed at s = 0.003); G_f moves it 4.02x (predicted <= 2x; missed); kappa is the dominant input over 4.96 decades; the predicted input ranking was not borne out (observed: kappa 4.96 > s 1.90 > recombination 1.44 > LTEE N_e 0.98 > G_f 0.61 > human N_e 0.47 decades). The check's pre-registered direction for the author was F <= 1 for every plausible combination (per generation); for critics F > 1 whenever humans recombine and kappa >= 125. At kappa = 125 the second held only at s = 0.003. Tally: 13 of 19 itemised predictions met.
- The check's own caveats: all F > 1 results are predictions of a supply-limited model, not observations of a human rate; the model uses a single selection coefficient per system; the author does not himself apply supply scaling.

## CHECK 3 (of 3): slightly deleterious fixations on the human lineage (id HF, part HF-d only)

**Claims (verbatim).** The book's author, blog, 2026-01-11: "At observed mutation rates and population sizes, the drift model fixes roughly 7.6 harmful mutations per actual generation. Using standard estimates for the damage caused by each mutation, collapse occurs in 9 generations—about 225 years." (The same post's phrase "The Drift Deathmarch closes it with a corpse" is rhetoric: a vivid image whose argumentative core is the 9-generation arithmetic; do not fact-check it literally.) A mathematical statistician on the same side (review, p.6): "so that the overall fitness of the population declines over time and the population eventually (and quite rapidly) dies."

**Method.** Kimura's fixation flux for new deleterious mutations, integrated over a gamma distribution of fitness effects (DFE) from Boyko et al. 2008: shape 0.184 (as quoted in Kim et al. 2017), mean |s| 0.029, heterozygote effect s, homozygote 2s. U_d (new deleterious mutations per haploid genome per generation) in {0.3, 1, 3}; U_d = 3 is the upper end (8% constrained x 3.1e9 sites x 1.2e-8, applying the nonsynonymous DFE to all constrained sites, an extrapolation). N_e in {1e4, 2e4}; T = 252,000 generations; exact back mutation only (at mu/3). Scenarios: S1 start at the optimum; S2 start at the mutation-selection-drift equilibrium at the same N_e; S3 start at equilibrium at the ancestral N_e = 2.06e5 then run at N_e for T; S0 selection switched off (the author's hypothesis). Sanity check: the fitted gamma puts 28.0% of mutations at |s| < 1e-4 (source: "tightly clustered around 28%"). Validation by per-site Wright-Fisher (N = 1000, 400,000 new mutations per 2Ns): 6 of 7 points inside the pre-registered 99% band; at 2Ns = -0.5, simulated 2.08e-4 against Kimura 2.91e-4 (z = -3.10); the neutral point is also low (4.10e-4 against 5.00e-4, z = -2.55, inside the band). Pre-registered, with the script's docstring holding the predictions.

**Pre-registered predictions and results (shape 0.184, N_e = 1e4).**

| U_d | S1 substitutions per lineage | S1 log-fitness decline (sum of 2s) over 252,000 generations | S1 per generation | S2 net | S3 net decline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.3 | 16,800 | 0.25 | 0.067 | about 0 (< 1e-18) | 0.24 |
| 1 | 55,900 | 0.82 | 0.22 | about 0 | 0.80 |
| 3 | 168,000 | 2.47 | 0.67 | about 0 | 2.39 |

- Sensitivity: N_e = 2e4 roughly halves the decline (U_d = 3: 1.09 in S1, 1.01 in S3). Shape 0.1 gives 2.54 / 2.44; shape 0.3 gives 1.77 / 1.73 (U_d = 3, N_e = 1e4). Everything scales linearly in U_d.
- S0 (selection off): harmful substitutions per generation = 0.75 x U_total = 28.8 at U_total = 38.4. The author's 7.6 implies U_total = 10.1. With selection on (S1), the rate is 0.67 per generation at U_d = 3: 43 times lower than S0 and 11 times lower than 7.6. The factor 43 is 9.6 from the 75%-harmful premise times 4.5 from selection; the ratio of the declines, selection off to on at U_d = 3, is about 1.8e4. The per-generation log-fitness decline under S1 is about 1e-5.
- Pre-registered predictions: PD1 S1 substitutions 3e4 to 3e5 and decline 0.3 to 5 at U_d = 3 (observed 1.68e5, 2.47); PD2 S2 net below 0.01 in absolute value (observed about 0); PD3 S3 net decline 0.1 to 2 (observed 2.39 at U_d = 3, N_e = 1e4, above the band; 0.80 at U_d = 1); PD4 S1 rate at most 1/10 of S0 at U_d = 3 (observed 1/43); PD5 validation passes at every 2Ns (6 of 7). A further pre-registered side-by-side: the author-side prediction was an S1 decline above ln 3 = 1.1 (observed 2.47 at U_d = 3; 0.82 at U_d = 1; 1.09 at U_d = 3 with N_e = 2e4); the critic-side prediction was S2 net about 0 and S0 more than 10 times S1 (observed).
- The check's stated limits: the DFE is extrapolated; the only return route is exact back mutation (no compensatory mutations at other sites, no beneficial substitutions, soft selection or epistasis); at equilibrium the net decline is zero, and S3 shows that a decline of N_e from about 2e5 to 1e4 puts the population out of equilibrium for longer than 252,000 generations (relaxation about 1/mu).
