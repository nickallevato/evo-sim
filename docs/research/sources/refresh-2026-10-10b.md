### What it bears on
A2e's flip is "the human lineage fixed about 191 adaptive substitutions or fewer" (R4-A2e.md, PH8). Published coding-sequence alpha values put coding-only counts from about 20-40 (0.2%) to about 1,100-2,200 per lineage (Boyko 10-20%, Uricchio 13.5%), and up to 40% (Eyre-Walker and Keightley, a bound conditional on an assumed Ne decline). So the threshold is inside the published range, not outside it, and which side a given study lands on is for the A2e owner to map; this refresh does not assign it. Caveats on both sides: (a) these are amino-acid substitutions, not the beneficial-mutation count Day's s7.3 "191 achievable" refers to; regulatory and other non-coding adaptive substitutions are not counted (Keightley 2005 argues about hominid non-coding constraint; numbers not extracted); (b) alpha estimates are downward-biased by slightly deleterious variants (Eyre-Walker and Keightley, Gayà-Vidal both say so) and by demographic change, which is why the published range is wide; (c) "adaptive" in MK means fixed by positive selection, which includes weakly beneficial changes, which Uricchio puts at 72% of the adaptive share. A critic can read the 1,100+ figures as showing human adaptation is not bounded by ~191; a Day-side reader can read 0.2% (about 20-40) and Galtier's "notable exception of primates" as showing human adaptive fixation is low.

## 3. A sourced human beneficial-mutation DFE
- **Not found as such.** Boyko 2008 states that they "did not look directly at the distribution of positively selected amino acid mutations" (quote above), modelling beneficial mutations at fixed gamma+ of 5, 25 or 100 (Fig. 4 legend) with the adaptive share between about 9% and 20%.
- Uricchio 2019 gives the best available substitute: alpha = 0.135, 72% from weakly adaptive variants (abstract). This is a split of fixed adaptive substitutions by strength, not a DFE of new beneficial mutations; the strength cut-offs are in the full text, not retrieved.
- Kim, Huber and Lohmueller, Genetics 2017 (PMC5419480) is the large-sample deleterious DFE and says: "Note that here we only consider the deleterious DFE". Tataru et al., Genetics 2017 (PMC5676230) estimates DFE and alpha jointly; its human numbers were not located by grep and were not read.
- Lin et al. 2025 (phylogenetic DFE variation across animals) exists in Europe PMC (preprint record), not read.
- Bearing for H follow-ups: any "beneficial fraction x effect size" input (A2e) has no direct human measurement; Day-side can note that the best human data point to low alpha and weak effects; critic-side that a 72% weak share means most fixations are of small s, which is what a high U_b needs.

## 5. Branch D: functional-sequence prevalence
- **Axe 2004**, J Mol Biol 341:1295 (PMID 15321723). Abstract (verbatim): "roughly one in 10(64) signature-consistent sequences forms a working domain"; combined estimate "may be as low as 1 in 10(77)". Method per abstract: beta-lactamase, clusters of ten side chains replaced randomly "within the boundaries of the signature", "four such experiments", "low-level function". Full text paywalled; not read. Locator: abstract.
- **Keefe and Szostak 2001**, Nature 410:715 (PMID 11287961; PMC4476321 is an author manuscript, not in the OA XML subset, efetch gives only a stub). Abstract: "Starting from a library of 6 x 1012 proteins each containing 80 contiguous random amino acids" (exponent flattened in the source: 6 x 10^12); "This selection yielded four new ATP-binding proteins". **No frequency is in the abstract.** The commonly cited ~1 in 10^11 is therefore **unverified** here; do not use it until the text is read.
- **Taylor et al. 2001**, PNAS 98:10596 (PMID 11535813; PMC58511, full text not in the OA subset). Abstract: "approximately 80% of the protein originates from the simplified modules and >90% of the protein consists of only eight different amino acids". No probability figure in the abstract.
- Two-sided reading: Axe's number is a sequence-space fraction for one function and a specified fold with low-level activity; critics' standard responses (many folds and functions, function is a continuum, starting-point choice) are not tested by the abstract. Keefe-Szostak is the counter-example type on the Day-critic side (functional proteins in random libraries) and Taylor shows heavy sequence simplification tolerated; Axe's own abstract describes weak function as sufficient for the count. Needs the user: PDFs of all three (publisher access).
