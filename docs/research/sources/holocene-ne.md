# Holocene effective population size of Europe: literature retrieval

Retrieved and read 2026-10-09 (branch `research`). Fills the gap recorded in `research/checks/results/R4-C1c.md` ("Holocene-Ne retrieval gap"). Related claims, read but not edited: C4 (Holocene Ne varied), C5b (keruru's temporal Ne), B2e (measured Ne vs Wright's formula). Bibliography keys below are in `bib-literature.md`; every quote is in `quotes-literature.md` under "Holocene Ne retrieval (2026-10-09)".

**What this file is.** A sourced table of published European or West Eurasian Ne estimates for roughly the last 10,000 years, grouped by method, with a summary of range, method dependence and what it means for C1c's flip point. Nothing was run on the C1c model. Numbers labelled **derived** are my arithmetic on published parameters (scripts are not committed; the formulas are stated), not published values.

**Rules followed.** Full texts were fetched into `sources/raw/holocene-ne-2026-10-09/` (gitignored, read only, 37 papers; sha256 in `bib-literature.md`). Open-access or free-to-read full text only; no paywall bypass; items that could not be read are listed in section 8 with abstract-level claims marked `secondhand`. Every quote below was machine-checked as a substring of the saved text (Unicode-normalised; dashes and superscript-lost exponents may differ from print). Where a number exists only in a figure, the row says "figure only" and no value is read off it.

## 1. Summary

1. **Range by method** (details in section 4):
   - Early to mid Holocene (about 10 to 3 kya), the fitted values cluster at **roughly 1.5k to 12k**: 1,530 (IBS tracts, until 5.9 kya), about 5,000 (ROH, early Neolithic Germany), 5,633 (SFS, until about 3.5 kya), 7,700 (SFS, until 1.4 kya; this one is a fixed input), 7k to 12k at 10 to 7 kya (SFS exponential, derived), and 7.8k to 10.5k (temporal F, a lower bound).
   - Present-day values from the same methods span **nearly four orders of magnitude** (3.7): 35,900 (SFS, 1000 Genomes pilot CEU), 0.65M to 1.1M (SFS), 4.0M (SFS, 14,002 people), 8M to 27M (IBD, UK), 169M (IBD, UK Biobank, extrapolated and above census).
   - Two results sit outside the cluster: Nelson et al. 2012 (growth of 1.7% per generation from about 9.3 kya; the start time is secondhand from a review table) and Schaffner et al. 2005 (a step to 100,000 at 350 generations ago, which is a fixed model input that "had little effect on results").
2. **Constant ~1e4, or growth to 1e5 to 1e6?** Every method that can see the present says Ne grew by at least one to two orders of magnitude, so a constant 1e4 to the present is not supported by any retrieved estimate. For the **timing**, the weight of the retrieved fitted models puts most of the growth after about 3.5 kya: Ne crosses 1e5 about 0.7 kya (Coventry), 1.4 kya (Gazave), and about 1.25 kya (IBD, UK, via a review). Nelson's central trajectory crosses 1e5 at about 5.4 kya (derived; 4.0 to 7.7 kya over the growth-rate CI). Direct aDNA evidence (Ttne, HapNe, Relate, short-ROH, IBD sharing) shows expansion but gives no absolute value in the text. So the literature is closer to a picture of about 5k to 10k for most of the window followed by growth in the last 1 to 3.5 ky than to one of 1e5 to 1e6 by 7 kya, but one fitted study and one fixed model input point the other way, and no study measures the drift Ne of a pooled European population over 7 ky directly.
3. **Method dependence is large** (section 5): IBD-based methods only see back about 50 generations (SNP data) to 200 generations (sequence data), i.e. at most about 1.25 to 5 ky, and give the largest recent values; SFS growth models depend on a fixed or assumed ancient Ne, a single-epoch growth form, sample size and mutation rate; coalescent HMMs resolve the Holocene poorly and two of them disagree (MSMC: "relatively constant" 2 to 10 kya; SMC++: growth starting 10 to 15 kya); temporal F (keruru, C1d) estimates a harmonic-mean Ne and is a lower bound.
4. **Implication for C1c** (section 7, stated both ways):
   - If the Ne that matters is about 5k to 25k through the window (Gazave, Coventry, Gravel, and the temporal-F values all give a harmonic mean of about 9k to 25k over 0 to 7 kya, derived), C1c's model gives 908 to 3,925 events, so Day's 21 is a deficit of roughly 40x to 190x on the model (the 2e4 and 1e4 cells).
   - If Ne followed a Nelson-type trajectory (harmonic mean over 0 to 7 kya about 1.6e5, derived), C1c's flip region (about 1.4e5 closed; 32 to 35 events for its growth and step cells) is reached and 21 needs no clock failure.
   - The retrieval does not decide between these.

## 2. How to read the table

- **Window** is the time span the estimate speaks to, or the span the method can resolve. Ne values at times outside it are not claimed.
- **Quote ids** point to `quotes-literature.md`.
- **Access** vocabulary: `OA full XML` = Europe PMC full-text XML; `free PMC HTML` = the publisher disallows XML download, the article page on PMC is free to read and was saved; `secondhand` = not read in the original, claim taken from a review that was read.
- Generation times follow each paper (25 y for the SFS papers, 29 y for SMC++, 30 y for MSMC, 29 y for HapNe).

## 5. Range by method and how much it depends on method

| Method family | What it sees | Early to mid Holocene (10 to 3 kya) | Late Holocene (3 kya to present) | Present |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IBD, modern samples (IBDNe, HapNe) | last 50 to 200 generations (about 1.25 to 5 ky); strongest in the last 30 | not resolved (one third-party conversion: oscillates 75k to over 4M at 3.9 to 1.4 kya, low confidence) | about 0.1M at 1.25 kya, 8M at 4 generations ago (UK); qualitative expansion (HapNe) | 27M to 169M (UK) |
| IBD, ROH and genealogy, ancient samples | each sample's last 10 to 50 generations; one site at a time | about 5,000 (LBK ROH); 382 (Malta, isolate); rising Ne qualitatively across the Neolithic (short ROH, IBD sharing, Relate) | CW about 3-fold in 1 ky; Britain growing to the 14th century; Viking-age expansion | n/a |
| Coalescent HMM, 1000 Genomes | few haplotypes; resolution weak in the Holocene | MSMC: "relatively constant" 2 to 10 kya; SMC++: rapid growth starting 10 to 15 kya (values in figures only); IBS tracts: 1,530 until 5.9 kya | MSMC: rapid increase since 2 kya | not stated in text |
| SFS growth models | rare-variant excess, i.e. recent growth, plus an assumed or fixed ancient Ne | 1.0k to 12k fitted (Gazave 5.6k; Coventry 7.7k fixed; Gravel 7k to 12k derived; Nelson 7k at 9.3 kya derived) vs 100,000 fixed (Schaffner) | growth starts 3.5 kya (Gazave), 1.4 kya (Coventry) or earlier (Tennessen 5.1 kya, Nelson 9.3 kya, Chen 7.3 kya secondhand) | 36k (Gravel) to 4.0M (Nelson) |
| LD | recombination distance | CEU ancestral about 2,500 (before 20 kya) | "dramatic" increase | not stated in text |
| Temporal F (keruru, C1d) | harmonic mean Ne over a window; includes non-drift F | 7.8k to 10.5k (lower bound) | same windows | n/a |

**Sources of method dependence, with what the retrieved texts say:**
- **Sample size and the age of the variants used.** A review states the case directly: "The present effective population size of Europeans as estimated by the model is 1.1 million (95% CI, 0.3 to 1.9 million) (18), compared to the present effective population size of only a few tens of thousands estimated by previous models that incorporated recent exponential growth" [Keinan2012, Main text] Models fitted to small samples give 0.2% to 0.7% growth over 20 to 30 ky; large samples give 5% to 14% over 0.9 to 2.8 ky. The present-day spread (36k to 169M, 3.7 orders of magnitude) is largest where one method's window ends and another's extrapolates.
- **A fixed or assumed ancient Ne.** Coventry's 7,700 and Schaffner's 100,000 are inputs. Gazave et al. refit the pre-growth size and got 5,633, "markedly lower than the fixed value of 10,000", and found no support for an earlier, milder epoch of growth ("equivalent to an epoch of constant population size"), while noting limited power.
- **IBD methods extrapolate at both ends.** They need many shared segments; Cai et al. 2023 and Browning 2015 both warn that the most recent generations are extrapolated and may be too high, and the 169 million figure exceeds the UK census. Huang et al. 2025 state that Ne beyond about 50 generations is "the result of regularization". IBD detection errors raise IBD-based Ne relative to LD-based Ne (Fournier et al.).
- **Census versus effective size.** Gazave et al. suggest that effective size "increases extremely slowly with the census population size, at least initially"; Gravel et al. find present Ne (35,900) "significantly lower than census sizes"; Browning finds Ne/census of about one third for the UK. Census growth in the Neolithic is therefore not an estimate of Ne growth.
- **Which population.** The SFS studies use European Americans or CEU; IBD uses UK samples; aDNA IBD uses single sites or cultures; the coalescent Ne of a present-day sample traces its ancestors, including admixture sources, and is not the drift Ne of a pooled continental frequency vector (C1c's model). Structure and replacement push in different directions; this retrieval does not quantify them.
- **Scaling.** All Ne values scale inversely with the mutation rate and with the generation time assumed (SMC++: mu 1.25e-8, 29 y; MSMC: 1.25e-8, 30 y; Nelson estimated a median 1.38e-8 per bp per generation; Schaffner 1.5e-8). C1c found 25 y per generation lowers S21 to 0.6x to 0.7x.
- **Temporal F.** Reads as a lower bound (R4-C1d); under the cumulative-F formula the sampling correction for pseudo-haploid data is 1/n0 + 1/nt, which moves keruru's two numbers up 8% to 24%.

## 6. Constant ~1e4 or growth to 1e5 to 1e6?

Stated as the retrieval shows it, not as a verdict on either side:
- **Supports about 1e4 or below for most of the window:** Gazave (5,633 until about 3.5 kya, two-epoch acceleration not supported), Coventry (7,700 until about 1.4 kya, a fixed input), Gravel (7k to 12k at 10 to 7 kya, derived), Harris & Nielsen (1,530 until 5.9 kya), Tenesa (about 2,500 before 20 kya), LBK ROH (about 5,000), MSMC (CEU "relatively constant" 2 to 10 kya), and the temporal-F numbers (7.8k to 10.5k, lower bound). Several of these are consistent with each other within a factor of about 2 to 3. This is the Day-side reading of C1c's question and it is also keruru's.
- **Supports growth well before 3 kya:** Nelson (1.7% per generation from about 9.3 kya, to 4.0M), Tennessen (1.95% over 5.1 ky), Chen via the review (1.49% from 7.3 kya), Gao & Keinan (2.2% from 4.95 kya), SMC++ (growth from 10 to 15 kya), Relate and short-ROH and IBD sharing (increasing local size from the Mesolithic onward), Ttne (3-fold in 1 ky from 5.5 kya), Schaffner (a fixed step to 100,000 at 8.75 kya). This is the critic-side reading. Only Nelson, Chen and Schaffner reach 1e5 before about 5 kya, and Schaffner's is an input.
- **Not supported by any retrieved estimate:** a constant 1e4 to the present, and a trajectory at or above 1e5 to 1e6 for the whole of 7 to 0 kya. C1c's growth cell (1e4 to 1e6, exponential) is not far in shape from Nelson's central trajectory in its first half and slower than it afterwards (derived table, section 4).
- **The least constrained interval is 3 to 7 kya,** exactly where C1c's S21 events accumulate: IBD methods have run out of signal, SFS models extrapolate a parametric form, coalescent HMMs have few coalescences there, and aDNA IBD and ROH give directions without absolute values.

## 7. What this means for C1c's flip point (two-sided)

C1c's conditional verdict (R4-C1c point 6): the Day-side reading (the 21 is a deficit) holds at Ne up to about 5e4 and under strong replacement; the critic-side reading (21 is neutral-compatible) needs closed-population Ne >= about 1e5, or growth or step schedules reaching 1e5 to 1e6 within the window. The retrieval gives:

**If the flatter fitted trajectories are the better description** (Gazave, Coventry, Gravel, Harris & Nielsen, the temporal-F values):
- the harmonic-mean Ne over 0 to 7 kya is about 9k to 25k, i.e. in the range of C1c's constant-Ne cells at 1e4 (3,925 events) and 2e4 (908), and about 6x to 23x below the harmonic means of the cells that give 32 to 35 events;
- Day's 21 would sit about 40x to 190x below the model's neutral expectation, so the reading that the 21 is a deficit holds on the model, subject to C1c's other caveats (one model family, no error term, no selection). C1d adds that the literal statistic gives thousands on the real data and that a transversion-only count (36 to 113) is the same order as 21, which is a separate matter from Ne;
- keruru's 8k to 10k is then not biased low by a large factor; it is within about 2.6x of what the SFS models predict for the same windows (section 4). That agrees with his reading and with the textbook value Day says should not be treated as constant.

**If a Nelson-type trajectory is the better description** (growth from the early Neolithic, harmonic mean about 1.6e5 over 0 to 7 kya; Ne above 1e5 from about 5.4 kya):
- the C1c cells with comparable harmonic means (growth 1e4 to 1e6, step) give 32 to 35 events, within about 1.5x to 1.7x of 21, so no clock failure is needed to produce 21;
- keruru's measured Ne (8k to 10k) would then be a harmonic mean dominated by 90% or more non-drift F (section 4). C1d did not exclude that (same-time spatial F is comparable to the whole temporal F), but it did not demonstrate it either;
- strong ancestry replacement, which C1c shows keeps S21 above 21 at Ne 1e6, would still need to be addressed on this branch.

**What is not decided:**
- which of the two branches is right for the pooled European drift Ne, which is the quantity C1c's model uses;
- how structure and replacement change the mapping from a coalescent or SFS Ne to the drift Ne of the pooled frequency vector;
- whether late growth alone (Gazave, Coventry: after 1.4 to 3.5 kya) matters for events dated 5 to 6 kya and younger, which C1c's harmonic-mean heuristic does not capture (the heuristic comes from three cells and is not a tested rule).

**Candidate follow-ups (not done here; not mine to schedule):**
1. Run the C1c model with the Gravel, Gazave II, Coventry and Nelson trajectories (the derived table gives their values at 20 y or 25 y per generation; they need regridding to 525 generations) and report S21, eligible and the ratio, as new cells next to the existing ones.
2. Add the three early-Holocene anchors (1,530 until 5.9 kya; about 5,000 at 7 kya; 7.8k to 10.5k temporal) as the flat branch in C1c and the Nelson trajectory as the growth branch, instead of the 1e4 / 1e5 / 1e6 schedules, which are not tied to a source.
3. `parameters.yaml`: `population.Ne_modern_human` 1.0e4 ("textbook, unverified") could carry the retrieved range as a note; I did not edit it.

## 9. Who this helps

**Day and allies.**
- Every fitted trajectory that resolves the early Holocene puts Ne at 1.5k to 12k, not at 1e5 or above, and the flatter models' harmonic means over the C1c windows (9k to 25k) are within a factor of about 2.6 of keruru's temporal numbers, so the textbook scale is the better-sourced one for most of the window in this retrieval.
- The literature agrees with the C4 point that Ne is not constant (growth to at least 1e5 by the present in every study that sees the present), and Day's point that Ne should not be treated as constant survives; the disagreement is only about magnitude and timing.
- The largest-growth estimates are model-dependent in ways the papers themselves state (sample size, a fixed ancient Ne, extrapolation), and the one study that gives early large growth (Nelson) has its timing only secondhand here.

**Critics.**
- No retrieved study supports a constant 1e4 to the present; every method that sees the present shows growth of two or more orders of magnitude, and several aDNA methods show expansion within the Neolithic to Bronze Age.
- A Nelson-type trajectory (and Schaffner's fixed step) puts the harmonic mean Ne in C1c's flip region; keruru's lower-bound estimates do not exclude it (non-drift F can be large, C1d); and Day's 21 is not reproduced from his stated method on either the model or the real genotypes (C1c, C1d).
- Cuts against critics: the flatter models are the more numerous and are mutually consistent; the growth sources that start earliest are the least well pinned (secondhand timing, a fixed input, a figure-only curve); and one third-party-converted IBD curve for the UK oscillates between 75k and over 4M, which is not a smooth growth path. The 1e5 to 1e6 within-window schedules that C1c uses for the critic-side cells are not tied to a retrieved source in the form it uses them.

## 10. Caveats

- Section 4 values are arithmetic on published parameters with stated simplifications (single exponential, flat before the start, 25 y per generation, windows approximated); they are not published results and carry no uncertainty beyond the sensitivity row.
- The harmonic-mean comparison with C1c's cells rests on three cells and is a heuristic.
- Estimates come from different populations (European Americans, CEU, UK, German LBK), so none is a pooled-Europe drift Ne.
- Several rows are figure-only; direction is recorded where the text states it and no value is read from a figure.
- Quotes are verbatim but short; their context (model assumptions, caveats) should be checked in the paper before reuse.
