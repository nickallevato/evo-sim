## 4. Balance notes

**B1. Tally and labels.** The primary-beneficiary counts add up (two-sided 3, critic-leaning 2, Day-leaning 1, neither 1). The "any credit" counts are consistent apart from GAP-06, which is labelled "neither" but whose text says it is "mildly favourable to the critics' B6 decomposition if the fit closes"; credit it to critics or drop the sentence. The statement that the three top gaps each contain a Day-favourable component holds on the page, but see B2 and B3 for how thin that component is in GAP-01.

**B2. GAP-01 headline depends on a coding-only α.** The "three to four orders of magnitude" drop comes from multiplying α (a coding-sequence statistic) by ~1.3-3×10^4 amino-acid differences and setting the noncoding term to zero on the strength of one study (Keightley 2005, hominid upstream regions and first introns). About 98.5% of Day's 17.5M differences are noncoding. A noncoding adaptive share of only 5% would give ~9×10^5 adaptive substitutions, above the whole coding K_a and close to keruru's 700,000, which GAP-01 dismisses as "unsourced". Neither number is sourced for noncoding as a whole. This asymmetry favours the critics' side and is not flagged in the gap's own "Likely beneficiary" paragraph. Also note the low-α estimates (CSAC, Zhang & Li, E-W & K) are the ones Messer & Petrov say are biased downward. Fix: carry a noncoding α range (0 to a stated upper value) through the K_a row, and say that the verdict "K_a ≪ 17.5M" requires the noncoding term to stay below ~1%.

**B3. GAP-01 / PA-36 Matheson framing.** The 8.5-95% selective-deaths range is reported as a measurement without the authors' own warning that the data are not representative of natural conditions, and without noting that it comes from one annual plant. The file then uses it as the key reason Haldane's 10% is "far" too low (PA-36, section D.3). This leans toward the critic side. Add the caveat, and add that the paper explicitly gives no general speed limit rather than "resolving" it for humans.

**B4. Nunney "two-sided" (C07).** The Day-favourable half of Nunney rests on an input range (K = 5,000-10,000) that the paper did not simulate. At K = 10,000 and the same u the cost falls below Haldane's 300. The correct statement is that the answer depends on the human beneficial-mutation supply M = 2Ku, which neither the paper nor the file supplies. Neutral wording: "Nunney shows the cost depends strongly on M; whether humans are in the M ≪ 1 regime is unmeasured."

**B5. GAP-04 beneficiary.** "Day (on an all-adaptive reading)" is a fair label only if the reading is tied to the claim Day actually makes. MITTENS (node A, LTEE as ceiling) does treat all fixations as rate-limited, so the reading is live for A; Term 3 (H, H1) was retracted to "adaptive only", so it is not live there. Say which. The bolded row "exceeds the ceiling by ≈ 2-4×" (really ≈ 4×) is the only bold result in the table, which visually favours the Day-credit reading; un-bold or bold both rows. The critic-credit conclusion (interference is negligible at α-scale rates) inherits the B2 dependency.

**B6. GAP-02.** Balanced as written. It states the saturation inference ignores the window (critic-favouring) and then the Hernandez / Murphy findings that classic sweeps were rare (Day-favouring). Two cautions: the Murphy leg is the unverified one (C13), and the Hernandez ~5% bound (C12) is unused; adding it would strengthen the Day-favouring leg with a number.

**B7. GAP-03.** "Critics (Day minor)" is fair. Add Birky & Walsh's own warning that linkage can confound cross-region rate comparisons, and note that rate invariance for neutral substitution (expected value) does not by itself answer a variance or independence claim; the file's remark that the paper does not engage Day's "independent-site assumption" beyond the mean is a limit on the check, and should be stated in "Size".

**B8. GAP-05.** "Day-leaning on the premise" is fair for the accumulation of slightly deleterious fixations (CSAC "relaxed constraint"). Its Day-credit also leans on Boyko's 27-29% being applied to all constrained sites (extrapolation) and on an equilibrium argument that the file leaves to a future simulation; the stated 1-10 log-fitness decline is a no-back-mutation upper-end figure, and Charlesworth 2013 and Kondrashov 1995 both list resolutions. State that the number is an upper-end scenario, not an estimate.

**B9. K&H "would remove the conflict" (S31, PA-03).** Presented as a partial-fidelity point against Day for citing K&H only as a neutral-theory challenge. K&H is a Perspective, its Haldane remark is contested (Jensen et al. 2019), and it says "300 years". Say so; otherwise the fidelity point leans on a contested secondary claim.

**B10. Search-bias statement.** "Why this is not lopsided" is plausible but is an assertion about the author's search; I could not audit the term list's pre-registration. My own checks found two small search errors (S15 Birky: 1 hit not 0; "13 rows" vs 12) that both happen to make "nobody cited" claims slightly stronger than the data. Neither changes a conclusion, but they point in the same direction.
