## Statement (verbatim)
> "The Legacy of the Wistar Conference. Using the 1966 conference called by some mathematical critics of evolutionary biology, explains why their critiques were refuted, and how present-day creationists misrepresent what was said at that conference."

Source: Joe Felsenstein, [Jason Rosenhouse's book on mathematical anti-evolutionism is available](https://pandasthumb.org/archives/2022/05/rosenhouse-on-mathematical-anti-evolutionism.html), Panda's Thumb, 2022-05-18, annotated table of contents, chapter 4 entry (quotes-critics RO-01). `secondhand`: Felsenstein describing Rosenhouse, *The Failures of Mathematical Anti-Evolutionism* (Cambridge UP, 2022). Felsenstein states he commented on the manuscript and wrote a blurb (opponents/jason-rosenhouse.md).

## Formal statement
Claim form: for each Wistar mathematical challenge C in {Eden, Ulam, Schützenberger}, a biologist reply R exists at or after Wistar with R rebutting C. Plus a secondary claim about the modern creationist use of Wistar.

## Assumptions
- Stated: the critiques were refuted.
- Implicit: refutation includes both the contemporaneous replies (Wright, Lewontin, Waddington, Mayr) and later molecular biology and computer science (p.124, D1a/D1b).

## Responses
- Against: Day (D2, D2a-D2i): contemporaneous replies were qualitative; ch.4 provides no quantitative refutation. Camestros says Day does not discuss developments after 1966 (D1d), which is the opposite complaint.
- In support: Wistar text itself (Wright p.117; Lewontin pp.76-78, 92-93; Waddington p.78, p.93; Mayr p.30).
- Weaknesses: Rosenhouse's book was not accessed; both Day's reading of it (via his quotes) and Felsenstein's summary are secondhand. Rosenhouse predates Probability Zero (2022 vs 2026) and Day's MITTENS; it cannot be a reply to the fixation arguments (branches A-H except D).
