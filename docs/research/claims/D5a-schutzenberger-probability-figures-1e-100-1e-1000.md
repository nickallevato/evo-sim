Source: Schützenberger, in Moorhead & Kaplan (eds), *Mathematical Challenges to the Neo-Darwinian Interpretation of Evolution*, Wistar Institute Monograph No. 5 (1967; symposium 25-26 Apr 1966), local copy = 1985 Liss reprint scan, OCR text; page = printed folio (page numbers from the PDF text layer headers; OCR garbles noted in place), p.75 (OCR: '« I 0-1.000)' read as '< 10^-1000').

## Formal statement
Two assertions: P(a random typographic change preserves 'meaningful' function) < 10^-100 (for programs/meaningfulness in his sense); P(the self-correcting mechanism appears spontaneously) < 10^-1000. Neither is derived in the text; on p.73 he says the cycle numbers are quoted from other speakers ('From their talks it is clear that even on the most schematic models the number of cycles involved is truly enormous').

## Assumptions
- Stated: the figures are illustrative upper bounds for a class of systems.
- Implicit: the system is computationally universal and biology resembles it (disputed by Waddington, p.77).

## Responses
- Against: Waddington: 'All proteins do something, so all changes in the program level have meaning' (p.79); Lewontin (p.79).
- In support: Day (D5).
- Weaknesses: the numbers are asserted; Day's own Bernoulli figures (10^-47,262; 10^-34,000,000) have the same status (asserted products, G).
