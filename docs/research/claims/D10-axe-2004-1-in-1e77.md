## Statement (verbatim)
> "The prevalence of low-level function in four such experiments indicates that roughly one in 10(64) signature-consistent sequences forms a working domain. Combined with the estimated prevalence of plausible hydropathic patterns (for any fold) and of relevant folds for particular functions, this implies the overall prevalence of sequences performing a specific function by any domain-sized fold may be as low as 1 in 10(77)"

> "Using these simplifications, the difficulty of specifying a working beta-lactamase domain is assessed here."

Source: Axe DD (2004) Estimating the prevalence of protein sequences adopting functional enzyme folds, J Mol Biol 341(5):1295-1315, abstract (`sources/raw/sources/abs/Axe2004.txt`; full text paywalled). Note: no Day text in the corpus cites Axe (searched 'Axe', 'lactamase', '10^77' in all Day blog and Zenodo texts); the 'Day cites 1e-77' entry in the pass-1 plan is unsupported by the harvested corpus.

## Formal statement
Prevalence f_fold-function = f_signature * f_pattern * f_fold: f_signature = 10^-64 (four experiments, 10-residue clusters, low-level function); overall f = 10^-77 'as low as'. Derived here: expected random hits among Eden's 10^52 molecules = 10^52 * 10^-77 = 10^-25; among the 10^55 of the working paper 10^-22. For comparison with D3: the fraction among all 20^150 sequences of a 150-residue chain is not stated in the abstract.

## Assumptions
- Stated: simplifications (hydropathic signature; coupled local problems); 'four such experiments'.
- Implicit: independence of local problems when multiplying; low-level function counts as working; one fold (beta-lactamase) generalises; the sampled ten-residue clusters are representative.

## Responses
- Against: Taylor 2001 (D11) and Keefe & Szostak 2001 (D12) measure higher frequencies for other functions; the multiplication assumes independent local problems (epistasis could raise or lower f). Rosenhouse (D1a) says protein space geometry makes such counts naive.
- In support: Day-side usage (D).
- Weaknesses: Axe's number is a prevalence of function, not reachability: it estimates f, not whether selection can reach functional sequences from nearby ones.
