## Statement (verbatim)
> "The transcript records Lewontin saying, “That is a very good question. I don’t know.”"

> "He does not mention Lewontin’s admission, under direct questioning from Schützenberger, that he cannot justify the continuity assumption that small genetic changes typically produce small phenotypic changes."

Source: [The Best They've Got I](https://voxday.net/2026/10/05/the-best-theyve-got-i/), 2026-10-05, voxday.net, paras 20 and 19.

The same exchange as printed in Day's chapter 6 (2nd ed.):

> "“Dr. Schützenberger: I would like to know how you could use a notion like quasi-continuity.” “Dr. Lewontin: That is a very good question. I don’t know.”"

> "Lewontin’s response was circular. The fitness topology, he suggested, was “itself a product of evolution.”"

Source: [Vox Day Interview on Evolutionary Theory](https://billdembski.substack.com/p/vox-day-interview-on-evolutionary) (Dembski Substack), 2026-09-28, appended chapter 'From Probability Zero (2nd ed.) on 1966 Wistar Symposium, Chapter 6: The 1966 Meeting of the Minds' (posted with Day's permission; primary Day text).

What the volume contains (Moorhead & Kaplan (eds), *Mathematical Challenges to the Neo-Darwinian Interpretation of Evolution*, Wistar Institute Monograph No. 5 (1967; symposium 25-26 Apr 1966), local copy = 1985 Liss reprint scan, OCR text; page = printed folio (OCR garbles noted in place)):

> "DR. LEVINS: I think the missing ingredient in this analysis is that you have left out evolution. The error of the reductionist methodology is to start out with a lower level and attempt to derive a higher level from it without considering the reciprocal relation. In fact, its topology is, itself, a product of evolution"
— Richard Levins, p.79 (the 'topology ... product of evolution' remark is Levins's, in the discussion of Schützenberger's paper; it is not Lewontin's).

> "DR. LEWONTIN: I think the answer is that you have over-estimated the number of absolutely meaningless changes that occur when you change a single nucleotide. If we list all single nucleotide changes and the known translation vocabulary between nucleotide triplets and insertion of amino acids, and then we list for a given protein all the results on that protein of changing amino acids all over the molecule, we will find, in fact, that a very large proportion of those do not render the molecule meaningless in an absolute context."
— Lewontin, p.79 (reply to Schützenberger on meaningless changes).

> "DR. LEWONTIN: The answer is no."
— Lewontin, p.92 (to Schützenberger's question whether viable configurations are rarer than grammatical sentences).

> "DR. SCHUTZENBERGER: That's a good question. I don't think I have time to answer it now."
— Schützenberger, p.79 (answering Mayr's question 'why do molecules have such-and-such properties?'). This is the only 'good question ... time' exchange in the volume; the speaker is Schützenberger.

## Formal statement
Day's claim is a fact about the transcript: Lewontin answered a question of Schützenberger's about continuity ('small genetic changes produce small phenotypic changes') with 'I don't know'. Searched the OCR text and the PDF text layer for 'very good question', 'good question', 'quasi', 'continuity', 'continuous', and every Lewontin turn containing 'know'. 'quasi-continuity' does not occur anywhere in the volume ('quasi' occurs only in 'quasi equilibrium' and 'quasimathematical').

## Assumptions
- Stated: Lewontin made an admission, 'under direct questioning from Schützenberger'.
- Implicit: continuity of the genotype-phenotype map is the load-bearing premise of hill-climbing (true in the sense of the NK literature, see D).

## Responses
- Against: neither the quoted question ('quasi-continuity') nor the answer occurs in the volume; the circularity claim (the topology is 'itself a product of evolution') is spoken by Levins (p.79), and Schützenberger's rejoinder ('the Ashby trap') answers Levins; the verbatim quote does not occur; the phrase 'That's a good question. I don't think I have time to answer it now' belongs to Schützenberger (p.79). Lewontin repeatedly asserts the opposite: single amino-acid changes in tryptophan synthetase and hemoglobin change enzyme properties without destroying the molecule (p.76, column-interleaved in the OCR and here read down the column: 'known cases where the enzyme, far from being destroyed, is changed in its pH optimum, changed in its isoelectric points'; p.79: 'a very large proportion of those do not render the molecule meaningless').
- In support: Lewontin's examples are enzyme-function-preserving changes, which does not establish continuity at the level of organism fitness, which was Schützenberger's actual question ('the mapping between the first and second [space]').
- Weaknesses: OCR coverage is complete for this passage (two-column interleaving affects some lines but not the speaker labels). The Day-claimed physical-copy check is not reproducible here. A misattribution (Schützenberger/Lewontin) by Day remains possible rather than invention.
