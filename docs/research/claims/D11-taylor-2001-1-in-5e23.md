## Statement (verbatim)
> "However, a 100-residue protein has 20100 (1.3 ⫻ 10130) possible sequences. Even a library with the mass of the Earth itself—5.98 ⫻ 1027 g—would comprise at most 3.3 ⫻ 1047 different sequences, or a miniscule fraction of such diversity."

> "we can estimate that if every position in the protein had been randomized, a library of ⬇1024 members would have been needed to obtain AroQ mutases."

> "The required library size can thus be calculated as follows: 4,500 (binary patterned H1) ⫻ 17,500 (binary patterned H2兾H3) ⫻ 206 (randomized active site residues) ⫻ 102 (fully randomized L1) ⫻ 102 (fully randomized L2) ⫻ 104 (templating effects) ⫽ 5 ⫻ 1023."

> "Our estimate of the low frequency of protein catalysts in sequence space indicates that it will not be possible to isolate enzymes from unbiased random libraries in a single step."

> "natural evolution, the design of new enzymes will require incremental strategies in which, for instance, a suitable scaffold is first generated"

Source: Taylor SV, Walter KU, Kast P, Hilvert D (2001) Searching sequence space for protein catalysts, PNAS 98(18):10596-10601; p.10596 (first), p.10600 (second and third), p.10601 (fourth, fifth); full text `sources/raw/sources/manual/Taylor2001.pdf` (user-downloaded). Extraction: '⬇' is the glyph for 'approximately', '1024' = 10^24, '206' = 20^6, '102' = 10^2, '104' = 10^4, '20100' = 20^100.

## Formal statement
Required library size N_req = 4,500 * 17,500 * 20^6 * 10^2 * 10^2 * 10^4 = 5.0e23. Recomputed: 4500*17500 = 7.875e7; 20^6 = 6.4e7; 7.875e7*6.4e7 = 5.04e15; x1e8 = 5.04e23. Implied frequency of catalysts among fully randomized 100-residue sequences f ~ 1/N_req = 2e-24 (derived). The 100-residue space is 20^100 = 1.27e130 (Taylor 1.3e130, reproduced). Expected hits among Eden's 10^52 molecules at f = 2e-24: 2e28 (derived; different length and function from Eden's 250-mers, so not a like-for-like count).

## Assumptions
- Stated: single amino acid tolerated at six conserved active-site positions; 1-10% of turn segments active; templating factor 1e4 'could turn out to be larger'; selection to a modest activity.
- Implicit: independence among the factors; AroQ-type chorismate mutase is representative; the binary-patterned modules are a good proxy for sequence families.

## Responses
- Against (for D): the frequency (2e-24) is vastly larger than Eden's implied 10^-273 and Axe's 10^-77, so at the 100-residue scale a large random library is not ruled out in principle; Taylor himself: libraries of this size 'far exceed what is currently accessible by experiment'.
- In support (for D): the paper says 'extremely low incidence of enzymes in completely unbiased libraries' and that single-step random isolation is infeasible; it recommends 'incremental strategies' as in natural evolution (i.e. the topology horn of Eden's dichotomy).
- Weaknesses: the paper neither measures connectivity nor evolutionary reachability, only frequency; Day-side uses of it as a 'counter-estimate' (in the task's labelling) must be read with that.
