## Statement (verbatim)
> "Rosenhouse quotes Crosby’s exasperated comment about “gorgeous unreality” as though it refutes the mathematicians. He does not mention that in the discussion of Eden’s paper, Crosby actually supported Eden’s argument, noting that each intermediate stage “has got to be capable of existing viably” and that frame-shift mutations “decrease enormously the possibility of intermediate stages being viable.”"

Source: [The Best They've Got I](https://voxday.net/2026/10/05/the-best-theyve-got-i/), 2026-10-05, voxday.net, para 24.

Primary text (Moorhead & Kaplan (eds), *Mathematical Challenges to the Neo-Darwinian Interpretation of Evolution*, Wistar Institute Monograph No. 5 (1967; symposium 25-26 Apr 1966), local copy = 1985 Liss reprint scan, OCR text; page = printed folio (OCR garbles noted in place)):

> "But, your reading frame shift seems to decrease enormously the possibility of intermediate stages being viable. This isn't a set of models we have got on a bench or a set of pretty pictures on a blackboard. We are going through a series of stages, each of which has got to be capable of existing viably, if it is going to have any possibility of giving rise to the next."
— J. L. Crosby, p.17 (discussion of Eden; he is addressing Eden's reading-frame-shift suggestion).

> "It always seems to me that there is such an air of gorgeous unreality when mathematicians come to deal with biological subjects"
— Crosby, p.31 (discussion of Ulam).

> "you cannot really start with year Xo; it has to be ION, where N is 3, 4 or 5. That seems to me to place the whole of that calculation completely out of court."
— Crosby, p.31.

## Formal statement
No formal content. Interpretation of two speech acts: (p.17) intermediate stages must be viable; (p.31) Ulam's calculation is 'out of court' because it begins at an unrealistic starting time.

## Assumptions
- Stated: Crosby 'supported Eden' in noting viability constraints.
- Implicit: viable-intermediate constraints are what make Eden's topology horn (D3b) bite.

## Responses
- Against: the p.17 remark is a criticism of the frame-shift route ('If your changes take place by reading frame shifts, we would need fewer of them. But...') that Eden floated as a way to shorten paths; it constrains one proposed mechanism and does not endorse a probability-zero conclusion. The p.31 remark is a dismissal of mathematicians' modelling ('amuse themselves').
- In support: Day's point that viability of intermediates is a premise accepted by a biologist at the symposium is accurate.
- Weaknesses: viable intermediates are required by any gradualist model; Crosby does not say they are rare.
