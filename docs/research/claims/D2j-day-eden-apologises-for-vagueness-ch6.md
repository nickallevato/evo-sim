## Statement (verbatim)
> "Eden’s response was polite but he showed Waddington no mercy: “I must apologize for being so vague on the nature of R,” he said, referring to the fitness function, “but let me say just one thing that led me to this conclusion: it requires a more complete definition of fitness or survival in which case one can discuss the absolute magnitude of the probabilities.”"

> "To put it in plain English, he was saying “show me the math.” Waddington couldn’t do it."

Source: [Vox Day Interview on Evolutionary Theory](https://billdembski.substack.com/p/vox-day-interview-on-evolutionary) (Dembski Substack), 2026-09-28, appended chapter 'From Probability Zero (2nd ed.) on 1966 Wistar Symposium, Chapter 6: The 1966 Meeting of the Minds' (posted with Day's permission; primary Day text).

Primary text (Moorhead & Kaplan (eds), *Mathematical Challenges to the Neo-Darwinian Interpretation of Evolution*, Wistar Institute Monograph No. 5 (1967; symposium 25-26 Apr 1966), local copy = 1985 Liss reprint scan, OCR text; page = printed folio (OCR garbles noted in place)):

> "DR. WADDINGTON: The variation is about 0.75 and you come to 0.001 away from the optimum in your earlier figure? DR. BOSSERT: I must apologize for having been so vague on the nature of R2 which appears in the figures. To explain it in detail requires a more complete definition of the abstract problem which led to the fitness function used. I had avoided doing this because it was of no biological interest except that it generated unimodal fitness functions over a character space such as the one shown in figure 2. I agree with you now, that it is important to complete the discussion of this point."
— W. Bossert, p.40 (discussion of Bossert's paper). The sentence 'I must apologize for having been so vague on the nature of R2' continues 'which appears in the figures. To explain it in detail requires a more complete definition of the abstract problem which led to the fitness function used'; R2 is a distance measure in Bossert's simulation, and the abstract problem is the solution of simultaneous linear equations.

## Formal statement
Day's claim is a fact about the transcript: Eden, responding to Waddington's smooth-landscape point, deferred to a missing definition of fitness. The text shows a different speaker (Bossert), a different topic (a simulation's plotted axis) and a different context (the discussion of Bossert's paper, not Eden's).

## Assumptions
- Stated: Eden's reply amounts to 'show me the math'.
- Implicit: the remark is evidence that biologists could not supply a fitness landscape.

## Responses
- Against: the quotation is from Bossert (p.40); Day's wording is almost verbatim ('so vague' vs 'having been so vague', 'for the nature of R' vs 'on the nature of R2'), the near-verbatim match indicates that the source of the sentence is this Bossert passage.
- In support: the substantive point that Waddington's smooth-landscape suggestion lacked data is made by Day at D2e and is supported by the p.93 passage.
- Weaknesses: the misattribution does not alter D2e.
