Probability Zero Audit

A neutral, two-sided audit of Vox Day's mathematical case against evolution (MITTENS, Hard Limits, Probability Zero) and of his critics. Each claim gets a numerical check and three verdicts: internal validity, citation fidelity, external validity.

Argument tree

ROOT · Day
No evolutionary mechanism can produce the observed human–chimp divergence in the time available.
AMITTENS rate limitF_max = t_div · d / (g_len · G_f)
  • A2LTEE generations per fixation (1,322 / 909)An asexual model reproduces 1,322, but only as a joint product of Nₑ, beneficial-mutation rate and s, so it isn't a universal constant. Asexual rate does saturate (partial credit to Day). With free recombination the rate would be 1.5–172× higher than the asexual rate; that is an extrapolation, not simulated at LTEE scale. LTEE Nₑ = 3.3e7 is unsourced.
  • A3Required fixations: 30M → 20M → 205M"410 Mb" not found in Yoo 2025 (average is 327 Mb per lineage). 205M counts base pairs, not mutation events.
  • A4Turnover coefficient d ≈ 0.45d works as a unit conversion, equivalent to a ~56-year generation time. Its value can't be identified from the data.
BNeutral theory is insufficientEmpty pipeline, Hard Limits, k ≠ μ
  • B1E[F(T)] ≈ μL(T − 4Nₑ)Exact for a population starting with zero variation. A population at equilibrium gives k = μ with no deficit.
  • B1bPopulation-size changesContraction gives a surplus and expansion a deficit, ≈ U·4ΔN. The analytic formula matches the simulation.
  • B1cSourced human Nₑ historyRerun with full burn-in: every sourced history gives 1.9–4.0× more fixed substitutions per lineage than U·T, matching the analytic formula (3.986 vs 3.984). Day's (T−4Nₑ)/T is right for new mutations alone (0.84). The excess includes alleles fixing in both lineages, so it is not a human–chimp difference count.
  • B2Hard Limits: P(fix within G) ≈ exp(−π²Nₑ/G)The exponent is correct. It bounds how long one allele takes, not the total rate of substitutions.
  • B3k = μ · N / NₑRefuted: neutral fixation probability is 1/N in census terms, so k = μ.
  • B3bk = 0.743μ (Balloux & Lehmann)The effect is real but ranges −2% to +38% at human-like parameters (k rises). 0.743 comes from the 4-cohort window Day chose.
  • B4Divergence including ancestral polymorphismSimulation matches 2μT + θ_anc to 0.3%. At the correct human–chimp ancestor (Nₑ 198k) predicted divergence is 1.55%, about 25% above the observed 1.23%; at Day's Nₑ = 10k it is 0.65%, about half. Observed sits between; it is a 3-parameter fit (μ, T, ancestral Nₑ). The CSAC polymorphic share (14–22%) matches the model (6–25%), so there is no tension.
CAncient DNA"Zero fixations in ~7,000 years"
  • C1Zero fixations across the 1240k SNP panelThe panel is built from sites that vary today, so it can't record completed fixations. Reviewers disagree: the critic side says the observed 1 and 3 exceed neutral (P≈0.0002); the Day side says the test has no power. Day's exact statistic is being simulated.
  • C1bDay's exact time-binned statistic (new check, reviewed)Verdict: not reproducible, cannot adjudicate. The model misses Day's own table, so it isn't a valid null. Likely cause found: real ancient samples give only a few calls per site in old bins. Lowering per-site coverage drops the neutral count from ~6,500 to ~120 and moves events into the oldest bin, the same direction as Day's table. A direction, not yet a reproduction.
  • C2Overlapping generations vs standard modelThe ratio is an algebraic identity, so d is not independently measured.
  • C6The 630 comparatorDay's comparison and this audit's earlier rescaling are both wrong. About 1,500–1,850 fixations are expected on an ascertained panel.

  • DQuotations from the Wistar proceedingsSix misquotes found, including Eden's 2.7M called "not implausible".
  • D-simSequence-space search simulationsSpecified, not started.
ELTEE hazards / punctuated equilibriumHypermutator founder hazard, relictation
  • EFounder hazard 2.3%; relictation exact chainsQueued.
F"Kimura irrelevance"Fixation time vs fixation rate
  • F1Slow fixation limits the rateLatency is not throughput. Overlapping sweeps give one fixation every 3 generations against an 847-generation fixation time.
  • F2Multi-locus interferenceInterference is real. Asexual genomes run at 9% of the independent rate at the highest supply tested and tight linkage at 20%. Free recombination stays at 97.5% with 272 sweeps in progress, and fwdpy11 agrees. Tested at N = 1,000, a single s, soft selection only.
GBernoulli Barrier0.02^(2×10⁷); ~230 simultaneous sweeps
  • G~230 simultaneous sweeps capNo collapse at ~260–270 open loci under soft selection, or under hard selection when R ≥ 5. Under hard selection with R = 2 the population fails at ~167. The cap depends on reproductive excess and load.
  • G10.02^(2×10⁷): specific outcome vs any outcomeQueued.
HCost of selectionHaldane 300 generations per substitution → 487
  • H1487 vs 20M requiredA category error, the same one Day conceded in his 2026-05-07 retraction. Haldane's D = 30 is an assumption; realistic values give 92–278 generations.
  • H2Hard vs soft selection (Nunney 2003)Corrected model: soft selection is 1.5–2.8× slower than hard at equal mutation supply, so the critics' "soft selection removes the cost" did not reproduce. Haldane's 300 is a bounding case; the human value is undetermined, so H is neither refuted nor binding.
  • H-hardMany simultaneous sweeps under hard selection (new check, reviewed with fixes)The limit is ln R / D (reproductive excess over cost per substitution), not a fixed 1 in 300. Populations sustained 9–120× Haldane's rate at R ≥ 1.3. Haldane's own case (R ≈ 1.1, diploid D ≈ 20–30) gives ≈ 1/300 and was not tested, so "1/300 falsified" is withdrawn; "10% is not a general bound" stands. Day's ~230 sweeps persist at R ≥ 5, fail at R = 2. Model favours critics (free recombination, imposed opportunity rate).
  • H8Hössjer's 15,80010.5× Haldane's own figure. Day's Term 3 differs from Haldane's cost by 17.1× at the same d.

Balance sheet

Day and allies

Holds up

  • LTEE G_f is a measured throughput, so "you forgot parallelism" misses
  • Hard Limits math is internally correct
  • Haldane's cost is real under hard selection, and soft selection does not remove it in these models
  • The aDNA panel does enrich mid-frequency sites
  • Asexual (LTEE-like) rates do saturate as mutation supply rises
  • His LTEE data paper is careful and corrects itself

Fails

  • Reads Kimura's fixation probability as 1/(2Nₑ); Kimura's N is breeding individuals
  • Uses Zeng's s (negative selection) as beneficial
  • Assumes the population starts with no variation; sourced Nₑ history gives an excess, not a deficit
  • Required fixations drift 30M → 20M → 205M
  • 0.743μ and 32.3μ

Critics

Holds up

  • k = μ regardless of population size
  • Fixation time is not substitution rate
  • Ancestral polymorphism adds to divergence
  • Base pairs are not mutation events
  • Recombination lifts the interference ceiling

Fails

  • Loose, unsourced inputs
  • Expected values without variances
  • No one engaged cost of selection, aDNA or the Bernoulli Barrier
  • Nothing peer-reviewed on either side
  • Hancock's and Nesslig20's 38M double-counts chromosome copies
