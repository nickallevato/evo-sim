# Milestone 5: R4 round two, the load-bearing checks
*2026-10-08 · stage: R4 (in progress) · commits `a89d06c`, `524550d`*

This round tested the claims that carry most of the weight of Day's root argument. It ran ten checks, followed by four reviews:
- a correctness review;
- a Day-side steelman;
- a critic-side steelman;
- a separate review of the two newest checks.

Fifty-four verdict fields across 33 claims changed as a result. All numbers below are final, after review.

## Results by branch
**B1c: sourced population-size histories.**
- **Setup:** ancestral populations of 198k or 132k (Yoo 2025), stepping down to sourced modern sizes (Prado-Martinez 2013).
- **Result:** every history gives an *excess* of substitutions (K/UT = 1.9–4.0), not a deficit.
- **Limits:** the review reframed this as an identity under step histories, not an independent finding.
- **For Day:** the new-mutation component, 0.84, equals Day's (T − 4Nₑ)/T exactly.

**B4a: two-lineage divergence.**
- **Result:** d = 2μT + θ_anc holds within 0.3%, and msprime agrees.
- **Fit to data:** neither side gets a clean fit.
  - Yoo's ancestral node overshoots the observed 1.23% by about 25%.
  - At Day's Nₑ = 10⁴ the formula gives about half the observed value.
  - The fit has three parameters, and Yoo's mutation rate is unrecorded.
- **Critics:** Hancock's and Nesslig20's "38M matches 35M" double-counts on an SNV basis. Roughly 19M of the divergence is post-split and about 15M is ancestral.

**B3b / B3c: the Balloux–Lehmann effect.**
- **For Day:** the effect is real; the paper's equation 3 was reproduced in 9 scenarios. The critics' blanket "k = μ" holds only for discrete generations.
- **Against Day:** at human-scale parameters the effect is small and points *upward*. The 0.743 figure is an artefact of the measurement window, and its mechanism is falsified.

**C1 / C1b: ancient DNA.**
- **C1:** the 1240k SNP panel is ascertained on variation found in present-day people. Zero fixations from below 50% frequency is therefore what neutral theory predicts, so the test does not discriminate.
- **C1b:** Day's specific "21 fixations" count could not be reproduced from the published procedure; the simulation gives 1,200–17,000. An early "deficit versus neutral" wording was withdrawn as unsupported.
- **Verdict:** cannot adjudicate yet.

**C2: the turnover coefficient d.**
- **For Day:** the derivation is correct, and d·s is exact when s is measured on the hazard scale.
- **Against Day:** for per-generation s the factor is 1. d cannot be identified from the data: Day's own priors give 0.48–0.57.

**F2 / A-sim: interference.**
- **Without recombination:** the realised rate collapses to 9% of the independent-sites rate. This is the regime the LTEE measures.
- **With free recombination:** it holds at 97.5% with 272 sweeps in flight. fwdpy11 agrees.
- **LTEE rate:** the figure of about 1,300 generations reproduces.
- **Limits:** the 1.5–172× factor for scaling the LTEE rate to humans is an extrapolation, not a simulation. Human-scale numbers of active loci are untested.

**H / H2-hard: cost of selection.**
- **Haldane's arithmetic holds:** 300, 487 and 41,068.
- **Hard-selection cap:** under hard selection a population persists while λ·D < ln R, with D ≈ ln M + 1. At R ≥ 1.3 that cap is 9–100× above 1/300.
- **Against the critics:** "soft selection removes the cost" was not reproduced. In both models, soft selection is *slower* than hard selection at equal mutation supply.
- **Untested:** Haldane's own regime (R ≈ 1.1, diploid D ≈ 20–30) and realistic human hard-selected load.

## What the reviews changed
There were no blockers. The eight major findings were all resolved:

| # | Problem | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mutation supply was inflated in the cost-of-selection model | Fixed and re-run. "Soft is worse" survives; "within 15%" was withdrawn. |
| 2 | One model was tuned twice toward Nunney's 300 | Labelled exploratory. |
| 3 | B4a compared the wrong quantities against CSAC 2005 | Fixed. The apparent tension disappeared. |
| 4 | B4a used the wrong ancestral node | Fixed. The verdict is now "contested". |
| 5 | B1c's "every history gives an excess" holds by construction | Reframed as an identity. |
| 6 | Extrapolations were stated as simulations | Relabelled as extrapolations. |
| 7 | Seeds were not reproducible | Fixed and re-run. |
| 8 | An ancient-DNA overclaim | Withdrawn. |

Two points from the steelman reviews shaped the verdicts:
- **Day's current position is narrower than the headlines.** The cost argument is partly retracted, a full pipe is conceded and d is dropped. Verdicts now say "premise withdrawn by Day" where that applies.
- **Credit goes only to whoever made the argument.** Results that came from the audit or the literature are not credited to the critics, because no critic made them.
