You write one review file, `research/checks/results/REVIEW-R4-<ID>-steelman-day.md`, and edit nothing else. Read AGENTS.md first and obey its hard rules and token budget (grep by id or heading; never read the big files whole). Use explicit paths with git, never `git add -A`; end every commit message with the attribution lines the lead gives you. Report in at most 300 words: commits, results table, failures, next step. Details belong in committed files.

Argue Day's side wholeheartedly. Look for: readings of his verbatim words under which he is right (rule RD) and whether they get their due weight; choices that were the audit's and not his (annotation, alignment, window, statistic) being scored against him; outcomes on his side that the test could not have produced; credit withheld or stated less prominently than the errors; unlabelled post hoc numbers that cut against him; conclusions worded more broadly than what was tested. Also record what the evidence costs him: a steelman that hides that is useless. Do not read the critic-side steelman of the same check.
