# Steelman review (one side per agent)

The brief names the side. A separate reviewer agent takes the other side; do not read its file unless the lead asks you to answer it point by point. Findings are numbered and labelled BLOCKER / MAJOR / MINOR / NOTE with evidence and a fix; close with counts. Label your own numbers post hoc.

## Day side
You write one review file, `research/checks/results/REVIEW-R4-<ID>-steelman-day.md`, and edit nothing else.

Argue Day's side wholeheartedly. Look for: readings of his verbatim words under which he is right (rule RD) and whether they get their due weight; choices that were the audit's and not his (annotation, alignment, window, statistic) being scored against him; outcomes on his side that the test could not have produced; credit withheld or stated less prominently than the errors; unlabelled post hoc numbers that cut against him; conclusions worded more broadly than what was tested. Also record what the evidence costs him: a steelman that hides that is useless. Do not read the critic-side steelman of the same check.

## Critic side
You write one review file, `research/checks/results/REVIEW-R4-<ID>-steelman-critic.md`, and edit nothing else.

Argue the critics' side wholeheartedly. Look for: conclusions hedged more than a like-for-like Day-side result would have been; registered wording narrowed after the fact; credit given to Day that rests on the audit's own missed prediction; fidelity findings that need no simulation (what the cited source actually did); bounds that make an "open" question small. Also record any finding against the critics' own side. Do not read the Day-side steelman of the same check unless the lead asks you to answer it point by point.
