## The question, answered from the critic side

The brief asks whether the evidence on record carries `contradicted` inside the scope the owner accepted (D-12): a per-generation count of adaptive coding fixations of any effect size, on model-inferred alpha. **It does, with a margin the record understates.** For A2e to survive inside that scope, the human-lineage coding count must be at most 191 (G1). On the audit's smallest denominator (N2, 11,090) that needs alpha <= 1.72%; on Uricchio's own human-specific count (29,925) it needs alpha <= 0.64%. The lower 95% HPD bound (9.6%) is **5.6x** above the first and **15x** above the second (post hoc). Put the other way, the lower-HPD count must be wrong by 82% (N2) or 93% (own base) for A2e to survive; the point estimate by 87% (N2). A second, independent, full-text data set (Boyko 2008) clears 191 at its lower confidence limit even after a 50/50 lineage split (599, 3.1x) and after a 25% human share (299, 1.57x). The single number the field rests on (798, 4.2x) is the product of three Day-favourable choices stacked on each other; two of them have no warrant in any rule (F1, F2).

## Findings

### MAJOR-1. The 25% "charity haircut" is not licensed by rule C, and on the quoted sentence it double-counts an effect already in the data
- **Attaches to:** claim file l.14 ("after a 25% charity haircut"), l.71; `R4-A2e-alpha.md` l.144, l.173 ("the 0.75 haircut covers it either way").
- **Evidence.** The only source for the 25% is the verified quote "We show that in this adaptation regime α is reduced ≈ 25% by linkage genome-wide" (raw output, quote 4; script l.58). Read plainly, this says linkage lowers the *realised* adaptive fraction relative to a world without linkage. The human substitutions Uricchio counts occurred under linkage, and ABC-MK is fitted to them with background selection modelled (refresh s3; addendum l.137: the method "models weakly beneficial alleles and background selection"). On that reading 0.135 is already the realised, linkage-reduced alpha; multiplying by 0.75 removes the linkage effect a second time (**reasoned**: full text not available to this review to confirm the Methods' exact definition). The addendum concedes the question is "not settled by the text read" (l.173) and then uses the haircut anyway.
- **Rule point.** Rule C ("try the most charitable reading") governs how the *author's words* are read. It does not license discounting a published estimate. Nothing in Day's quotes (Q131, Q138, the Statement) bears on linkage in human coding sequence. The haircut is audit generosity with no rule behind it, and the field cites its output as the "basis".
- **Effect.** Without the haircut, the floor on N2 is the lower-HPD row: **1,065 (5.6x)**. The verdict does not move either way; the stated margin does.
- **Fix.** Restate the basis as "lower 95% HPD on the smallest audit denominator: 1,065 (5.6x)", and carry 798 (4.2x) as a further Day-favourable sensitivity "with a 25% haircut that the source does not support as a correction to this estimate (reasoned; full text not re-read)". The Day-side steelman's attack on the haircut being too *small* has to be answered against the same point: there is no source-based haircut to enlarge.

### MAJOR-2. The verdict rests on a denominator Uricchio did not measure, while the paper's own like-for-like count is set aside as "unreconciled"
- **Attaches to:** claim file l.14 (N2 named as "the audit's most Day-charitable denominator"); `R4-A2e-alpha.md` l.147 ("not used for the verdict"), l.158 (P4), l.194.
- **Evidence.** Alpha is a fraction of a specific set of substitutions. Uricchio's 0.135 is the fraction of *its own* 29,925 human-specific, parsimony-polarised nonsynonymous substitutions (Methods quotes 1-2; table row in quote 8). The like-for-like count is therefore alpha x 29,925: 2,873 at the lower HPD (15x), 4,040 at the point (21x). N2 is Boyko's 22,180 *pooled over both lineages*, halved on an even-split assumption, from a smaller gene set. It is the smallest of five audit denominators and the only one that needs a lineage split at all, for an estimate that is already human-branch. The record treats the paper's own count as suspect (P4 "unreconciled") and the foreign, halved count as the verdict basis.
- **"Unreconciled" points one way.** The record's own candidate (l.194): Gayà-Vidal's 18,936 human-branch substitutions on 9,785 genes, scaled to Uricchio's 17,028 genes, is 32,953 (recomputed), within 10% of 29,925. If proportional scaling holds even roughly, N1 to N3 are lower bounds from smaller gene sets, not rivals. Nothing on record suggests 29,925 is inflated. A gap explained by gene-set coverage is not a reason to prefer the smaller count.
- **Like-for-like test.** A Day-side result would not be scored by multiplying a Day-favourable rate by a denominator from a different study chosen because it was the smallest. The audit would call that the least favourable reading for Day and would not let it set the field.
- **Fix.** Present the own-base rows (2,873 to 4,040; 15 to 21x) as the like-for-like comparison, with N1 to N3 as Day-favourable sensitivity rows, and replace "unreconciled" with "larger gene set; the candidate gene-count scaling (unverified) puts N1 to N3 below it". The field value is unchanged; its stated margin is 15x, not 4.2x.

### MINOR-1. "On model-inferred alpha" scopes the human side only; Day's G_f is also a model inference
- **Attaches to:** claim file l.14 (scope clause), l.74 ("the figure is a model inference (D6 stands)").
- **Evidence.** The ceiling 1/1,322 is computed from fixations called in pooled metagenomic trajectories (A2 l.18, l.34) by a counting rule that moves G_f by 20-50% (A2 l.59; A2b l.31: the strict count is 36.7% lower than the naive one) with Good 2017's "ad hoc" hidden Markov model whose fixed state is absorbing by design (refresh-2026-10-11b-lit s5). Neither side of the inequality is an observed fixation count. The record carries "model inference, not observed" as a Day-side point against the human number and never as a qualifier on Day's own number.
- **Fix.** Either state D6 for both sides of the comparison or drop it as a one-sided discount. No value moves.

### MINOR-2. The strong-only "lower HPD 0.0" overstates the Day-side room
- **Attaches to:** claim file l.14 ("lower HPD 0.0"), l.72; `R4-A2e-alpha.md` l.146, l.167.
- **Evidence.** The components' HPDs are weak 0.0-0.21 and strong 0.0-0.13, but the total is 0.096-0.17 (quote 8). The two components cannot both sit near zero: the posterior mass that puts strong at 0 puts weak near the total. A marginal interval that touches 0 for one component is not evidence that strong adaptation is absent, only that the split is poorly identified (as the addendum says at l.198). **Reasoned** from the table; the joint posterior is not on record. The strong/weak boundary in ABC-MK is also population-scaled (2Ns); a "strong-only" reading of Day's ceiling would need the same threshold applied to the LTEE count, where N_e ~3e7 makes nearly every fixed beneficial mutation "strong". This is outside the field (another scope) and the owner's scoping is not reopened; it bears only on how much weight the claim comment gives the strong-only row as Day-side credit.
- **Fix.** Add to the strong-only row: "marginal interval; the strong and weak components are not separately identified, and the total excludes 0".

### MINOR-4. The GRADE downgrades that make A2e "very low" count other scopes as inconsistency and an adverse basis as indirectness
- **Attaches to:** `docs/research/criticality/grade-certainty-2026-10-12.md` l.330.
- **Evidence.** Inconsistency is downgraded "serious" because "scopes named in the comment give different verdicts" (strong-only, total throughput). GRADE inconsistency is heterogeneity among estimates of the *same* quantity. Within the scope, every bias-corrected estimate lands above 191 (Boyko full text, Uricchio full text, EK's conditional 40%); the only sub-threshold values are Gayà-Vidal's naive MK null, which its authors flag as biased low, and a gene *subset* (l.194). Indirectness counts `rd` 1 because "Day's stated basis is total throughput". On that basis the claim file gives F = 50,765, conditional only on k = mu (l.15): the alternative basis is far worse for Day, so it cannot be why the count-scoped `contradicted` overstates. The risk-of-bias downgrade (post hoc, self-set trigger) is fair as to the *process* (see Costs, item 2), but the number it protects is the arithmetic of a published interval that no registration could have changed.
- **Fix.** For a later criticality pass, not this field: rate inconsistency within the scope; record the total-throughput basis as an adverse alternative, not as indirectness that weakens the verdict.

### NOTE-1. The threshold used is the most Day-favourable of Day's own
The field uses G1 = 190.6 (252,000 / 1,322, all fixations). Day's own adaptive-only reading (1,408) gives 179.0 and his 2nd-edition figure (1,587) gives 158.8 (addendum l.11; recomputed). Comparing a human *adaptive* count against an LTEE count of *all* fixations gives Day about 6%.

### NOTE-2. Coding-only counts are floors
Regulatory and other non-coding adaptive substitutions are not counted (R4-A2e-alpha l.45; refresh-2026-10-10b l.49). Every count above is a lower bound on the quantity A2e's formal statement names. No sourced non-coding number is on record, so this widens the margin by an unknown amount and is not scored.

### NOTE-3. The framework objection (AS 3.6, MW5) cuts against Day's own inputs
AS 3.6 notes that the MK contrast assumes the neutral model for the reference sites, "the framework in dispute". Day's A2e argument is stated in the same frame: generations per fixation, N_e, fixation counts from Good 2017, and elsewhere Kimura's 1/(2N) (claim B). He cannot drop the frame for the human estimate and keep it for his ceiling. **Reasoned, not sourced:** the main known bias of the MK contrast in humans (slightly deleterious polymorphism) pushes alpha *down*, which is why naive estimates sit at 0.2-7.6% and corrected ones at 9-14%.

## What the evidence costs the critic side (stated as prominently as the above)
1. **Two data sets, not three estimators.** The claim comment (l.14) and the resolution (l.186: "three estimators ... would all need a ~4-fold error in the same direction") count the aMK baseline (0.076) as a third leg. It comes from Uricchio's own data, in the same paper. The independent support is two data sets (Boyko 2008, Uricchio 2019), both in the MK/SFS family. A shared model error (demography, background selection, ancestral-state errors) could move both. The record should say "two data sets".
2. **No blind test.** The Uricchio values, the HPD and the "~799" figure were in the docstring before the run (MINOR-1 of the combined review, l.192). The trigger and blockers were set by the scorer (MAJOR-2, l.188). The critic case therefore rests on the published interval, not on a prediction that could have failed. It is strong on arithmetic and weak on process, and the review tier is the remedy D-21 chose.
3. **No search strategy** (AR-05, GRADE l.330). Four quantitative sources were gathered ad hoc. A later or larger human-lineage, bias-corrected alpha with its upper bound below about 1.7% (N2) or 0.64% (own base) would move the field. None is on record, but none was searched for systematically either.
4. **Model inference.** Every count is inferred, none observed (D6). MINOR-1 asks only that this be stated for both sides of the inequality.
5. **Other scopes remain open.** Strong-only and fitness-weighted readings stay `contested`; the total-throughput basis waits on A5c. Nothing here touches them.
