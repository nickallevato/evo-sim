## Headline findings
1. **Arithmetic.** Every headline power reproduces. The CV of the beneficial-allele count does fall as 1/√n, as Day says. On the "specific" reading his conclusion is right, even allowing recurrent mutation (10^−8.4e6).
2. **None of the p^n numbers is the probability that n fixations occur in the window.**
   - Ga and G4 price a pre-specified list of arisings. Gb prices one genotype, which Day's own s7.10 says is not needed.
   - p^n does not depend on timing, so it cannot force sequential fixation.
   - **Day's own Gd (s7.9) is an "any n of M" calculation:** 157,000/0.02 = 7.85e6.
   - The book's step just before the Darwillion (secondhand, via McCarthy; neutral p = 1/2N) is also an "any" comparison: "one in 23,000" required against 1 in 20,000 available. Read that way, the book's own inputs meet the requirement.
3. **The interchangeable case flips sharply, but only for adaptive changes.**
   - With n_f required functional changes, each satisfiable by m interchangeable alternatives, P(all achieved) jumps from about 0 to about 1 as λ (the Poisson mean of successful arisings per required change) crosses 12.3–17.2. The band is 4.07 wide.
   - This applies only to the adaptive subset. Neutral differences have no "required" function.
   - Neither m nor the adaptive n_f is established in the corpus. At n_f = 2e7 and s = 0.001, m\* is close to every possible SNV change (Day-favourable bound).
4. **The Barrier mechanism depends on the fitness model.**
   - Under multiplicative fitness (Day's stated s3.1 model) the expected per-locus response does not fall with n. No cap appears near 230.
   - Under additive fitness, which is the scale of 14.7 and 1,570, a dilution appears. **What it depends on is set by the convention, and was settled by computation (post-hoc):**
     - If fixed alleles still count, the dilution depends on *total alleles carried*, so a sequential chain is diluted as much or more.
     - If only segregating alleles count, the dilution is *concurrency-specific*: 0.85× at 230 and 0.60× at 1,000 concurrent loci.
     - If effects are normalised to the mean, there is none.
5. **The reproductive excess depends on the configuration (post-hoc grid).**
   - In the s3 configuration (157,000 loci all at p = 0.5, s = 0.01, N = 10⁴), best/mean relative fitness is about 340 and N_eff about 333.
   - It is 1.33 at Day's own s7.8 standing crop of 230 loci, about 1.3 for his s7.8 steady-state pipeline, and 2.1 at s = 0.001.
   - When var(ln w) is O(1), per-locus P_fix falls by about 14% (scaled run). That is a real penalty, but only where var(ln w) is O(1).

**Internal validity**
- **The event priced.** None of these is the probability that n fixations occur in the window.
- **Timing.** p^n is the same whether n specified one-shot events succeed together or one after another. The strongest serial reading has been considered too: all attempts must succeed, and retries are spread over time. Even then, per-site retries run in parallel across sites; q = 0.38 per specified nucleotide over the window is exactly that. So the formula does not force serialisation.
- **What can be credited to Day.** He states that interference pushes joint success *below* p^n under concurrency. Part C supports that direction for clonal and tightly linked genomes (see §4), not for free recombination.

## 3. Specific vs any (part B; P2 and P3 held)
**Any n of M.**
- P_any = P(Binomial(M, p) ≥ n), with the transition centred at M₅₀ = n/p.
- The 5–95% band has relative half-width ≈ 1.645/√n (the √(1−p) factor is immaterial for p ≤ 0.02). That is ±5.2% at n = 10³, ±0.42% at 157,000 and ±0.037% at 2e7. The switch is sharp for n of 10⁵ or more.
- Exact scipy values at the band edges are 0.046–0.050 and 0.948–0.952.

| n | p | M₅₀ = n/p | Beneficial fraction needed (supply 4.5e11 McCarthy / 2.3e11 Gd basis) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 157,000 | 0.02 | 7.85e6 | 1.7e-5 / 3.4e-5 |
| 200,000 (Day's "99% neutral" scenario) | 0.02 / 0.002 | 1e7 / 1e8 | 2.2e-5 / 4.3e-5; 2.2e-4 / 4.3e-4 |
| 2e7 (all differences required as adaptive; Ga's framing) | 0.02 / 0.002 | 1e9 / 1e10 | 2.2e-3 / 4.3e-3; 2.2e-2 / 4.3e-2 |

For n = 2e7 the "any" reading needs 0.2–4% of *all* new mutations to be beneficial at s = 0.01–0.001. That is a demanding external requirement on the all-adaptive framing. For n ≤ 2e5 it needs 2e-5 to 4e-4. The DFE evidence that would settle this is not in the corpus.

**McCarthy's inputs** (M = 4.5e11, p = 5e-5, n = 2e7). The requirement lies 527 SD below the mean, so P_any is indistinguishable from 1 *under his neutral k = μ model*, which branch B contests. (The Chernoff bound puts the failure probability at ≤ 10^−62,689.)

**The middle case of Day's dilemma, formalised.**
- Take n_f required functional changes. Each can be met by any one of m interchangeable alternatives, and each alternative independently succeeds (arises *and* fixes in the window) with probability q.
- Then P_all = (1 − (1−q)^m)^{n_f} = (1 − e^{−λ})^{n_f}, where **λ = −m·ln(1−q)**.
- λ is the Poisson mean number of successful arisings per required change. q is the chance of at least one. λ ≈ m·q only for small q. At the specific-nucleotide q = 0.33–0.38, m·q at the flip is 13.7 rather than 17.2 for n_f = 2e7.
- m = 1 is the specific horn.

**Assumptions of the middle-case model, stated so the bias direction is visible:**
- Alternatives are independent, equally beneficial (same s), non-epistatic and available throughout the window. The n_f requirements are independent of each other.
- λ is homogeneous across requirements.
- These assumptions **favour the interchangeable case**. Stepping-stone dependence and functional-island structure (Day's branch-D premise) would lower the effective m.
- A small fraction of truly unique requirements (m = 1, factor about 0.38 each) is very costly.
- Cross-gene or polygenic routes, and a smaller n_f of larger-effect changes, would favour the critics' case.
- The sharp 4-unit band holds only for homogeneous λ.

**Reading the table (neutral as to biology):**
- **Specific horn (m = 1):** P ≈ 10^−8.4e6 even with recurrent mutation. Day's conclusion holds. McCarthy concedes it too ("hitting a lottery … 20 million times in a row would indeed essentially be probability zero"). But no critic defends a pre-specified-list reading, and Day calls the Darwillion "rhetorical" (G4). So the agreement is about a reading nobody advocates.
- **The threshold applies only to the adaptive subset.**
  - Critics hold that most differences are neutral (Nesslig20 PART I; Mansfield as quoted by Day; KITTENS §12: "The number of adaptive substitutions on the human lineage is a genuine research question, and estimates are modest and contested").
  - For a neutral difference no function is required, so it adds no requirement (P ≈ 1) to *this* product. Whether the neutral *count* is reachable is branch B (k = μ is disputed).
  - The n_f = 2e7 row treats every difference as an adaptive requirement (Ga's framing). n_f = 2e5 is Day's own scenario input ("Even if 99% of divergence is neutral, 200,000 fixations remain required", Z18165980). It is not a measured adaptive count.
  - Context only: `docs/research/ledgers/gaps.md` GAP-01 (**an unreviewed audit note**, not a source) gathers α estimates implying K_a ≈ 0 to about 10⁴ adaptive substitutions per lineage. At n_f = 10³–10⁴, m\* = 16–41 at s = 0.01 and 152–410 at s = 0.001, which is 0.0002–0.04% of possible SNV changes.
- **The genome bound cuts for Day at high n_f and low s.** At n_f = 2e7 with s = 0.001, the flip needs 77% (T = 3e5) to 158% (d-corrected T) of all possible SNV changes to be equally good alternatives. The interchangeable case cannot flip there in this model.
- **Per-site m is not established.** Branch D has per-*sequence* prevalences of domain-level function that span about 53 orders of magnitude: Axe 2004, about 1e-77 (D10; no Day text cites it); Taylor 2001, f ≈ 2e-24 (D11); Keefe & Szostak 2001 (D12). These are not per-site alternatives and cannot be converted into m without a model of how many sites or paths realise a function. → `external: contested`.
- **Throughput is a separate question, and it is conditional on n_f:**
  - n_f = 2e5 over 2.52e5–3e5 generations is 0.67–0.79 adaptive fixations per generation. That is above every λ tested in H2-hard (max 0.4/gen).
  - n_f = 1e3–1e4 is 0.003–0.04/gen, inside the tested range.
  - So the throughput objection is live only if adaptive n_f is around 1e5 or more.

## 4. Is multiplying probabilities valid when fixations co-occur? (part C, plus post-hoc g1b `linked`)
Setup: haploid WF, N = 1000, s = 0.01, multiplicative fitness, soft selection. L = 50 new mutations were introduced at t = 0 in distinct individuals. Kimura u = 0.01980.

- **Free recombination: independence holds.** p^k is the right joint probability for a *specified* set, and K is Poisson/binomial. Here the "any" count and the "specific" product are each correct for their own event.
- **Linkage and clonality: Day's direction holds.** Fixations become negatively dependent, so joint success falls *below* p^k, as Day says in Z18165980. Per-locus P_fix and K's dispersion fall too.
- Camestros' objection that Day "states these are independent events without justification" (Reading Vox Day 2026 [4], 2026-01-29) is borne out: independence holds only with recombination.
- **Pre-registered prediction failed at 1 M.** I predicted a pair ratio of 0.5–0.95 and got 1.015 ± 0.022. The 1 M map was too loose at this concurrency (about 2 cM spacing, about 12 recombination events per sweep). It did not show that interference is absent. At 0.05 M and 0.005 M the ratio is 0.84 and 0.65, inside the predicted range (post-hoc, after reviewer scratch values of 0.889 and 0.629).
- For scale: 157,000 loci over a genome of about 35 M is about 4,500 loci/M, between the two tight maps. Free recombination is Day's own stipulation (s7.10), so testing it is fair to the claim, but it is optimistic for humans at high concurrency.
- **Clonal vs free bears on G1/A5f.** The LTEE G_f comes from clonal, interference-penalised populations, while Ga's independence is invoked for recombining humans. At this concurrency a clonal-derived rate is plausibly a *lower* bound for a recombining population. KITTENS §11 makes the related transfer point: "applying one constant rate to a genome that differs by orders of magnitude in size, mutation supply and recombination … builds the same assumption in at the aggregate level". This is a modelling indication, not a measured human ceiling.
- **Rate vs product (G1).** E[K] = Σp_i over arisings, which is rate × time. With free recombination K is Poisson-like (dispersion 1.03). So the right quantity for "n fixations in T" is a throughput, as G1 and G1b say, not p^n.

**Steelman disagreement 1: what does the additive dilution depend on?** (post-hoc g1b `dilution`; N = 1000, s = 0.01, p₀ = 0.1, free recombination)

Ratios are to the single-locus u, with t_fix in parentheses.

| Convention | Single locus | Concurrent L = 230 | Concurrent L = 1000 | Sequential chain, total 230 | Sequential chain, total 1000 | Other |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mult, w = (1+s)^c | 0.98 (608) | 1.00 (609) | 0.99 (602) | 0.99 (610) | 1.00 (608) | — |
| add_incl, w = 1 + s(c + F_fixed) [g1 part D] | 1.00 (616) | 0.83 (1000) | 0.54 (1428) | **0.74** (996) | **0.40** (1378) | L = 230 on 770 already fixed: 0.26 (1791); one locus on 999 fixed: 0.25 (1900) |
| add_excl, w = 1 + s·c (segregating only) | 1.00 (608) | **0.85** (873) | **0.60** (1265) | 1.00 (608) | 1.01 (604) | — |
| add_norm, w = 1 + s(c − c̄) | 0.99 (602) | 0.99 (599) | 0.97 (605) | 0.99 (605) | 1.00 (605) | — |

**Answer.** The dilution is a property of the fitness convention, not of "additivity" as such.
- **add_incl** (the convention in the original part D): the dilution depends on *total beneficial alleles carried*. A strictly sequential chain of the same total is diluted *more* (0.40 against 0.54 at 1,000). 230 concurrent loci on a background of 770 already fixed behave like one locus on 999 fixed (0.26 against 0.25). On this convention the effect is **not a parallel-specific barrier**: it would equally hit Day's sequential 180 and any long adaptive chain. **The critic-side reading is confirmed for this convention.**
- **add_excl** (fixed advantages re-baselined, segregating ones additive in absolute terms): the dilution is **concurrency-specific**. It gives 0.85× at 230 and 0.60× at 1,000 concurrent loci, and none in a chain. This is the only tested convention under which a parallel-specific barrier of order 10²–10³ loci appears. **The Day-side reading is confirmed for this convention.**
- **add_norm** (each allele worth s relative to the current mean) and **mult**: no dilution.
- **Which convention is Day's?** Day's s3 numbers (157,000 loci all segregating, nothing fixed) are consistent with add_excl or with add_incl at F = 0. The paper does not say how fixed alleles count. His s3.1 states multiplicative fitness and flags diminishing-returns epistasis as strengthening the Barrier (citing Wei & Zhang 2019, not retrieved). So the paper does not pin the convention. Which one describes hominin fitness is external (`contested`).
- The mechanism in every diluting case is mean-fitness inflation (s/w̄). It is not the LLN: the count's CV does not enter.
