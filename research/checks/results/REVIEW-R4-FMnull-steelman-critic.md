# Review of R4-FMnull (steelman, critics' side)

Reviewer role: argue as the most capable and honest critic of Day's "Fourth Mechanism" would (a population geneticist who holds that substitution density is overdispersed across windows in every lineage through regional mutation-rate variation, CpG content, biased gene conversion, ancestral coalescence depth, linked selection and alignment artefacts, so that a large variance-to-mean index is expected and is no evidence for a new mechanism). Questions asked: does the premise carry any weight for FM10 at any window where it is true; is lineage-sharing stated as prominently as the Day-side points; what does the rule license about a number that cannot be reproduced as stated; is the band too narrow anywhere; is the null unfalsifiable; could S1-S4 have fired; does "no verdict move" withhold anything the registration licenses; is Day credited with the audit's own misses.

Tier: load-bearing, three reviews. The correctness review (af8a960) and the Day-side steelman (5a36667) are taken as read and not repeated. Where I disagree with the Day-side review I say so in the finding ("Conflict with Day-side #n").

Reviewed (2026-10-13, head 6c5d8cf): `R4-FMnull.md` (b3c0ab3), whole; both existing reviews, whole; docstring of `fm_mesa_null.py` (model, S1-S4, PREDICTIONS, SCORING, A4-A10); `REVIEW-R4-FMnull-prerun.md` (MAJOR-6 to MAJOR-9 and resolutions); `raw/fmnull_sim.json`, `fmnull_real.json`, `fmnull_post.json`, `fm_analyse.json`, `fm_mirror_analyse.json`; `R4-FM.md` (headline table, Reading, mirror test); Q162-Q171; claim files FM3, FM10 (verdict fields); `refresh-2026-10-12c-lit.md`; FML-S2, FML-S8, FML-M3 in `quotes-literature.md`; R4-X1 rules C, N, F, U, RD; ST-10. No literature is cited from memory.

**Reviewer's numbers are post hoc and descriptive.** From the committed json files and the committed script's `score` mode (one process, seconds, nothing committed; `analytic`, `simulate`, `realdata` not run). They are for the fix pass to check. None is registered.

Overall. The run is honest and I agree with the bottom line: no verdict *value* moves. The write-up is nonetheless tilted against the critics in its wording, in six places. (a) It never states the one fact that settles the premise: there is no reading, lineage, masking or window up to 1 Mb at which Day's sentence is true of the data and the measured index is outside a neutral band. (b) It reports as "calibration-dependent" a set of cells where the variants disagree only between two critic-side labels. (c) It books as a "failure" of the null, and as Day credit, the result that variation in the de novo mutation rate seen in human pedigrees is by itself enough to produce the measured index. (d) It gives lineage-sharing one bullet near the end. (e) It calls the alignment term "unsourced" without mentioning that R4-FM measured it by changing the reference. (f) It lists as Day-side results a class of the number 80 at windows where nothing measured is within 8x of 80, and a miss of the audit's own hand estimate. Where the critics' case stops is in findings 9, 10 and 14.
