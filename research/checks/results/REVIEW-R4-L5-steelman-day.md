## The two questions in the brief

**1. Is the withheld C-external flip rightly withheld? Yes, and on more grounds than the write-up gives.** See S1 (endorsement and two extra grounds), S2 (the scoped content is stated for the zero only, while Day's own reported data bite inside and next to the registered scope), S5 (R-a's status).

**2. Is the registered s range fair to him? Not entirely.** The two bands were drawn so that both band calls were fixed by the registration's own P3 arithmetic, and the one grid point between them (s = 0.02) is where Day's own non-zero statistic, on the validated design, already excludes the required rate. The write-up reports s\* only for his zero statistics, at grid ceilings, and states "neither show nor exclude" more widely than its own json supports (S2, S3, S4).

## Findings

- **S1 (NOTE). The withholding is right; two further grounds.** Section 8 (l.258-261) withholds on the weak-test caveat and on reading R-c. Both stand. I checked the K5 condition against Q275 (comparator: "approximately 20 million fixations over the 9 million years ... approximately 15,500 fixations per 7,000-year period", no s, no mechanism), Q276 (the inference) and Q277/Q278 (framing: "evolution by natural selection"). Nothing in them fixes the s of the 15,500, so R-c is the literal state of the source, not the audit's construction. Two further grounds the write-up does not state:
  (a) *R-a turns a premise of Day's reductio into his prediction.* Q44 gives s = 0.001 "(selection coefficient, Zeng et al 2021)", a mainstream estimate, in an argument that a fixation takes "t ≈ 19,800 generations". In Day's frame, at that s the required rate is not invisible, it is unattainable. Reading R-a puts that s into the comparator and then scores the comparator as predicting zero.
  (b) *The scoped content concerns the zero only* (S2). Under R-b at Day-side clocks his own reported M10 already bears on s = 0.01. A field value written as "contradicted (scoped: s <= 1e-2)" would say more than the run shows.
  **What this costs Day (record it as prominently):** the withholding protects the field value, not the inference. Under R-a, R-b and R-e the sentence "This result decisively favors the MITTENS prediction over the Modern Synthesis expectation" (Q276) fails as an inference from the zero. At s <= 0.01 the zero is predicted by the required rate, by the mainstream model even at 100 times its rate, and by neutrality, and the only beneficial s values in his own corpus (0.001, Q44; 0.01, see S6) lie in that range. The only reading that rescues the zero inference (R-d) is one he never states.

- **S4 (MINOR). The printed crossovers are grid ceilings, and on Z50 / Z10 they are also biased against Day. - **S7 (MINOR). The non-independence caveat is scoped to s >= 0.05, but at low s it is two to three orders larger, and it is the very question Day's corpus disputes.** Open 7 (l.354) and Result 2 limit C10 to s >= 0.05 (about 28,000 sweeps in flight). With the write-up's own sweep time (2/s) ln(2N), 15,556 completions per 280 generations need about **110,000 concurrent sweeps at s = 0.01** (one per 0.033 cM) and **1.1 million at s = 0.001** (one per 0.0033 cM) (post hoc arithmetic). Day's own pipeline figure is "approximately 230 simultaneous sweeps" (Q25) at s = 0.01 (Q26, Q27). Interference would slow sweeps, so the low-s zeros are not threatened. But R-a's and R-b's "zero is what the required rate itself predicts" (l.303) assumes the required rate can run as up to 10^6 independent sweeps, and whether it can is the MITTENS question, which L5 does not test. **Fix:** extend Open 7 to s <= 0.01 with these figures, and add to R-a / R-b: "if the rate is attainable at that s as independent sweeps; whether it is (Q25-Q27) is not tested here".

- **S8 (MINOR). C1a has readings and no Readings table; under one of them the run tests Day's sign and he is right.** C1a's Statement has two clauses: "such bias would favor detecting recent fixations, **not obscure them**". (i) *"Not obscure"*, read absolutely: L5 tests it. At s >= 0.05 linked panel SNPs register hundreds to thousands of events. At s <= 0.01 the window, not the panel, removes the power (l.225). Supported for strong sweeps. The proposed comment (l.271) says this but calls the claim untested. (ii) *"Favor", against a random-site sample of the same size*: this is the claim file's own formal wording ("higher than for a random site sample", C1a l.44), and the comparator the audit's own C1 point 1 used for the critics' argument (uniform scaling, "4.8 events"; now recorded as an error, l.228). Against it the swept standing site is on the panel 240 to 670 times more often, and a random-site panel, mostly invariant, would carry few hitchhikers. On these figures, supported. (iii) *Against whole-genome data*: wrong (the panel loses 75% to 91%). (iv) *Against a random-SNP panel*: not computed. The fix pass withdrew the credit on (iii) and (iv) (K3) without recording (i) and (ii), which rule RD requires ("Record every plausible reading with its verdict"). **Fix:** a C1a Readings table in section 8 with all four.
