## Honest concessions (points against Day that stand)
- **The flip is robust to how humans recombine.** kappa\* is identical across map 1.5, map 36.8 and free. Even clonal humans exceed the LTEE rate at kappa = 93,659 (F = 7.3-167). Day's "no recombination overhead" argument does not reach the threshold.
- **Day's own cost limits all exceed the LTEE rate per generation** (F cap 1.98, 4.41, 33.9). If both H and the LTEE were ceilings, the LTEE would bind and H would be redundant for A (P6).
- **On Day's own figures the human population receives more new mutations per generation than the LTEE**: 2 x 1e4 x 38.4 = 768,000 vs 3.3e7 x 4.1e-4 = 13,530, or 57x (19x at N_h 3,300). This contradicts "effectively unlimited mutation supply" as an LTEE advantage (p.4).
- Day's in-paper human N_e (1e4-3.3e4) lowers kappa\* (65.8 at 3.3e4).
- Day's own adaptive-only figure (1,408) is close to 1,322. The Day-favourable G_f = 2,890 is the audit's construction, not his.

## Verdict view (Day steelman)
Per generation: `contested`. The coupled-s grid puts the flip below the derived supply ratio, but the ratio of beneficial fraction and effect size that decides it is unmeasured. Day's own text (s4.2) points toward a smaller human s, and a plausible factor (>= 430 central) restores the ceiling. Per year: out of scope for A2e. Internal: non-sequitur stands. The "unlimited supply" premise is contradicted by his own numbers, and the steelman does not dispute that.
