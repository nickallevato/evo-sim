# R4 X1: the verdict rule (revision 2; one rule, applied to Day, critics and allies alike)

Revision 1 was written in the X1 fix pass, after the verdicts were known. A blind audit (`REVIEW-R4-X1-rule-audit.md`) applied it to Day's 20 error nodes and all 51 critic nodes and agreed on 90% of Day calls and on 100% of critic error / no-error calls, but found clauses that bite one side harder. This revision answers it (section 9). The re-score under it is `R4-X1-rescore.md` (generated by `x1_rescore.py`). Nothing here edits a claim file; the lead applies the re-score and the corrections in section 8 after checking them.

Because revision 1 was written with the verdicts in view, the rule and the re-score are not an independent test of each other; the independent test is the audit.

## 0. Definitions

- **Author's own inputs and premises** = what the quoted source states, plus text the claim itself cites. A premise that is false or contested is not a defect in the author's reasoning; it is external (N1).
- **epsilon** = |stated - recomputed| / recomputed, with the recomputation made from the author's stated inputs and method, **on the most charitable reading of an ambiguous referent** (rule C).
- **Stated conclusion** = the literal assertion the author draws from the number: a figure, a match ("38M is close to the observed"), a negligibility ("a rounding error"), an inequality ("less than half"), a bound. It is tested **on the author's own basis** (their unit, their model, their count of events or SNVs), not on a basis the scorer prefers.
- **Omitted term** = a term absent from the author's model. A different value for a term the author did include (Day's Ne_anc in B6a) is a contested premise value (N1), not an omitted term.
- **Slip ledger (L)** = one table of every slip on any side with source, locator, epsilon and the clause that put it there. A ledger entry changes no node verdict.

## C. Charity (new)

Before any slip, mismatch or conclusion is scored against an author, the scorer tries the most charitable reading of every ambiguous referent: parts vs totals in a list, per lineage vs pairwise, additive vs log scale, per haploid vs per diploid, a spoken number vs a caption, a stated premise of a different participant. The reading tried and its result are recorded on every row of the re-score ("Charitable reading tried"), whether or not it rescues the node. Applied on both sides. A3a and G are the two Day nodes this rescues (section 1).

## R1. Materiality: one test for printed-number slips and omitted-term slips

Both kinds of slip are judged the same way. Recompute the number, or the comparison, with the correction, on the author's own basis. The slip is an **error** if, and only if, the correction:

- (i) moves the stated conclusion by more than 25%, or flips it (a sign, an inequality, a match that becomes a mismatch); or
- (ii) moves a downstream number stated in the same source by more than 25%.

A figure that is itself the stated conclusion (a headline total) counts under (i): epsilon over 25% on that figure is an error. Otherwise it is a ledger entry; epsilon of 5% or less is rounding and is not even ledgered.

Labels: a printed-number slip that is an error is `arithmetic-error`. An omitted-term slip that is an error is `non-sequitur` (the conclusion does not follow from the author's own basis once the omitted term is counted).

**N3 (omitted terms) is therefore one case of R1, not a separate escape.** The same external value must be used for every node it is applied to: the ancestral polymorphism term is theta_anc L = 4 Ne_anc mu L at Yoo's human-chimp-gorilla node, Ne_anc = 1.32e5 (20.28M), with the human-chimp-bonobo node 1.98e5 (30.41M) as the upper case. If the author asserts no match or negligibility (keruru B4g, "an unreconciled twofold"), there is no stated conclusion that the omitted term can change, and the node is `holds`.

**R1c. Input looseness (revised).** A figure that is not reproduced at the author's stated input but is reproduced when the input is moved within the author's own stated looseness ("about" or "around" X is X +-25%) is not an arithmetic-error **only where the output is roughly linear in that input** (absolute elasticity d ln output / d ln input of about 1.5 or less across the range). For steep outputs (tail probabilities, exponentials) the rescue does not apply: keruru's p = 0.5 value has elasticity -92 in 2N, so +-25% in Ne moves it by a factor of about 10^9 and any figure could be rescued. For steep outputs where the method is unretrieved the node is `pending` and fidelity `unverifiable`.

**R1d. Author-flagged provisional numbers.** A number the author has marked unresolved at the number itself (`[CHECK]`, "to be confirmed") is ledgered, not scored. A general disclaimer ("back of the napkin") does not count.

**Justification of the 25% line.** (1) It is inside the spread of the audit's own inputs (mu 1.1 to 1.5e-8 is +-15 to 20%; the measured E. coli rate has a 95% CI of -55% to +57%; Ne "about 10,000" is quoted as 7,000 to 20,000). (2) The slips found fall into two groups with a gap: 12 to 13% (Day A5e, Day G1a, Hancock's "4e-5") and then 33% or more. (3) Clause (ii) catches the small slips that matter (Day B6a). **Sensitivity:** at a 10% line the three 12 to 13% slips become errors; at 5% nothing else moves (the next are 2.4% and 2.6%).

## S. Scope: which text is scored against the claim

- **S1.** A node is scored on its verbatim Statement quotes and the derivation chain in its Formal statement. A sentence that carries a verdict must be quoted in the Statement; where it is not, the claim file should add it (section 8).
- **S2.** A slip elsewhere in the same source (a Substack or YouTube comment, a reply, a spoken aside, a caption, a side example in a post or paper) is a ledger entry and does not change the node, whichever side wrote it. This covers Day's blog comments and replies and critics' comments and video captions identically.
- **S3.** Exception: if the slipped number is a premise of an argument and no node holds it, it gets its own node, scored by this rule. Applied to Day's s6.4 "38,400" (proposed node `A5h`). Not applied to McCarthy's "35 million" (comment 340149310): "35 million, not 180" is unchanged by 20M.
- **Node extraction is outside the rule.** Which comments became nodes (Day's C7 and B9 from a comment thread; the critics' A2i, B3i and G5) was decided at extraction, not by this rule. It is recorded as an exposure, not corrected.

## SC. Self-corrected slips

A slip corrected in the same source object (same video, same post revision, same URL after an edit) is a ledger entry marked SC (Hancock's 76.8). A slip corrected in a later, separate source is scored on the version quoted, with the note "corrected in X" (Day's B3e, withdrawn in B3g; Day's H1 retraction holds).

## N. Non-sequitur

A conclusion is a `non-sequitur` if, with the author's stated premises and model:

- **N2a** it fails when recomputed (the author's own table or equation gives another value);
- **N2b** it needs a premise the author's own text contradicts or excludes;
- **N2c** it rests on a circular match (fitted to the target);
- **N2d** it is stronger than the premises support;
- **R1** an omitted term, tested as above, moves the stated conclusion by more than 25%.

- **N1.** A disagreement about whether a premise is true is external, not a non-sequitur.
- **N4.** A flagged hypothetical or illustration is scored on its arithmetic only.
- **N5.** A conclusion truncated or unrecoverable from the quote is `pending`.

**N2b exposure, stated openly (clause unchanged).** "Contradicted by the author's own text" exposes authors who have written more. Day's corpus is 39 Zenodo records and 154 kept blog posts and replies, with multi-step arguments across documents; most critic claims are one-line identities from a single comment, post or talk. So the Day error count partly measures output volume. "Own text" means the same source and texts the claim cites; B3c's former "contradicts the companion paper" basis is cross-document and has been dropped. The re-score reports errors per 10,000 quoted words beside the per-node rate; derivation steps per node were not counted.

## F. Fidelity for uncited inputs

- `accurate`: cited and read correctly. `partial`: cited, read with a flagged omission or error. `misread`: the cited source text is in hand and contradicts the reading. `unverifiable`: a numeric input stated as an empirical fact without citation (non-standard), or cited but not retrievable, or a garbled relay. `n/a`: no external input borrowed (own derivation, definition, flagged hypothetical, position statement).
- `partial` is not used for uncited inputs. Standard values are exempt from the citation requirement: generation time 20 to 30 y, genome 3.1 to 3.2 Gb, human mu 1.1 to 1.5e-8 per site, about 60 to 100 de novo mutations per newborn, world population, E. coli genome size.
- **Contested-standard: human Ne about 1e4.** It is exempt from citation, but it is flagged, because Day contests it as circular (B3h, C5a). Any node whose conclusion depends on it carries the flag in its external column ("contested"), never in its internal verdict. It is not silently exempt.
- Day's `n/a` nodes that cite an accurate source are a bookkeeping change to `accurate` and are listed, not changed.

## U. Unidentified authors, relays, and counts

- A passage whose author is unidentified (Day's Milton blockquote, D8), a relay of an unidentified speaker (B5d), or an ally relaying another's work (D14) is `n/a` internal (not scored), fidelity `unverifiable`, and its slips go to the ledger. C2b's regression was run by an unidentified "doctor": only Day's interpretation is scored.
- **Allies are never merged with critics.**
- **Denominator.** A node is numeric if the author's **quoted Statement text** contains an equation or operator, or a number with a magnitude, unit or percent word (same regex on every side; the audit's own `derived:` lines are not counted). E1 (no verbatim critic quotation) and the U-excluded nodes (B5d, D8) are in no denominator. The formal-statement version used in revision 1 and the all-files version are reported beside it.
