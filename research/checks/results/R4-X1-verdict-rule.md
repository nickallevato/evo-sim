# R4 X1: the verdict rule (one rule, applied to Day, critics and allies alike)

Written 2026-10-09 in the X1 fix pass, after the three reviews found that X1 had no stated rule, that the Day-side steelman found critic slips excused that Day had been penalised for, and that the critic-side steelman found the reverse. This file is the rule. The re-score under it is in `R4-X1-rescore.md` (generated by `x1_rescore.py`). Nothing here edits a claim file; the lead applies the re-score after checking it.

The rule changes the vocabulary of the audit, not its evidence. Where it gives a different answer from the earlier claim-file verdicts, that is stated in the re-score table.

## 0. Definitions

- **Author's own inputs and premises** = what the quoted source states, including the author's own earlier or companion text where the claim cites it. A premise that is merely false or contested is not a defect in the author's internal reasoning; it is external.
- **epsilon** (relative error) = |stated - recomputed| / recomputed, with the recomputation made from the author's stated inputs and method, on the most charitable reading of an ambiguous referent.
- **Slip ledger (L)** = one table of every numeric slip found on any side, with source, locator, epsilon, whether it propagates, and the clause that put it in the ledger. A ledger entry does not change a node verdict. Both sides are ledgered by the same procedure; the counts are reported side by side.

## R1. Materiality: `arithmetic-error` or `holds` with a ledger entry

A printed number is an `arithmetic-error` on the node whose Statement or derivation contains it if either:

- **R1a, size:** epsilon > 25%; or
- **R1b, propagation:** the slip changes a downstream number stated in the same source by > 25%, or flips a stated conclusion (a sign, an inequality, a "less than half"), at any epsilon.

Otherwise:

- epsilon <= 5% is rounding (no entry, no tag);
- 5% < epsilon <= 25% without propagation is a **ledger entry** and the node stays `holds`.

**R1c, input looseness.** A figure that is not reproduced at the author's stated input but is reproduced when that input is moved within the author's own stated looseness (an input given as "about" or "around" X is read as X +-25%) is **not** an arithmetic-error. The node is `holds` (or `pending` if the method is unretrieved) with the comment "not reproduced at stated X; reproduced at X' (within +-25%)". Applied to: keruru C5.

**R1d, author-flagged provisional numbers.** A number the author has marked unresolved at the number itself (`[CHECK]`, "to be confirmed", a DRAFT watermark on the figure) is ledgered, not scored. A general disclaimer ("back of the napkin", "rough") does not count.

**Justification of the 25% line.**
1. It sits inside the spread of the audit's own inputs (mu 1.1 to 1.5e-8 is +-15 to 20%; the measured E. coli rate carries a 95% CI of -55% to +57%; Ne "about 10,000" is routinely quoted as 7,000 to 20,000), so a slip smaller than that cannot change any verdict a reader would draw.
2. The observed slips fall into two groups with a gap: 12 to 13% (Day A5e 12%, Day G1a 12% on the charitable reading, critic Hancock 4e-5 13%) and then 33% or more (the next is G1a on its uncharitable reading, then McCarthy's 75%, keruru's ratio, Day A3a 85%, Day C6 86%, Day G, Day s6.4 900%). The line is placed in the gap rather than fitted to a verdict.
3. R1b catches the small slips that matter (Day B6a: "less than half" is 0.6, which flips the sign of the net adjustment).

**Sensitivity.** At a 10% line the three 12 to 13% slips become `arithmetic-error` (Day +2, critics +1 if the speech slip is scored). At 5% nothing else moves (the next largest sub-threshold slips are 2.4% and 2.6%). The re-score reports both.

## S. Scope: which text is scored against the claim

- **S1.** A node is scored on its verbatim Statement quotes and the derivation chain in its Formal statement.
- **S2.** A slip anywhere else in the same source (a Substack or YouTube comment, a reply, a spoken aside, a caption, a side example in a post or paper) is a **ledger entry**. It does not change the node's verdict, whichever side wrote it. The same applies to Day's blog comments and replies and to critics' comments and video captions. Spoken numbers in auto-captions are treated as caption-noise unless the same number is recoverable elsewhere in the same talk and conflicts.
- **S3.** The exception: if the slipped number is used as a premise of an argument and no node holds it, it gets its own node, scored by this rule. Applied to Day's s6.4 "38,400" (proposed node `A5h`): the number feeds 0.999^3,840 = 0.02 and 0.99^3,840 = 1e-17, an argument that the supermutator human would not survive. Not applied to McCarthy's "35 million" (comment 340149310): "35 million right there, not 180" is unchanged by 20M, so it is a ledger entry (epsilon 75%, immaterial, comment-level).

## SC. Self-corrected slips

- A slip corrected **in the same source object** (same video, same post or paper revision, the same URL after an edit) is a ledger entry marked SC; no node effect. Applied to Hancock's 76.8 (diploid genome, corrected on screen at 01:52:48).
- A slip corrected in a **later, separate source** is scored on the version quoted, with the note "corrected in X". The corrected statement is its own node if one exists. Applied to Day's B3e (kept as scored, withdrawn in B3g) and Day's H1 (Term 3 retraction: the retraction holds).

## N. Non-sequitur

A conclusion is a `non-sequitur` if, with the author's stated premises and model:

- **N2a** it fails when recomputed (the author's own table or equation gives another value);
- **N2b** it needs a premise that the author's own text contradicts or excludes;
- **N2c** it rests on a match that is circular (fitted to the target);
- **N2d** it is stronger than the premises support (a dilemma with a middle case, a bound with the wrong direction, a "not bottlenecked" from a positive response).

Additional clauses:

- **N1.** A disagreement about whether a premise is true is external, not a non-sequitur (it goes to the external column or fidelity).
- **N3. Omitted-term comparisons.** If the author asserts a match or a negligibility and a sourced term the author omitted changes the conclusion, the node is `non-sequitur` or `arithmetic-error` (Day B6a: "a rounding error"). If the conclusion survives with the sourced term, the node is `holds` with the comment "omitted term; conclusion survives" (Hancock and Nesslig20, "38M matches": the SNV null with ancestral polymorphism is 39.6M against 35 to 37.8M observed). If the author makes no match or negligibility assertion (keruru B4g, "unreconciled twofold"), `holds`.
- **N4.** A hypothetical or flagged illustration ("if even 2% ... ", "I present this as an illustration") is scored only on its arithmetic.
- **N5.** A conclusion that is truncated or unrecoverable from the quote is `pending`, not `holds`.

## F. Fidelity for uncited inputs

- `accurate`: the input is cited and read correctly.
- `partial`: cited, read with a flagged omission or error, or part of the inputs cited.
- `misread`: the cited source text is in hand and contradicts the reading (a source sentence, not a recollection).
- `unverifiable`: a numeric input stated as an empirical fact without a citation (non-standard), or cited but the source is not retrievable or a garbled relay.
- `n/a`: no external input is borrowed: a derivation from the author's own stated assumptions, a definition, a flagged hypothetical or illustration, or a position statement.

`partial` is not used for uncited inputs. **Standard values are exempt** (they need no citation to be `n/a`): human Ne about 1e4, generation time 20 to 30 y, genome 3.1 to 3.2 Gb, human mu 1.1 to 1.5e-8 per site, about 60 to 100 de novo mutations per newborn, world population, E. coli genome size. The rule is applied identically to Day: a non-standard uncited numeric input moves `n/a` to `unverifiable` (11 Day nodes and 4 critic nodes A5c, B5c, B5d, B5e in the re-score, plus critic C5 (method unretrieved) and A3d (slide absent), and Day H1 `partial` and B1d `unverifiable` from their own tables). Day files that cite an accurate source but carry `n/a` are a bookkeeping change to `accurate`, not an uncited-input case; they are listed in the re-score appendix and not changed.

Informal sources (comments, videos) are held to the same bar as papers on this axis; the author's own hedge ("rough", "illustration") affects `n/a` vs `unverifiable` only through N4 (flagged hypotheticals) and R1d (provisional markers on the number).

## U. Unidentified authors, ally blockquotes, and counts

- A passage whose author is not identified (Day's Milton blockquote, D8) or an ally relaying someone else (D14, Davis relaying Eden) is **not scored internal** unless it is the relayer's own derivation; fidelity is `unverifiable` if the original is not retrieved. Its slips go to the ledger.
- **Allies are never merged with critics.** Counts are reported as three columns: Day, critics, allies. The per-claim rate uses the same denominator definition on every side: files whose Formal statement has a `derived` or "Arithmetic audit" block or an explicit numeric equation (Day 81 of 112, critics 31 of 51, allies 10 of 15). The hand-listed 45 of X1 (35 critic, 10 ally) is reported beside it.

The two steelman reviews' opposite complaints are both partly right, and the rule is the same for both sides; what it cannot do is create critic-side node errors that the audit did not find. Whether the remaining gap reflects the corpus (long multi-step derivations on one side, one-line identities on the other) or an unmeasured bias in how the nodes were chosen and audited cannot be settled from these counts.
