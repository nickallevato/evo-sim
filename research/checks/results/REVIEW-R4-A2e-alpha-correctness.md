## 2. Verdict rule (R4-X1) applied

- **S:** The scored statements are Q138 and the Q130 core; the threshold is Q139. Alpha counts amino-acid substitutions only. The write-up states this and the asymmetry.
- **R1:** None of M1 or m1-m8 moves A2e's internal verdict (`non-sequitur`, set by R4-A2e). The check computes no number that Day states. Day's figure enters only as the threshold. On the external axis, M1 removes the only Day-side *count* but leaves a Day-side null estimate. m1 and m2 move the critic-side counts by up to 63% (36,138 base) and down to 3.1x (lower CL) without changing the side.
- **N:** Not applicable (no Day derivation is under test here).
- **F:** EK's "0-5%" is the audit's reading, not an input from either side, and is labelled. Uricchio is cited and standard, so it is not an F case; it is an abstract-only fidelity limit (m5).
- **C:** The charitable-to-Day reading is to count GV as Day-side, and the write-up does that. Rule C does not license reading a null (FI = 1.00) that its authors flag as a possible underestimate as a count of 29-49 (M1).
- **RH:** Correct (N4).

## 3. Verdict recommendation

**No verdict change on this review alone.** A2e external stays `contested`. Internal `non-sequitur` and the R1 "no verdict moves" statement stand. After M1, though, the Day side of the "named disagreement" is an estimate of about zero that its authors flag as biased low, set against two bias-corrected estimators. The correct description is "contested; the low end is a naive-MK null, the high end is model-based". The critic review's C2 (GV's accelerated genes) is the one extraction that could move the external verdict, and it should be done before integration.
