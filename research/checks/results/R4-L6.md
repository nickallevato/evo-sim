## Interpretation
- **Internal: holds on Day's rule.** The ≥95% snapshot count falls in 10 of 12 populations. Day's Q178 is a faithful description of his own statistic.
- **The word "fixations" is the issue (A2c/E6).** A ≥95% snapshot is not a fixation in Good 2017's sense. Under Good's hidden-Markov fixation calls, no population ever loses a fixed mutation, but in the published output the well-mixed "fixed" state is never left (post hoc check below), so that count cannot fall: it is a definition, not a measurement of reversals. Most of the snapshot "undoing" is mutations that dipped below 95% and came back (34%; with no depth margin, part of this is threshold noise around 0.95) or stayed at high frequency (62%). Only 4.6% were actually lost, which is clade displacement, the quasi-stable coexistence Good 2017 describes: "Instead of fixing, the “missing” mutations segregate into at least two intermediate-frequency clades that coexist for long periods (Fig. 1)." (Good et al. 2017, Nature, main text, local `sources/raw/sources/txt/Good2017.txt` l.30, sha256 75a86550...).
- **Where Good-style calls can fall (review MINOR-4, moved from the caveats).** Good's clade-aware HMM has clade-level fixed states (FM, Fm: fixed within the major or minor clade; `parse_file.py` l.974). Those can be lost when a clade dies, and they are the closest model-based analogue to Day's "undone". They were not read here.
- So "fixations being undone faster than new ones complete" holds for "≥95% snapshot counts fall". For fixations in Good's well-mixed sense it does not apply, because those calls are never undone by construction; the disagreement is about which statistic the word "fixations" names. The audit's own prediction that a third of cases would be real losses was wrong: real losses are rare.

## Who this helps
- **Day.** His count is reproduced exactly from public data: 10 of 12, with the same per-cell numbers. High-frequency mutations do reverse in the LTEE, and in mutator populations net losses of 39-86 high-frequency mutations per interval are real data features. Clonal interference does undo near-sweeps, which is the empirical point behind his "stalls, reverses" language.
- **Critics.** The fixation counts of the source he cites never go down (in the published calls the fixed state is never left, so this is Good's definition at work, not a separate finding). The reversals are threshold effects and clade dynamics, not lost fixations, so "fixations being undone" relabels a snapshot statistic. The A2c/E6 counting-rule objection applies.
- **Both.** The dispute here is definitional and can be stated exactly: snapshot ≥95% versus Good's HMM. Both numbers are now on the record.

## Caveats for the reviewer
1. Snapshot samples are the population timepoints nearest each 10K mark (±500 generations), with depth ≥ 5. Day's exact timepoints are not stated.
2. Only PASS mutations are counted. Day's paper does not say whether he filtered.
3. The HMM "fixed" count is Good's well-mixed state (F). Clade-level states: see Interpretation.
4. "Dip" (back to ≥95% at a later snapshot) has no depth margin, so threshold noise around 0.95 counts as a dynamic.
