## Interpretation
- **Internal: holds on Day's rule.** The ≥95% snapshot count falls in 10 of 12 populations. Day's Q178 is a faithful description of his own statistic.
- **The word "fixations" is the issue (A2c/E6).** A ≥95% snapshot is not a fixation in Good 2017's sense. Under Good's hidden-Markov fixation calls, no population ever loses a fixed mutation. Most of the snapshot "undoing" is mutations that dipped just below 95% and came back (34%) or stayed at high frequency (62%). Only 4.6% were actually lost, which is clade displacement, the quasi-stable coexistence Good 2017 describes.
- So "fixations being undone faster than new ones complete" holds for "≥95% snapshot counts fall". For fixations as Good defines them it is contradicted. The audit's own prediction that a third of cases would be real losses was wrong: real losses are rare.

## Who this helps
- **Day.** His count is reproduced exactly from public data: 10 of 12, with the same per-cell numbers. High-frequency mutations do reverse in the LTEE, and in mutator populations net losses of 39-86 high-frequency mutations per interval are real data features. Clonal interference does undo near-sweeps, which is the empirical point behind his "stalls, reverses" language.
- **Critics.** The fixation counts of the source he cites never go down. The reversals are threshold effects and clade dynamics, not lost fixations, so "fixations being undone" relabels a snapshot statistic. The A2c/E6 counting-rule objection applies.
- **Both.** The dispute here is definitional and can be stated exactly: snapshot ≥95% versus Good's HMM. Both numbers are now on the record.

## Caveats for the reviewer
1. Snapshot samples are the population timepoints nearest each 10K mark (±500 generations), with depth ≥ 5. Day's exact timepoints are not stated.
2. Only PASS mutations are counted. Day's paper does not say whether he filtered.
3. The HMM "fixed" count is Good's well-mixed state (F). Clade-level states (FM/Fm), which can be lost when a clade dies, were not read.
