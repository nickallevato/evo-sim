# Review of R4-G1: steelman from the critics' side

Reviewer stance: the most capable, honest defender of the critics (McCarthy, Mansfield, Camestros, Hancock, Bowers, Myers, keruru, Nesslig20, and the Reddit/KITTENS authors). Scope: `research/checks/results/R4-G1.md`, `research/checks/g1_specific_vs_any.py`, claims G, Ga-Gd, G1/G1c, G2/G2d-g, G3/G3a/G3b, G4/G4b, and the local critic sources under `sources/raw/critics/` (read and grepped only, never executed).

Overall: the arithmetic parts (A, B) and the independence parts (C) look sound and are scrupulously labelled (pre-registered failures reported, post-hoc E2 flagged). The weak spots are (i) how the "middle case" is framed, (ii) two Day-favourable readings (additive dilution, 335x excess) that depend on model choices, and (iii) credit attribution: several critic arguments in the corpus are not credited. I found nothing that changes an arithmetic result. Numbers marked "reviewer scratch" below were recomputed quickly in a throwaway `python3 -I` session (not saved to the repo) and should be re-run through a proper script before being cited.

## Critic-side points the write-up should state more prominently

1. The p^n product prices a pre-specified list of arisings; Day's own Gd (s7.9) and the book's preceding step are "any n of M" calculations. Several critics said this in different words (McCarthy, Camestros, Bowers, Nesslig20, Mansfield), not only McCarthy.
2. In Day's own stated multiplicative model, per-locus response does not fall with n (to 0.6% at n = 157,000), and 814 concurrent active-zone sweeps fix at ~98% of single-locus probability with flat time. The "~230" is target x transit time / window to 0.01%; the paper's own criterion gives 13.8; 14.7x is really 2.34e6.
3. "p^n forces sequential fixation" does not follow because p^n is timing-independent. This gives Hancock/Myers a textual basis in Day's own abstract and Appendix A, which is a credit to them, not just to the audit.
4. Neutral differences need no function; the flip threshold applies only to the adaptive subset, and its size n_f is the single missing external input (Day's 200,000 is a scenario).
5. The additive-scale "barrier" is a parametrisation effect that applies to sequential accumulation as well; the 335x reproductive excess is a feature of the 157,000-concurrent-sweep configuration (about 1.3x at 230) and of global soft selection.
6. Clonal populations show an interference penalty that recombining populations mostly escape (part C): LTEE-derived G_f is plausibly a lower bound for recombining humans, which is the critics' real answer to G1.
7. Critics who are credited in the sources but not in the adjudication: Nesslig20 (any-winner lottery, sharpshooter), Bowers (explicit "no pre-specified targets"), the Reddit commenter (CV vs absolute variance; Fisher), KITTENS/guest (soft vs hard selection), Camestros (independence "without justification"; the G1c concession).

## Where the write-up is fair to Day and I would leave it alone

The specific-horn result (10^-8.4e6 even with recurrent mutation), the throughput-untested caveat (conditional on item 7 above), the reproductive-excess point as a real constraint at large concurrent n, the non-pre-registered E2 flag, and the version-tension item (Z18167588 s7.11 vs G3b) are all stated accurately and I have no objection beyond the framing changes above.
