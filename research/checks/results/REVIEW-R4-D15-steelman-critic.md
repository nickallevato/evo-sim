**Summary: 0 BLOCKER, 3 MAJOR, 5 MINOR, 2 NOTE.**
- The sweep is a careful test of the prediction *as Hössjer framed it*. Its biases all run in his favour, and the S-cell failures survive them.
- The critic's strongest case is not in the grid. It is about how the target is framed:
  - the sweep fixes in advance which genes must change (M1);
  - Hössjer's own paper contains sub-9-My regimes that the write-up does not mention (M2);
  - "neither side has measured" ignores a literature outside the corpus (M3).

## 2. Honest concessions: points against the critics that stand
- **For the specific target with non-beneficial intermediates, the prediction holds strongly.** All 96 in-scope cells (m ≥ 2, kmult ≤ 1, neutral or valley, both N_e, both rho) have p9 = 0 and p90 ≤ 0.017. Every engine bias runs toward Hössjer, so no correction rescues the critic case here.
- **Valley intermediates of 1e-3 to 1e-2 essentially never complete** at kmult ≤ 15. Recombination does not help: even full linkage equilibrium changes little.
- **The apparent N_e = 1e4 tunnelling advantage is not real** (correctness M1). At N_e = 1e4 the no-tunnelling chain is accurate for the Fin cells.
- **The fixation sojourn (≈ 4N_e generations) lengthens neutral routes at large N_e** (correctness M2). Large N_e is not uniformly critic-friendly.
- **No critic in the corpus has engaged this prediction or supplied a per-step s or a redundancy value.** The critic-favourable cells come from the auditor's grid, not from a published rebuttal.

## 3. View on the proposed verdict
- **Internal:** `holds` only with the d_max = 0 qualification and an explicit answer to N2d (M2), or `n/a` (m3).
- **External:** `contested` is right, and if anything generous to Hössjer. The gene-set specificity question (M1) and the unread turnover literature (M3) are open, and both point toward the prediction failing for any non-specific reading.
- **Fidelity:** `partial` (M2), unless the Day-side "already present ≠ new" reading is adopted explicitly.
