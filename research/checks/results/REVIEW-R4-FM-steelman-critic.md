## Verdict in brief
Every registered discriminating cell went the critics' way. The pattern is shared across four lineages. It follows a shared rate (Spearman H~C 0.84, H~G 0.66). It is not human-specific in single-copy sequence (φ_HC 1.8; 0/0 specific mesas). It holds 1-2.5% of human-chimp differences, not most of them. The write-up is, if anything, generous to Day in three places. It gives FM3's "80x" an internal `holds` although the number depends entirely on an unreleased window size. It calls the F0 human excess "real" without testing the reference projection. And it keeps a residual (φ_res 2.3-2.7) as a possible Day-side opening, when a better-specified null would shrink it. None of this changes a registered score.

## The strongest honest critic-side case
1. **The "80x" is a function of the window, not a finding.** φ − 1 ≈ m p CV², so φ grows roughly linearly with W: H at F0 goes 3.3, 10.4, 36.9 and 136 at 1 kb, 10 kb, 100 kb and 1 Mb. Any "X times" can be reached by choosing W, for any lineage (G reaches 93). Against a uniform rate, the figure carries no information about mechanism. Critic steelman point 1 is confirmed.
2. **Shared pattern, so k = μ.** About half of the human-mesa windows lie in chimp mesas (32-82x enrichment). H density tracks C at 0.84 and G at 0.66. A null that borrows only the other lineages' rates removes 81% of the human φ (14.3 → 2.7, F2 100 kb) and reproduces 69% of human mesas. Neutral theory predicts exactly this shape: a high-μ region shows excess on every branch. Critic steelman point 3 is confirmed.
3. **The human lineage is not special even in the residual.** After the null, G's residual is larger than H's in every F1-F3 cell, though not in F0 (F2 100 kb: G 4.36, H 2.68, C 2.41; F1 100 kb: G 8.15, H 4.48). Chimp-specific mesas outnumber human-specific ones wherever either exists (70 vs 35, 8 vs 1).
4. **Haldane and MITTENS bound selected substitutions.** At 100 kb, every mesa of every lineage exceeds 191, and most exceed 840. A bound that every chimp and gorilla region also "exceeds" tests nothing about humans. Critic steelman point 4 is confirmed.
5. **"Dominant source" fails by an order of magnitude.** Human mesas hold 1.25% of DHC at the registered cell. That is below even the audit's own 2-15% expectation.

## Fairness judgement
On autosomal single-copy sequence the critics' case is as strong as a replication can make it, and the write-up states it plainly. The overreach runs mildly toward Day, in the FM3 `holds` and the "real" F0 signal. That is the right direction to err for a neutral audit, but it should be corrected to what the data show. My verdicts: FM3 internal `holds` (weak) / fidelity `unverifiable` / external `supported`, non-informative; FM4 `supported`, shared; FM5 literally true, external `supported` but non-discriminating (I accept the Day-side review's point that rule N belongs on FM10, not FM5); FM8b `contradicted`; FM10 external `contradicted` for the mesa route, with internal N2b or N2d pending the corpus grep. The F0 duplicated-sequence and chrX cells stay open until the mirror test.
