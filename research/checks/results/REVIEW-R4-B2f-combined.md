## 2. Steelman: Day side

- **D1 (MAJOR). The write-up's own identity shows Day's described route is real, and it does not say so.** The modular form in "The exact law" is F(x) = (4π/x)^1.5 e^(x/16) Σₙ (−1)ⁿ(2n+1) exp(−π²(2n+1)²/x). Its leading (n = 0) term carries exactly exp(−π²Nₑ/T). So the Gegenbauer eigen-expansion, resummed, does yield Day's exponent as its dominant short-time term: "converting Chalub's abstract series into" this law is a correct description of a derivation that exists, to the extent Chalub's expansion is the Kimura (1955) one (B1e to confirm). And the arcsine coordinate is HL's own transform: "the transform that turns neutral drift into a symmetric random walk" with distance "π√(2Nₑ)" (HL l.297-298, 2026-08-27) is the angular (arcsine) transform, whose range is π. The write-up's item 3 reports only that HL lacks the strings "arcsine" and "eigenvalue", and its Limits say "Whether Chalub's series yields this exponent by any route was not examined", which its own derivation answers in Day's favour. What remains wrong is one word: "eigenvalue" (the dominant eigenvalue gives 1 − 3e^(−T/2Nₑ), the long-time law; the short-time law is the dominant term of the dual series). Fix: credit the route in "What is new" item 3 and in "Who this helps"; narrow the ledger slip to "dominant eigenvalue" for "dominant term of the resummed series"; correct the Limits line.
- **D2 (MINOR). No row shows the formula where it belongs.** All three paper-scale comparisons sit at T/4Nₑ = 6.3, 1.26, 1.0, outside HL's "T ≪ 4Nₑ".
