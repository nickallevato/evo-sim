# Review of R4-B3 (steelman, Day's side)

Reviewer role: argue as Vox Day's most capable and honest defender would. The question asked: does B3 score "k = 32.3μ" and "median factor of 25 across 55 vertebrates" on Day's own basis, at their strongest, and are the Day-favourable results reported with the same prominence as the critic-favourable ones?

Reviewed: `R4-B3.md`; `b3_family_reconstruction.py` (docstring, P2-P4); `raw/b3_family.out`; claims B3, B3c, B3d, A3a, A3x, A3x1, B4a; Q99-Q103 (quotes-day, grep); Day blog 2026-10-01 ¶7 (grep); `R4-X1-verdict-rule.md` §0, C, R1, N, F; Bergeron2023.txt (grep only); `parameters.yaml`. Only trivial arithmetic was run locally.

Overall: the arithmetic is honest, and the write-up already gives Day a "Who this helps" bullet. The weakness is the verdict. It re-scores Day on the critics' unit, and the write-up calls Day's "55 vertebrates" sourceless although Day names Bergeron 2023 for it in the same post.

**What Day gets right here (should lead the section):**
1. 32.3 is Day's own arithmetic on Day's own inputs. It is not a slip.
2. "Not k = μ" holds at his own SNV floor before ancestral polymorphism (2.0-2.8).
3. At his mammalian mu, and with the audit's mandated N_anc, a residual of 1.3-1.65 remains, consistent with Keightley's published "about twofold".
4. His 55-vertebrate figure has a cited, peer-reviewed source (Bergeron 2023, named in the same post), and the audit has not yet recomputed it.
