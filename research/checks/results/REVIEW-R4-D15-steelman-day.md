Reviewer: Opus 5.5. Date: 2026-10-10. Role: argue as Hössjer's (and Day's) most capable and honest defender would. Question asked: does D15 test Hössjer's prediction as he stated it, and does the write-up frame the result fairly to him?

Severity labels as in the correctness review (BLOCKER / MAJOR / MINOR / NOTE).

**Summary: 0 BLOCKER, 2 MAJOR, 6 MINOR, 3 NOTE.**
- The check is fair in design. It runs Hössjer's own model, reproduces his tables to 0.3%, and uses his valley values. Every bias it knowingly carries (the f = 3 scaling, the 25 y window, the all-absent start at kmult = 1) runs in his favour.
- The weaknesses are in the framing, in two places:
  - the scorecard counts cells his statement excludes (M1);
  - "external contested" is reached with selection and redundancy values that no source supplies (M2).

## 2. Honest concessions: points against Hössjer that stand
- **The prediction is conditional, and the condition is a premise he asserts without numbers.** With +1% intermediates (S2), 9 My is reached in 57-77% of replicates at the specific site (N_e = 1e4) and 100% at N_e = 1e5, for every m up to 4. That holds even though the f = 3 scaling biases those cells long. If intermediates are beneficial, the prediction fails; his "would most likely have low fitness" is the whole case, and it is not shown.
- **Tunnelling is real at N_e = 1e5**: Fin2 m = 2 is about 5x faster than the chain. Hössjer's fixed-state (no-tunnelling) tables overstate times when only the finished set is selected and N_e is large.
- **His own 2021 Tables 5 and 9 contain neutral regimes below 9 My** (W = 6, d_max = 1: 4.5e4 generations). The defence is that such a site is present by chance (E0 ≈ 4.6) and is not a new feature (m3). But the review does not state which W and d_max the prediction assumes.
- **He did not compute the prediction.** The review says the model "has not yet been applied to humans and chimps". The sweep supplies the computation he did not make.

## 3. What the questions asked come to
- **Does D15 test the claim as stated?** Yes for the in-scope cells, and generously to Hössjer in every disclosed bias.
- **Is the framing fair?** Not yet. The headline counts out-of-scope cells (M1), and "contested" is supported only by unsourced grid values (M2).
- **On the proposed verdicts:** internal `holds` is right. External should be `supported (conditional)`, or `contested` with the explicit note that no opposing source exists. Either is defensible, provided the conditionality is in the verdict comment.
