## 2. Steelman: Day side

- **D1 (MAJOR). The h = 0.1, R = 1.111 hit and miss both rest on the registered 50% tie, and survival is still falling at the horizon.** The pass counts at 20k are 7, 5, 4, 4, 2 of 8. lam50 = 0.013 is the last lam with share >= 50%, a 4-of-8 tie. Under a strict "> 50%" convention the boundary is 0.0094 (5/8) = 2.55x the 10k cap, below 3x. That would score the Day-side prediction (< 0.0110) a hit and CA2 at R = 1.111 a miss. This is the same marginal-CA2 position the fix pass recorded ("limit about 3x, marginal"). The new "holds" is a tie-convention flip. The run history shows extinction is a time-to-event: alive at 10k / 20k / 40k is 8 / 6 / 5 at 0.0111 and 7 / 6 / 4 at 0.013, and runs at 0.013 were still dying between 20k and 40k (reps 4 and 7). An infinite-horizon boundary is lower than the 40k one. The scored result is correct by the registered rule, and the section does say "4 of 8", but the closure row, the critic bullet ("about 3.5x at both R (CA2 restated holds)") and the verdict text carry "about 3.5x" with no range. Fix: at R = 1.111 write "about 2.6 to 3.5x (3.5x by the registered >= 50% convention; 2.55x if ties are excluded; survival still falling at 40k); CA2 marginal at R = 1.111, holds at R = 2 (7/8 and 0/8 either side, sharp)". Keep 3.3 to 4.3x only as the bracket.
- **D2 (MAJOR). The proposed H text drops the Day-1 clause that the fix pass made mandatory.** Day-1 (R4-HF.md, Review resolution): "with purely relative fitness (soft selection) the population cannot go extinct in this model, and the rate is limited only by sweep latency and the locus cap." The new H sentence says "there is no cap in-model". `ph` itself shows the locus cap is not negligible: the mean open count is 3,600 to 11,200 in C and 11,754 in N (g = 0.1, lam 1.6) in a K = 1000 population, with tau up to 9,300 generations. Fix: "... no cap on the number of substitutions per generation in-model, the rate being limited by sweep latency (tau 4,300 to 9,300 generations at s = 0.01) and the open-locus count (1,000 to 11,000 concurrent sweeps at lam 0.8 to 1.6)". The H8 sentence already carries the N = K condition; add the same latency clause there.
- **D3 (MINOR). Restated DA2 is scored on a stricter test than it states, while DA2 and PA4 get "by the letter".** The pre-registered discriminator for "not explained by the fill lag" is "k/lam falling with lam". It is met at K = 4000 (0.894 to 0.871); it is also met at K = 1000 (0.931 to 0.916), so it does not discriminate. Also, tau itself is 25% longer at K = 4000 (stage L, verified), so a fill lag that scales with K is a K effect in Day's latency sense (C4), not only an artefact. The section says the second half is "not settled", which is right, but the first half (3/4 against 0/3 at the same lam) is a Day-side result that is not credited in "Who this helps" as a hit. Fix: score restated DA2 "first half met; discriminator met by the letter but non-discriminating (also met at K = 1000)", consistent with how DA2 and PA4 are treated, and state that the K-dependence of tau is itself the Day-side latency finding.
- **D4 (MINOR). Term 3 at h = 0.3 is not reported, and it favours Day.** The section compares only to the H8 hard cap. Against Term 3 = ln R / (2 ln 2000) (0.00693 at R = 1.111, 0.0456 at R = 2): h = 0.3 gives 0.00575 / 0.00693 = 0.83x and 0.0479 / 0.0456 = 1.05x, so Term 3 holds as a rate bound at 30% hard selection. At h = 0.1 it is exceeded 1.9x and 2.8x. That makes Term 3 a fair bound at h = 0.3, and it should be in the H8 sentence ("supported for h = 0.3" would then have a number behind it).
- **D5 (NOTE). The 10k-cap choice is already the Day-favourable one.** It gives smaller multiples (3.53 against 3.71 and 4.27 at h = 0.1; 1.32 against 1.60 at h = 0.3, R = 2). It was pre-registered, so no complaint from Day; see K1.

## 3. Steelman: critic side

- **K1 (MINOR). - **K2 (MINOR). "Undecided" for the cap-release cells can be tightened without new runs.** `hf_ph.jsonl` holds a trace (t, nopen, cumulative fixations, N) every 250 generations. A fit of nopen(t) toward lam x tau_L (tau_L from stage L, same series) would show whether the C cells are converging, for R = 1.111, K = 1000 and K = 4000. "Open after ph" item 1 says only a long-burn run can decide; a trace fit is a cheaper first test (the fit is post hoc and should be labelled so). Also: no backlog in 26 of 26 runs, realised rate >= 0.62 per generation (>= 185x 1/300), and L shows the same series reach k/lam >= 0.97 at lam <= 0.4. "Undecided" is defensible and symmetric with Day's half.
- **K3 (MINOR). "A modest hard component restores a limit near Haldane's" is true only at R = 1.111.** The H8 hard cap at R = 2 is 0.0363, about 11x 1/300; at h = 0.3, R = 2 the boundary 0.0479 is about 14x 1/300. At R = 1.111 it is 0.00575, about 1.7x. Fix: "restores a limit within 1.3-1.7x of the H8 hard cap (near 1/300 at R = 1.111, about 14x it at R = 2)". The Limits paragraph's remark that the mix has no density compensation (h is the share of load expressed as density-independent survival at fixed R) is the right caveat, and it makes the model pessimistic for sustainability; the critic can say the h = 0.3 limit is a floor. This should be attached to the Day bullet, not only the "Both" bullet.
- **K4 (NOTE). Stage N is not understated.** Both R reach the grid top at all g, so "lam_max >= 231x / 35x Term 3" is the right lower bound. At g = 0.1, R = 1.111, lam 1.6, nopen is 11,754 (largest single run 11,823) against max_open 20,000, so g < 0.1 would probably backlog. The H8 text correctly says "down to 0.1". - **K5 (NOTE). The 20k rule is noisy at small counts, which biases lam50(20k) low (the Day-favourable direction).** h = 0.3, R = 2, lam 0.0454: 8 of 8 alive at every horizon but 5 of 8 on the 20k rule against 8 of 8 on the 40k rule; h = 0.3, R = 1.111, lam 0.0037 (nopen q1 about 10): 5/8 at 20k against 7/8 at 40k.
