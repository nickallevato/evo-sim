# Review of R4-L1c (steelman, critics' side)

Reviewer role: argue as the most capable and honest critic of Day's dN/dS claims would (a population geneticist who holds that human-chimp dN/dS is about 0.2-0.25, that the published 0.23 needs no protein-guided step, and that 0.80 is an extraction artefact). Questions asked: is `contested` the right word for what the run found, and which reading may set the DN6 field under rule RD? Is the fidelity result, which needs no simulation, placed and scored where it belongs? Do the data bound the dense-run regime? How strong is the register-loss account of 0.80, and what does the rule license without Day's pipeline? Is Day credited for results that are not his, not blind, or true by construction?

Reviewed (2026-10-13, head 02b87c6): `R4-L1c.md` at a5f92a8 (whole); docstring of `l1c_alignment_method.py` (registration 536b19b, amendment 0e0e840: arms, PREDICTIONS, WHAT MOVES WHICH VERDICT, the amendment block); `REVIEW-R4-L1c-prerun.md` (findings, resolution table); `REVIEW-R4-L1c-correctness.md` (3429895; findings not repeated, rulings taken as given except where a separate question is raised and marked); `raw/l1c.out`, `raw/l1c.json`, `raw/l1c.genes.tsv.gz`, the transfer table as printed in the write-up; `R4-L1b.md` (X1 section, the two post hoc sections); Q180-Q186; the CSAC2005 block of `quotes-literature.md`; claim file DN6; R4-X1 rule RD. This file was written after the Day-side steelman by the same reviewer; that file was not changed and is not answered point by point. Where the two disagree the fix pass has both. No literature is cited from memory.

**Reviewer computations.** All numbers marked "reviewer" are post hoc, from `raw/l1c.genes.tsv.gz` (one process, seconds, no rerun). "Indel-free" is the correctness review's rule (16,545 genes, reproduced). "Affected" is L1b's rule: UT differs from U0 in Sd or Nd (2,637 genes, reproduced). "Left out" is CDS length / 3 less the terminal stop, minus the codons U0 counts. None is registered. None moves a band.

Overall. The write-up is fair to the critics on the numbers and timid on what they mean. Three things are settled by this run and by the held source, and each is stated in a hedge or in a list:
- the published 0.23 was not made by a protein-guided alignment (a matter of what the source says);
- the low value is there with no protein step, on 12,724 genes of the real 2005 list, together with the nonsynonymous share Day's own table gives for the published row;
- whatever the realistic arms remove, signal or error, its total size is 2% to 6% on all genes and 1% to 4% on the real list, against the 71% the claim needs.

The run also contains the strongest evidence so far for where 0.80 comes from, and it is filed under "descriptive".

## Findings

### 1. MAJOR. `contested` for the DN6 field comes from a reading wider than the quoted statement; under RD an extended reading cannot set the field
- RD, last bullet: "Extended readings (a different object, or a wider claim) are reported with their own verdicts but cannot set the verdict field or the headline tag." And the field takes "the reading under which the speaker's *quoted statement* is most defensible ... It is **not** the reading that yields the best verdict for the speaker."
- The quoted statement has an object. Q186: "The difference is not a matter of gene selection, quality filtering, or computational error. It is a methodological artifact of using a conservation-dependent alignment to measure conservation." "The difference" is 0.7989 against "Published (Consortium 2005)13,4540.2300~40%" (Q184). The post is about one species pair and one gap. Reading b as filed, "Protein-guided alignment pipelines in general (and later ones in particular) ... bias dN/dS downward", with the amended gloss "any size", is a wider claim about other pipelines and other divergences. Day did not write it; the audit wrote it for him, and then the amendment gave it a rule with two outcomes only (`supported`, `contested`). Pre-run MAJOR-9 objected that Reading A "comes out `supported` whatever real pipelines do". After the fix it cannot come out `contradicted` whatever the data do. A reading that cannot lose is not a test from either side.
- The reading that is Day's and that the run does test: "at human-chimp divergence, protein-guided alignment lowers dN/dS by a material amount". Results on it: realistic arms lower ALL by 2.3% to 5.8% and the real list by 1.3% to 3.7% (reviewer, from the main table: PA 1.3%, PL38 1.7%, PAG 1.8%, PGB 2.4%, PAF1 3.7%); all five carry flag `none`; R1's materiality line is 25%; the claim needs 71% of nonsynonymous differences removed and the arms remove 2.5% to 6.4%, a shortfall of 11 to 28 times. In indel-free genes the effect is 0.2% to 0.7% (correctness finding 2). The registered move sentence for this case reads "CRITIC with flag none -> contradicted", with the registered gloss "no bias at human-chimp divergence".
- The correctness review ruled that the docstring's text licenses `contested` because the flag in the move sentence includes PF2. That ruling is on the docstring and is not reopened. The question here is a different one and belongs to the claim file and the owner: which reading sets the field. `contested` is reached only through an arm the amendment says "decide[s] nothing" and that the registration called adversarial ("it removes nonsynonymous changes because they are nonsynonymous"). A verdict that exists only because a filter built to delete amino-acid differences deletes them credits Day with a tautology.
- Fix: split Reading b in the DN6 Readings table. b1, human-chimp, material size (the object of the post): external `contradicted` on these data, with the numbers and the three limits (one alignment, one annotation, in-script aligner). b2, any pipeline, any divergence, any size: `contested`, marked extended, cannot set the field. Put the choice between "field takes b1" and "field takes b2" to the owner as an RD question, with this finding and the correctness ruling side by side. If the field stays `contested`, its comment must open with the size (2% to 6%; 71% needed) and not with "direction real".

### 2. MAJOR. The fidelity result stands without any simulation, and the write-up leaves the field `pending` and the fact in a subordinate clause
- Held, machine-checked text (`quotes-literature.md`, CSAC2005): the transcripts "were indirectly aligned to the chimpanzee sequence via the genome alignment"; "A subset of 7,043 of these genes with unambiguous mouse and rat orthologues were realigned using Clustal W158 for the lineage-specific analyses."; the 0.23 is "the value of 0.23 obtained with all human–chimpanzee orthologues". Q180 names "the protein-guided CDS alignment method" as the thing with the problem, and Q186 gives it as the cause of "the difference" from the published row. The source's method for that row is a nucleotide genome alignment. That is fidelity `misread`, and it does not depend on the in-script aligner, the trimming rules, the kappa convention or Cactus. Every objection that can be raised against the simulation leaves it untouched.
- Where the write-up puts it: one printed line quoted after the READINGS block; one sentence inside Reading B ("Independently of the run ..."); the last of six Critics bullets; and in the verdict table: fidelity "no value licensed by the run; the numbers go in the comment". The claim file already records `misread` for Reading a and still shows the field `pending`. Nothing is pending: the docstring fixed it before the run ("under the reading '0.23 was produced by protein-guided alignment' fidelity is `misread` regardless of this run").
- The run adds a second, independent leg that the write-up does not join to it. On the 12,724 mapped genes of the real list, with no protein step, U0 reads 0.219 with a nonsynonymous fraction of 0.426. Day's own table gives the published row as 0.2300 and "~40%". Both numbers of the row he calls an artefact of protein guidance are reproduced by a nucleotide alignment on 94.6% of the same genes. At CSAC's site ratio the value is 0.265; the registered range was 0.18 to 0.30.
- Which field: fidelity (what the cited source did), not external. Reading a's external `contradicted` and the fidelity `misread` are two verdicts on two questions and both should show.
- Fix: propose fidelity `misread` now, as a verdict that needs no run, with the three quotes; state it in the status line and as Interpretation 1, ahead of the simulation results; add the sentence that U0 reproduces both figures of Day's "Published" row; in the verdict table replace "no value licensed by the run" with "`misread`, licensed by the held text before the run (docstring, pre-run note)".

### 3. MAJOR. The size bound makes the "signal or misalignment" question moot, and the regime hedge is unjustified for the genes that matter to the published number
- The correctness review showed that x does not separate saturated runs from misalignment (its finding 1) and that the indel-free genes bound the regime (its finding 2). Neither is repeated. What follows from them, and is missing: **grant Day everything about what the dropped codons are.** Suppose every codon the realistic arms remove is real, fast-evolving, correctly aligned protein. The bias is then exactly the measured drop: at most 5.8% on all genes, 3.7% on the real list, 0.7% in the 16,545 indel-free genes, 0.6% in the 11,397 indel-free genes of the real list (reviewer: U0 0.1817; PA 0.1816, PGB 0.1812, PAF1 0.1807). The claim under test needs a factor of 3.5. The x discriminator, the frameshift-adjacency share and the nonsynonymous-fraction signature all address a question whose answer cannot change the result.
- The dense-run regime is excluded where the published number lives. The synthetic cell that gives Day his transfer hit has PAF1 dropping 0.80% of codons; real indel-free genes: 0.005% (407 codons in 95 genes; correctness review), 160 times less. The real list is 90% indel-free (11,397 of 12,724 genes; reviewer). In the cell that produces the hit, a fifth of genes carry a 15-residue stretch replaced at omega 64 at ordinary synonymous divergence; in real indel-free genes PA differs from U0 in 3 genes of 16,545.
- Half of what PAF1 removes sits in 50 genes (53.7%; correctness review). Reviewer: without those 50 genes U0 reads 0.2565, PAF1 0.2473 (-3.6%), PGB 0.2494 (-2.8%). The first twelve are WDR87, MUC17, FLG, MUC3A, AC236972.4, SAMD9L, TCHH, AC242842.3, NBPF19, IGFN1, AHNAK2, ZNF227, with chimp coverage as low as 0.22 and identity of 0.72 to 0.96. The "mechanism" on real data is a handful of low-coverage, low-identity genes, not a genome-wide property.
- The codons no arm sees do not reopen it (reviewer). They are 1.40% of annotated sense codons (the write-up's 1.6% includes about 19,400 terminal stops). To lift the pooled value to 0.80 they need a pN of 0.67; to 0.65, 0.48. Unrelated sequence gives 0.73 (arm NU). So the route requires the left-out sequence to be all but non-homologous, at which point it is not a measurement of selection on orthologues. The 13,307 genes with no codon left out read 0.209; Q183 ("The signal is uniform across the genome, it's not concentrated in problematic genes.") predicts about 0.80 there. And the 351 genes with 20% or more left out are not a sample of fast-evolving single-copy proteins: 73% carry a Cactus frameshift run, 49% are dS-noisy by the audit's rule, 46% are one2one, 29% are on the 2005 list.
- Fix: replace "nothing in it fixes the regime on its own" as the correctness review asks, and add the bound above as the headline of the section: "if all of it is signal, the bias is 2% to 6% (1% to 4% on the real list)". State the left-out-codon bound in Results with 0.67 and 0.48, corrected for the stops. Reading A's basis item 3 ("this run tests one aligner, five rules and one species pair") should say what the one pair showed: an upper bound, not an absence of evidence.

### 4. MAJOR. The register-loss account of 0.80 is undersold: it reproduces five features of Day's table at once, on real and on synthetic data, and the write-up files it as a resemblance
- What the write-up says: one bullet marked "Post hoc, descriptive", ending "This is a resemblance, not a location of Day's method"; Interpretation 4; caveat 13. What the data say, taken together:
  1. **Level.** UT reads 0.853 on all genes with no noise added (Day: 0.7989).
  2. **Uniformity across gene sets.** UT 0.800 to 0.883 over ALL, O121, CSACp, CSAC23, NOT23, HQ, HQs, DSC (Day's rows: 0.7939 to 0.8237). U0 over the same sets: 0.193 to 0.344.
  3. **The share.** UT 0.730 to 0.748 (Day: 73.8% to 74.4%); U0 0.395 to 0.537.
  4. **The order of the rows.** Genes outside one2one read higher than those inside under UT: 0.898 against 0.844 (reviewer; 2,486 and 16,824 genes), as Day's row C (0.8237) is above row B (0.7956).
  5. **"High without protein guidance, low with it."** The protein family applied to UT's bases takes 0.853 to 0.535, 0.340 and 0.190 (write-up table). Day's central observation, that his number collapses when a protein-guided method is used, is what a register-broken input does. Protein alignment restores the register; it does not hide divergence.
- Why UT is uniform, which the write-up does not show (reviewer; affected = UT differs from U0):

| set | genes | affected | UT, affected genes (share) | U0 on the same genes | UT, unaffected genes (share) | affected genes' part of UT's Nd |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ALL | 19,310 | 2,637 (13.7%) | 0.960 (0.764) | 0.427 | 0.205 (0.411) | 96.8% |
| O121 | 16,824 | 1,983 (11.8%) | 0.966 (0.764) | 0.364 | 0.196 (0.399) | 96.6% |
| CSAC23 | 12,724 | 1,265 (9.9%) | 0.960 (0.765) | 0.351 | 0.182 (0.382) | 96.3% |
| NOT23 | 6,586 | 1,372 (20.8%) | 0.960 (0.762) | 0.485 | 0.249 (0.456) | 97.2% |
| HQ | 17,504 | 1,256 (7.2%) | 1.005 (0.769) | 0.241 | 0.188 (0.389) | 94.8% |
| DSC | 18,433 | 2,118 (11.5%) | 0.971 (0.765) | 0.314 | 0.196 (0.399) | 96.6% |

  In every set, 7% to 21% of genes read 0.96 to 1.01 with a share of 0.76 to 0.77, which is unrelated in-frame pairing (NU: 1.018, 0.774), and those genes supply 95% to 97% of all nonsynonymous differences counted. The pool is therefore a constant, the random-pairing value, whatever the gene set. Uniformity is not evidence that the signal is genome-wide; it is the mark of a pool dominated by one source. Under UT the per-gene median is 0.189, and 80.5% of genes with a defined ratio read below 0.65 (reviewer). Q183's "it's not concentrated in problematic genes" is false of the one extraction on these data that reproduces his table.
- The cause is ordinary and is not a reading-frame fault: L1b's breakdown gives in-frame indels 55.7% of the excess, and genes whose only events are in-frame indels read 1.009 under the naive pairing and 0.281 by column. The transfer table repeats it with no real data: UT reads 0.811 to 0.828 at a truth of 0.211 to 0.218 when 15% of genes have a correctly gapped indel, at all three clustering levels. So the effect is reproduced where the truth is known.
- Under transition-weighted sites Day's value is not a weak-constraint number at all. The script's conversion: his 73.9% is dN/dS 1.02 at CSAC's site ratio; on the kappa-5 line the naive arm reads 1.22. A genome-wide ratio of 1 or more over 20,400 genes means no constraint on protein sequence anywhere. The write-up notes that the naive arm "rises too" and draws no conclusion.
- What the rule licenses without his pipeline. Not "his error is register loss": his code is unseen (rules F and U; L1b's "candidate error class", "LOCATED is not licensed"). What it does license, and the write-up should say in its Interpretation: (i) on his stated assemblies, the only extraction found that returns his level, his uniformity, his share and his row order is one that pairs codons out of register after the first length difference; (ii) the same extraction on synthetic genes of known omega 0.22 returns 0.82; (iii) the frame-preserving extraction of the same bases returns 0.19 to 0.34 on the same gene sets; (iv) his "protein-guided gives 0.23" observation is predicted by (i) and not by weak constraint. That is a candidate with a real-data instance, a synthetic instance and five matched features. It is still a candidate. It is also the best-supported account on the table, and Q180-Q186 offer no competing observable.
- Fix: a Results subsection "The naive arm against Day's table" with the five features and the table above, labelled post hoc; Interpretation 4 rewritten to list (i) to (iv) and to keep "candidate"; the decisive outside input named for ROADMAP (his per-gene table or median: a register-safe pipeline at a true 0.80 has a per-gene median near 0.8, a register-broken one near 0.19), to be requested only with the owner's approval.

### 5. MINOR. Day is credited for a transfer "hit" that the audit wrote for him knowing the answer, and for a strict filter no pipeline is shown to use
- Not blind: the correctness review (finding 7) has the row relabelled. Beyond that: the "Day side" amended predictions were written by the audit on his behalf, and this one was written after the miniature had shown PAF1 0.25 and PGB 0.29. A statement known to be true when written is not a prediction and should not sit in a hit count beside blind ones. The one blind transfer prediction was the critics' and is scored as a miss on 1 arm-cell of 40.
- What the hit takes: 80% of replacements in 1% of codons, omega 64 inside the run. At the next level down (50% in 2% of codons, omega 20 inside runs) no realistic arm reaches 0.40, and four of five are within 1% of truth. "The mechanism exists in principle" should read "exists under near-total replacement of 15-residue stretches".
- "A strict protein-score filter is not harmless ... Any pipeline with a filter of that kind would understate dN/dS by a material amount" is listed as a point for Day. The printed rules table shows what the filter is: it deletes an isolated replacement, with its neighbours, when the replacement's BLOSUM62 score is 0 or less beside common residues. It censors the quantity being measured. That such a rule lowers dN is arithmetic, and no pipeline is cited that applies it. The docstring called it "ADVERSARIAL" at registration.
- Fix: move the transfer row out of the Day hit count into a line "confirmed, not blind"; in "Who this helps", Day bullets 2 and 3, add "no pipeline shown to use it" and the omega-64 description; keep the facts.

- The same section says "'No bias' is not shown". No critic-side prediction required a zero effect; the registered wording was "within ±0.03" and "< 0.03", and both are met.
- Fix: move the bullet under an "audit" heading and reword: "the audit's registered protein arm was invalid; the four critic-side predictions that name it hold on the valid arm". Drop or attribute "No bias".

### 7. MINOR. The amendments did not cost the critics a verdict, and the write-up should say which way each one cut
- Checked for the question "does the amendment disadvantage the critics relative to the registration": as registered, Reading A scores `supported` (by substitution of PAF2) and Reading B `contradicted`; as amended, `contested` and `contradicted`.
