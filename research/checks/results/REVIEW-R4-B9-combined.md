## 2. Steelman-Day (strongest case the check is unfair to Day)
- **MINOR-6.** The check is fair on arithmetic but unfair in two places. (i) It credits Day only with reading A while his words most naturally describe a *mutation-load* argument ("rendered infertile"), which is exactly Lynch's concept; he did not write "fixed", he wrote "producing actual changes to the genome" and "infertile". The infertility consequence only makes sense as load, not as fixation (a fixed neutral change causes no infertility). So reading A is arguably *the* literal reading of the consequent, and reading B only the reading of the "one million years" sentence from earlier in the thread. Day can say the check applied N2a across two different arguments in one comment. (ii) The "~one million years" figure is 4Ne generations conditional fixation time, which Day cites as "since 1969" correctly; a critic's parallel-fixation reply (k = mu) answers a different question, so using it against him in the internal column would be an N1 matter anyway.
- The strongest quantitative point for Day: his own premise 2 gives 12-970 y, all within 10-999 y with no stretching, so "within centuries" is not an exaggeration of his stated premises; Lynch independently gives 340 y at his upper value. The statement under Day's premises is true, and the 3x figure (uncited) is only an F issue, not an internal one.
- Where the check under-credits him: the "premise 1 false" external judgement leans on the claim that selection and drift act concurrently, but Day's premise is about *maximum speed* (k = mu requires full relaxation at that class); Ne reduction by background selection is real (Hudson-Kaplan), so premise 1 is weaker than "false", closer to "overstated". That supports `contested`-flavoured wording in MAJOR-2.
- Rule R1c/RH reading: Day wrote a one-line comment in a thread; the owner rule says charity, and the check applied charity. The only objection is the placing of the burden: the critic side's best reply (the antecedent never obtains) answers premise 1, which this check records but does not test numerically.

## 3. Steelman-critic (strongest case the check is too generous to Day)
- **MINOR-7 (what "remotely close" means).** The central estimate is millennia: Lynch's central 1% gives 1,724 y to half fitness and 5,728 y to a tenth; the mouse extrapolation 3,457 / 11,484. "Centuries" is 100-999. 17 centuries is a factor 1.7 beyond the upper bound for the *most generous* threshold (W = 0.5), and 6-57 for the others. A criterion of "within 10x" allows 9,990 y (100 centuries, a hundred-fold on "centuries" in the sense of 1-10 centuries) to count as "centuries". Only one anchor (Lynch's own "less conservative", 5%) and Day's unsourced premise 2 land in range. The mouse-based value, which Lynch himself flags ("likely imposing some selection against further decline"), is the one with the data; the 5% value is a "less conservative calculation" that Lynch cites to another paper.
- **MINOR-8 (the tag changes the internal column more than the number does).** The RH tag is a classification of a number ("remotely close"). Having tagged dialectic, the mapping then upgrades internal from non-sequitur to `holds`, a statement that the conclusion *follows from the premises*. But the number being "close" under reading A does not show the argument follows: reading A discards the premises' "substitution/fixation" content (MAJOR-1), the premise 2 f = 0.75 conflicts with Lynch's 1-10% (so Lynch's own numbers give millennia, not centuries), and the quoted conclusion has no dependence on drift. A dialectic tag should make the statement scoreable, not make it correct. The critic's cleanest reply: the step from "3x more harmful mutations" to "extinct within centuries" requires s >= 0.1% for 75% of all new mutations, contrary to every DFE estimate that puts most new mutations at |s| << 0.1%; the check's s grid is its own input.
- **MINOR-9 (reproductive excess).** Absolute fertility, not relative W, decides extinction. Humans have large reproductive reserve in historical populations; W = 0.1 relative to an ancestor is a threshold chosen by the author, and a population can lose most relative fitness without being "infertile". Stated as a caveat, but it is the lever on the headline; the check should report W = 0.01 and, if possible, a demographic floor.
- **NOTE-3.** The owner's rule was applied correctly; the point is that the *operationalisation* (10x band, min..max, centuries = 100-999) was chosen after seeing 338-11,484 y. A rule with a window narrowed to 100-3,000 y (the 3x band) gives 338-3,457 y overlap only from the 5% and mouse anchors, still dialectic by overlap; so the tag is robust to a tighter band, a point in the check's favour not stated in the write-up. Add it: this reduces MAJOR-3 to a statement-strength issue.
