## 1. What was run
Diploid free-recombination treadmill, K = 1000 (Kscale: 500, 4000), s = 0.01 per ancestral copy, log-additive. Hard arm: 8 R values (1.05 ... 3) x 8 x-values plus lam = 1/300, 6 reps, 40k generations. Kscale: K in {500, 4000}, R in {1.111, 2}, 4 reps, 20k. softJ (juvenile competition, R-limited) 5 R x 3 lam, 3 reps, 30k. softWF (relative-fitness WF, R-free) 4 lam, 3 reps, 30k. D is measured in every cell. Mean-field D_det = 2 ln(2K) matches the measured D (15.2 vs 15.7-16.9 at K = 1000). Rescaling to Haldane's D = 30 uses lam50 x D_obs / 30. lam50 is "the rate at which 50% of 6 replicate populations of K = 1000 survive T generations"; it is a finite-population survival threshold, and Haldane's own budget is its mean-field limit (phi = 1, interval D / ln R, shown alongside below). R values: 1.111 is Haldane's 10% selective mortality; the others, including R = 3, are a sweep (no Day quote anchors R = 3; see 8.1 #7).

**D = 30 rescaling and K (corrected in the fix pass).** D = 30 intervals use D_obs (about 16-17 at K = 1000) and assume lam50 x D is invariant in D. It is not: lam50 x D rises with K (R = 1.111, 10k: 0.053 / 0.062 / 0.068 at K = 500 / 1000 / 4000). D = 2 ln 2K = 30 for a new mutation needs K of about 1.6e6, about 6 ln-units above K = 4000. Linear-in-ln K extrapolation gives lam50 x D of about 0.088-0.105 there (capped at phi = 1, i.e. ln R = 0.105), an interval of about **285-340 at 10k** (about 400 at 20k), not 484. Extrapolating over 6 ln-units from 3 points is speculative either way; what the data bound is: **the D = 30 interval at R = 1.111, 10k, lies between about 285 (phi = 1, Haldane's mean-field) and 484 (the K = 1000 value; 439 at K = 4000).** The earlier sentence "a true D = 30 would shorten the intervals by up to about 10-25%" had no derivation and is withdrawn. Every "slower than 1/300" statement in this file is therefore **conditional on K = 1000 (and K <= 4000)** until the proposed K = 16,000 run (8.2) is approved and run.

## 5. Interpretation (conditional; every verdict is conditional on the model)
- **The budget scales with ln R.** lam50 grows 16-21x from R = 1.111 to R = 3; the cap is phi ln R / D with phi falling from 0.9 (large R) to 0.3-0.6 (small R, long windows, K = 1000). At R = 2 the D = 30 interval is 52-66 generations, about 8-10x shorter than at R = 1.111. "10%" is Haldane's parameter, not a constant. Day's verbatim shared-budget statement holds; the size of the budget is ln R. Whether natural populations are under pure hard selection with ceiling regulation is not tested here (parallel to the soft-arm caveat below).
- **Haldane's figure is confirmed in his regime; no critic contested it.** With R = 1.111 and D rescaled to 30, the sustained interval at K = 1000 is 484-673 (bracket 363-727), and the extrapolated range at the K where D = 30 is about 285-484 (10k). Day's verbatim upper bound ("no more than approximately one ... per 300") holds in every window. Whether the cap is tighter than 1/300 (Day-favourable) or about equal to it (Haldane's mean-field) depends on K and awaits 8.2. At low R hard selection is fragile: R = 1.05 sustains about 1 per 1,000-2,000 generations at K = 1000 (resting on post hoc D_obs). At D = 20 (critics' lower D) the R = 1.111 interval is 323 / 339 / 449; D ranges from about 6 (recurrent mutation, H3) to 30 (new mutation at large K), and lower D shortens the interval.
- **Hossjer's 0.0351 (H5) is reachable under hard selection** from R between 2 and 2.7 at in-model D, the same R the earlier H3 run found, with the whole excess spent on adaptive substitution. That fits Hössjer's "far less than what equation (4) predicts" at any lower R: the run confirms his conditional; it does not refute it. Whether human-lineage R reached that is the open question both sides leave unsourced (R5 draft V17).
- **Soft selection sustains rates far above the hard cap in this model, provided fitness is purely relative.** Both soft arms sustained 10-60x the hard cap. softWF's fittest adult has 1.09-1.22x the mean expected offspring; softJ's differential is <= R by construction. The cost is relabelled, not removed: softWF D_obs (16.1-16.5) equals the hard cost, incurred as variance in relative fitness with no deaths, and softJ's log load per substitution is 1.2-6.5x the hard value (61-113 at R <= 1.111). At R = 1.111, lam = 0.1 softJ's per-generation log load is k x D_obs = 6.4, i.e. mean viability exp(-6.4) = 0.0017 relative to the all-derived genotype **(post hoc F)**; under the hard arm's accounting (viability = survival probability) such a population falls about 500-fold per generation. That genotype is absent from the population (the best individual is only about 1.3x the mean), so the number measures distance from a non-existent optimum, not deaths; whether natural populations can treat that cost as purely relative is the Haldane-ReMine versus Wallace question itself, which both soft arms assume answered Wallace's way. Whether natural populations resemble the soft arms is not tested here.
- **Day's Σ s_i <= s_max premise vs the data.** H8's Formal statement derives n_max = s_max / s̄ concurrent sweeps from Σ s_i <= s_max ≈ 1 (claim file, "Stated" assumptions; verbatim §3.3 locator to be attached at integration). In softWF at lam = 0.2 there are about 249 open loci, Σ s_i ≈ 2.5-5, with maxrel 1.22 and I = 3.9e-3; in softJ at R = 1.05, lam = 0.1, about 692 loci (Σ s_i ≈ 7-14). With free recombination the fittest individual's advantage tracks the variance (Σ s_i² p q), not Σ s_i, so a bounded differential does not bound Σ s_i. In the hard arm Σ s_i at the cap is 0.4-1.3 (R = 2-3), close to ln R: there the bound on concurrent sweeps comes from the death budget, not from the differential. So "s_max ≈ 1 bounds Σ s_i" is contradicted in-model under soft selection (sustainability conditional on purely relative fitness), and under hard selection the bound holds for a different reason (cost).
- **Term 3.** Term 3 (0.0658) is the mean-field formula ln R / D with s_max = ln R and d = 1. At the audit's mapping s_max = 1 means R = e, where lam50 is 12-18% below 0.0658, inside R1's 25%. It is not met literally even at R = 3 (brackets wholly below). Any shortfall makes the rate lower, which is the direction of Day's conclusion.
- **Model omissions all favour selection** (lowering the cap if corrected): no deleterious load sharing the budget, no non-selective mortality, free recombination, unlimited supply with re-seeding, an environment-only treadmill. In cost accounting the model is Day-favourable (re-seeding charges lost attempts to D). It is Haldane's setting, not a human model (H3 covers the map and load).

## 6. Who this helps
**Day side.** Day's verbatim Haldane statement is an upper bound and holds in every window: at Haldane's R and rescaled D the interval is 484-673 at K = 1000 (>= 300 in every bracket). Day's shared-budget sentence (H, §2.3) holds: the hard-arm budget is a population total over all concurrent loci (lam50 x D <= phi ln R). Day's §3.3.1 "None of them raises smax above order unity" holds in every soft cell. Term 3 is reproduced within R1 at R = e under the audit's mapping; the shortfall favours Day's conclusion. Hard selection at low R is fragile: R = 1.05 sustains about 1 per 1,000-2,000 generations at K = 1000 and phi falls to 0.3 in long windows. softJ's cost per substitution is 1.2-6.5x the hard value, and its per-generation log load at high rates would be fatal under absolute-fitness accounting: the soft-arm result needs fitness to be purely relative. The model's omissions all favour selection.
**Critics' side.** The budget's size is ln R, not a fixed 10%: the cap is 9-18x 1/300 at R >= e in-model. Hossjer's 0.0351 is sustained under hard selection from R = 2-2.7. Under soft selection the rate is not capped near the hard cap: 0.1-0.2 per generation with the fittest individual at 1.09-1.31x the mean and Crow's I under 1.2e-2, so "twice as many descendants" does not appear; the cost is incurred as relative-fitness variance (softWF D_obs 16.4, P8), not deaths. Day's premise Σ s_i <= s_max is exceeded in-model (Σ s_i ≈ 2.5-5 at softWF lam = 0.2) while the differential stays near 1. The "slower than 1/300" factor is a finite-K result: the data allow about 285 (Haldane's mean-field) at the K where D = 30. At the critics' lower D (20) the R = 1.111 interval is 323-449.
**Both.** Haldane's 1/300 at R = 1.111 and D = 30 is uncontested by critics and confirmed here within finite-size error. The dispute reduces to inputs the repo cannot source: R for the human lineage, how hard or soft selection was, and D (6-30). Neither side's headline number is "wrong"; they answer different R. The pre-registered mean-field predictions (this audit's own: P2, P3, P7) were too optimistic at low R and too pessimistic for soft selection; the audit's own model has no more standing than either side's.

## 7. Caveats for the reviewers
- Survival-based lam50 from 6 reps per cell; brackets are one x-step wide (about 10-25% in lam); window dependence is large at low R (R = 1.05: 0.0019 to 0.00095). With exactly 3/6 at a grid point lam50 sits on the lower bracket edge, so ties resolve downward (Day-favourable; pre-registered, unchanged).
- lam50 is 50% survival over T at K = 1000. For any finite K with fluctuating load phi tends to 0 as T grows, so a window-specific phi of 0.3 at K = 1000 says little about a population of 1e4-1e6; Haldane's budget is the mean-field (phi = 1) column.
- D = 30 rescaling depends on K (Section 4 note); all "slower than 1/300" statements are conditional on K = 1000.
- D_obs is taken from persisting reps in low-lam cells, so it carries a small survivor bias, and lam50 x D_obs mixes D at low lam with lam at lam50.
- Soft-arm D_obs is not the same quantity as the hard arm's death cost, though it is computed the same way (interpreted in Section 5).
- The extension decision (Section 2.1) was made after seeing 19 rows; it added points to every R and changed P1 and every R = 1.05 rescaled number (Section 2.2).
- P1 slope at R = 1.111 and the K = 500 cells rest on few persisting reps.
- Scored with the pre-registered estimators. The fix pass rescored P1 (pre-registered-cells view), PD1 40k, PC2 R = 2 10k, P3 and P6 d = 0.45 20k/40k (bracket rule), PD2/PD3/PD4 (split into Day's verbatim statements and the audit's glosses), PC1 (wording), and added the verbatim-bound and Term 3 at R = e rows.
