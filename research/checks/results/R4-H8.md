## 1. What was run
Diploid free-recombination treadmill, K = 1000 (Kscale: 500, 4000), s = 0.01 per ancestral copy, log-additive. Hard arm: 8 R values (1.05 ... 3) x 8 x-values plus lam = 1/300, 6 reps, 40k generations. Kscale: K in {500, 4000}, R in {1.111, 2}, 4 reps, 20k. softJ (juvenile competition, R-limited) 5 R x 3 lam, 3 reps, 30k. softWF (relative-fitness WF, R-free) 4 lam, 3 reps, 30k. D is measured in every cell. Mean-field D_det = 2 ln(2K) matches the measured D (15.2 vs 15.7-16.9 at K = 1000). Rescaling to Haldane's D = 30 uses lam50 x D_obs / 30.

## 2. Post hoc disclosure (everything changed or chosen after seeing data)
1. **Grid extension (af09e77).** After the first 19 main rows showed low-R extinctions at x = 0.6 (K = 4000, R = 1.111 extinct within 20k generations), lower-x cells were APPENDED: hard x in {0.1, 0.2, 0.3} for every R (6 reps, 40k), Kscale x in {0.2, 0.3, 0.45} for both K and both R (4 reps, 20k). Existing cell indices and seeds are unchanged; no prediction was changed. The run was relaunched with resume, so the first 19 rows come from the acd3a9e script and the rest from af09e77 (the engine is the same; only the cell list differs). The extension was applied to all R, not only the low-R cells; it was triggered by one cell's outcome, but it only adds bracketing points and does not drop or reweight any pre-registered cell.
2. **Estimator side effect.** The extension changes the lam50 brackets at low R (more points below the old lowest x = 0.4). The pre-registered estimator is unchanged. The R = 1.111 values would have been unbracketed or lower without it.
3. The analyse stage was run once, on workhorse, after collection. No other post hoc analysis, rerun or selection was done.

## 5. Interpretation (conditional; every verdict is conditional on the model)
- **Haldane's arithmetic is reproduced in his regime.** With R = 1.111 (his 10% selective mortality) and D rescaled to 30, the sustained interval is 484-673 generations, 1.6x-2.2x longer than 300. A reader holding Haldane's inputs fixed gets "about 1 per 300-700 generations", not "far from 300". The 10% budget is a ln R budget: PD1 holds as a statement about R = 1.111, not about mammals.
- **The budget is not R-independent.** lam50 grows 16-21x from R = 1.111 to R = 3; the cap is phi ln R / D with phi falling from 0.9 (large R) to 0.3-0.6 (small R, long windows). At R = 2 the D = 30 interval is 52-66 generations, about 8-10x shorter than at R = 1.111.
- **Hossjer's 0.0351 (H5) is reachable** from R between 2 and 2.7 at in-model D, which is the same R the earlier H3 run found. Whether human-lineage R reached that is the open question both sides leave unsourced (R5 draft V17).
- **Soft selection removes the cap in this model.** Both soft arms sustained rates 10-60x above the hard cap, with relative fitness spread of the fittest individual (maxrel) 1.1-1.4, far below Day's "twice as many descendants" (2). The cost still appears as load-equivalent variance (softWF D_obs about 16.4). Whether natural populations resemble the soft arms is not tested here.
- **Day's formula structure vs parameter.** Term 3 (0.0658) is the mean-field critic formula ln R / D with s_max = ln R; at s_max = 1 it needs R about e and is not met even at R = 3 in the grid (P6).

## 6. Who this helps
**Day side.** PD1: at Haldane's R and rescaled D the interval is 484-673, within a factor 2.2 of 1/300, so Haldane's number is not an arithmetic slip; the cost is real in Haldane's regime. The hard-cap structure (rate proportional to ln R / D, measured D equal to 2 ln 2K) agrees with Day's formula and with the cost-of-selection logic. Hard selection at low R does collapse: R = 1.05 sustains about 1 per 1,000-2,000 generations and extinction rates rise in long windows (phi falls to 0.3). Term 3 at s_max = 1 is not reachable even at R = 3 here.
**Critics' side.** PD2 (an R-independent budget), PD3 (soft selection capped at the hard cap) and PD4 (0.0658 at R = 2) are refuted. The cap grows with ln R and is 9-18x 1/300 at R >= e in-model; Hossjer's 0.0351 is sustained from R = 2-2.7; soft selection (softJ and softWF) sustains 0.1-0.2 per generation with maxrel under 1.4 and Crow's I under 1.2e-2, so the "twice as many descendants" requirement does not appear.
**Both.** The entire dispute reduces to two inputs the repo cannot source: R for the human lineage, and how hard or soft the selection was. Neither side's headline number is "wrong"; they answer different R. The pre-registered mean-field predictions (this audit's own, P2, P3, P7) were too optimistic at low R and too pessimistic for soft selection; the audit's own model has no more standing than either side's.

## 7. Caveats for the reviewers
- Survival-based lam50 from 6 reps per cell; brackets are one x-step wide (about 10-25% in lam); window dependence is large at low R (R = 1.05: 0.0019 to 0.00095).
- D = 30 rescaling is approximate (Section 4). Diploid, free recombination, one new copy per substitution, no deleterious load, no linkage: it is Haldane's setting, not a human model (H3 covers the map and load).
- Soft-arm D_obs is not the same quantity as the hard arm's death cost, though it is computed the same way; in softJ it is 61-113 at low R, which this write-up does not interpret.
- The extension decision (Section 2.1) was made after seeing 19 rows, though it added points to every R.
- P1 slope at R = 1.111 and the K = 500 cells rest on few persisting reps.
- Scored with the pre-registered estimators; no score was altered after the analysis.
