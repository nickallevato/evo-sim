## 2. Day-side steelman
- **MINOR-4.** Zeng does estimate a mean s of ~0.001 and a mean 0.0007 by extrapolation for variants that affect traits, and trait variants under selection are the raw material beneficial changes would also draw on (selection on a trait acts in both directions on its alleles). Day's use of 0.001 as an order-of-magnitude scale is defensible as a stand-in; "misread" attaches to the wording "empirical mean for beneficial mutations", not necessarily to the magnitude. The write-up credits the "faster" sentence surviving across 0.0005-0.0010, but should say outright that fidelity is about the label and R1 about the number, and that the second is undetermined.
- **MINOR-5.** The finding that the sentence fails at N_e = 3,300 mixes two Day inputs from different texts. The write-up notes rule S/C and does not score it; make sure integration does not carry it into the verdict.
- **NOTE-3.** Latency does not cap the steady-state rate (F1); the throughput leg uses the LTEE rate. The misread is confined to latency arguments.

## 3. Critic-side steelman
- **MINOR-6.** Critics can say the sign matters, not just the label: Zeng's s is |s| under purifying selection, and selection coefficients for deleterious variants say nothing about adaptive ones. The (2/s) ln(2N_e) formula overstates stochastic fixation time 2.3x, and at N_e = 3,300 (Day's Z18525547 value) it is slower than 4N_e. The write-up has all three; ordering the first (attribution) ahead of the arithmetic would match the strength.
- **NOTE-4.** Neither side can fix the number without a sourced distribution of beneficial s in the human lineage (QUEUE item 8); the write-up says so.
