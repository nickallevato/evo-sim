## Rule C and the frame-error inference (asked explicitly)
- **Is it labelled?** Yes. The mixture is labelled post hoc in the commit, the docstring, caveat 5 and the table. The exon follow-up was pre-registered and failed its own predictions, and the failure is reported. That is to the write-up's credit.
- **Is it charitable?** Partly. Rule C was applied in the right order: Day's own reading, a real gene-set effect, was tested first and fails on the Q176 arithmetic (NON121 would need 2.42). After that, attributing the 0.80 to an extraction problem is legitimate. Day's own ¶17 "10 remaining genes" under "no stop codons in either species" supports this independently of any audit model.
- **Is it falsifiable?** The general class is falsifiable by Day's per-gene table. The specific gene-level mixture is falsifiable, and Day's own Q175 already falsifies it (finding 2).
- **Does it claim more than shown?** Yes, in three places. "The share is an independent match" (finding 1); "matches out-of-frame reading" in the critic bullet and the DN1 comment, where nothing selects frame errors over other noise sources; and the omission of the counter-evidence in Q175. The closing hedge ("evidence for a class of error, not a finding about Day's code") is right, but the class has to be stated broadly enough for the evidence to support it.

## R4-X1 applied to the proposed verdicts
- **DN1** "0.80 on 20,400 genes". Internal: `pending` agreed. The method is unreleased (N5-like: the derivation cannot be recovered). Rule F does not apply. F scores borrowed uncited inputs, and this is Day's own computation, so fidelity is `n/a`. "Releasing no code" is not by itself "unverifiable"; it leaves internal `pending`. External: the proposal "contradicted on hg38, pending on T2T" is two values in one field. **Recommend `contradicted`**, with the comment "hg38/panTro5 replication; the T2T route is bounded (finding 12); L1b confirmatory". If reviewers prefer caution, use `contested` until L1b, but not a split value. Slip: the implied N/S of 3.55 against 3.34 is 6% (R1: ledger).
- **DN2** "no filter combination below 0.65". Internal: `pending` (thresholds unstated). The ¶17 0.64 against "below 0.65" is a 1.5% self-inconsistency in the same paragraph (R1: slip ledger). External: the proposition is about Day's pipeline, but as a statement about human-chimp genes, every one of the 32 stacks gives 0.23-0.28. **Recommend `contradicted` (hg38)**, on the same basis as DN1.
- **DN3** the Q176 conditional, plus ¶15/¶18 once quoted. Internal: the conditional's dialectical core ("a set difference of 0.23 against 0.80 would show a gene-set artefact") is well formed. "Cherry-picked" and "artificial" are rhetoric (RH3, already RH-22). Under rule C the core holds as a test design. ¶18's "only way to get 0.23" is hedged ("As far as I can tell so far"). **Recommend internal `holds`** (the core), with an N2d note that even the antecedent would not show *cherry-picking*, because a difference concentrated in non-one2one genes would equally fit misaligned paralogs. External: the antecedent fails on hg38 (ALL 0.271, an effect of +0.06 against the +0.57 needed). **Recommend `contradicted`**, with the credit "a real, pipeline-level set effect in Day's direction (+31%)" in the comment, not as a second value. Fidelity `pending` (CSAC 2005 unread).
- **DN4** "not the primary mechanism of adaptive evolution". Rule N can be applied now from Day's own text, so "not scored until checked against Day's own material" is not needed. ¶6 defines the ratio as a measure of *purifying* removal ("If natural selection is purifying and removes harmful mutation then Ka/Ks should be well below 1.0"), and ¶20 reads 0.80 as "purifying selection is removing only about 20%". The conclusion in ¶21 is about *adaptive* evolution. With Day's premises granted (0.80 true), the step from the purifying-removal rate to the mechanism of adaptation is stronger than they support (N2d). It also runs against direction: a higher ratio is equally consistent with more positive selection. Charitable reading (C): "most coding substitutions are not driven by selection". That reading follows from 0.80, but it also follows from 0.23 under neutral theory, so it does not support the stated conclusion. **Recommend internal `non-sequitur` (N2d, own-source basis ¶6/¶20/¶21)**, fidelity `n/a`, external `contradicted` on the premise (DN1). The "secondary ... tertiary" sentence stays rhetoric (RH-23).
- **DN5** (relay of CSAC 0.23). External `supported` (in kind: 0.207/0.200 on an analogue set) is reasonable. Fidelity stays `pending` until the 2005 text is read (rule U: not internal).

## Summary
The numbers are right, the estimator is right, the provenance is clean and the pre-registered scoring is honest. The core results stand: all genes give 0.27, one2one genes 0.21, filters span 0.23-0.28, and the Q176 conditional fails on hg38. They are enough for the DN1-DN3 external verdicts. The post hoc frame-error attribution overclaims. Its "independent" share match is an identity of the fitted ratio (finding 1), and Day's own filter results contradict the specific gene-level mixture (finding 2). Rewrite it as an estimator-free, mechanism-unspecified extraction/alignment class, and run the per-gene post hoc dump (finding 3) before any frame-error wording is integrated.
