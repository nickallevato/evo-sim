Reviewer role: argue as Vox Day's (and Athos's) most capable and honest defender would. Questions asked: how much of the result rests on choices that were the audit's and not his? **Reviewer's own numbers below are post hoc**, computed from the per-gene table after reading the write-up, one process, no rerun. None was registered. They are offered for the fix pass to check, not as results.

Overall. From Day's side the write-up is more careful than L1's first draft. It says the pipeline is not his, it reports the failed audit prediction A4 without softening, it declines to name his error, and it gives him the strict-stack point. What is left falls in four places:
- DN1 carries one reading where rule RD asks for the readings to be listed.
- The single-difference share is used in two opposite directions.
- The frame-preserving arm does not test his 2026-10-12 mechanism, and the one number that bears on it is not reported.
- His gene-set credit is marked down with a rule that was written for something else, against a comparator he did not name.

The honest cost to Day is stated first, so that nothing below is read past it. Nothing in L1b supports 0.80 as a property of human-chimp coding sequence. The 16,899 genes (87.5%) in which V0 leaves out no codon at all read 0.212 pooled, with a single-difference share of 40.3%. His Q183 ("The signal is uniform across the genome, it's not concentrated in problematic genes.") predicts about 0.80 there.
