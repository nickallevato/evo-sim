# Review of R4-L1b (steelman, critics' side)

Reviewer role: argue as the most capable and honest critic of Day's dN/dS claims would (a population geneticist who holds that human-chimp dN/dS is about 0.2-0.25 and that 0.80 is an extraction artefact). Questions asked: is the conclusion worded as strongly as the registered result licenses; is the X1 result booked on the right side; how far does the "register error" inference go under the rule without Day's pipeline; is credit given to Day that the data do not support.

Reviewed (2026-10-13, head e163ca2): `R4-L1b.md` at 188ea43 (whole); docstring of `l1b_t2t_dnds.py` (0fe3c7f); pre-run note (L1b section); `raw/l1b.out`, `raw/l1b.json`, `raw/l1b.genes.tsv.gz`; quotes Q172-Q177, Q180-Q186; claim files DN1, DN2, DN3, DN5 (verdict lines and R4 paragraphs); `R4-X1-verdict-rule.md` (R1, N, F, U, RD); `REVIEW-R4-L1b-correctness.md` (findings table, so as not to repeat it). The Day-side steelman was not read. No literature is cited from memory.

**Reviewer computations.** All numbers marked "reviewer" below are post hoc, computed from `raw/l1b.genes.tsv.gz` (one process, seconds), with the write-up's own "affected" rule (X1 differs from V0 in Sd, Nd, s1 or n1). They reproduce the write-up's table (affected 2,637 genes, X1 0.960, share 73.6%; unaffected 0.205, 39.8%; ALL 0.2712 and 0.8532). None is registered. None moves a band.

Overall: the run is clean and the registered scoring is honest. The band is CRITIC and no verdict value changes; I agree with every row of the verdict-move table. The weaknesses are on the wording side. (a) The write-up proposes, post hoc, to qualify the registered CRITIC wording in Day's direction on the strength of an extraction that compares unaligned positions, which is not what Day's own sentence describes. (b) It books the X1 result as help to Day. (c) It understates how closely the mispaired genes match the pattern Day reports in Q183 and Q184. Where I think the critics' case stops, I say so (findings 4, 11).
