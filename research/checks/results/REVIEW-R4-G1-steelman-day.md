# Review of R4-G1 (steelman, Day's side)

Reviewer role: argue as Vox Day's most capable and honest defender would. Question asked: does the G1 check test the "Bernoulli Barrier" claims as Day stated them, at their strongest, and are Day-favourable results under-reported?

Reviewed: `research/checks/results/R4-G1.md`; `research/checks/g1_specific_vs_any.py` (docstring and Part B only); claims G, Ga, Gb, Gc, Gd, Ge, G1, G3, G3b, G4, D, D10; Day sources read-only under `sources/raw/day/` (Z18167588 s2.3, s3, s6.1, s7.6 to 7.11; Z18165980 Discussion; blog 2026-09-30 and 2026-10-01). I ran no code.

Overall: the arithmetic audit and the specific-versus-any separation are sound, and the write-up already credits Day with many points (its section 7, "Day: valid points", items 1 to 8). The weaknesses are in framing and in how the verdict table is worded. Several Day-favourable findings are in the prose but not carried into the verdict rows. A few labels attribute intent or call something an error where "numerical coincidence", "revision" or "different question" would be accurate.

## Day-side points the write-up should state more prominently

1. Day is right on the arithmetic of every headline power, on the 1/√n compression of the count's CV, on independence of co-segregating sweeps under free recombination (pair ratio 1.03 ± 0.02), and on the specific horn (about 10^-8.4e6 even with recurrent mutation). These should sit in the first paragraph.
2. In Day's own configuration, 157,000 concurrent p = 0.5 sweeps would need best/mean fitness of 335× and N_eff ≈ 355 (of 10,000). His infeasibility conclusion is supported; only the named mechanism differs, and s6.1 names the ceiling as a complementary constraint.
3. On the additive scale Day's numbers use, there is a genuine barrier of the stated order (halving at n ≈ 200; 786× dilution at 157,000), and he flagged diminishing-returns epistasis in s3.1. The multiplicative case is one end of a bracket, not "the model Day states".
4. The "no cap" results are soft-selection results. H2-hard shows 70–86% selective death at 167–260 open loci, and extinction at low reproductive excess.
5. The check does not test his current main argument. His 2026-09-30 and 2026-10-01 posts treat parallel fixation as already inside G_f, and he reports a serial LTEE figure of about 24,500 generations/fixation. The throughput question for 2e5 adaptive fixations (0.67–0.79 per generation) is open.
6. The "any n of M" calculation supports his rate framing. Gd is itself an "any" calculation, and the flip-point analysis shows P_all falls to zero below λ ≈ 11–16; the interchangeable case is not free.
7. McCarthy's P_any ≈ 1 holds only under the neutral k = μ model that Day disputes in branch B, and a neutral count does not explain functional divergence (the write-up credits this; it deserves the same prominence as the critic-side finding).
