# Review of R4-E: steelman, critics' side

Reviewer stance: the most capable honest defender of mainstream population genetics and of the critics who answered Day. Scope: `results/R4-E.md`, `e_founder_hazard.py`, `e4_relictation_chain.py`, claims `E`, `E1`-`E4`, `E7`. Local sources under `sources/raw/` were read or grepped only. Statements marked "(recalled)" come from memory and are not retrieved. Nothing was re-run.

Overall: R4-E is unusually candid about the consequence gap and about the diploid mapping, and several of the strongest critic-side points are already in it. The issues below are mostly about labels, ordering and emphasis, plus one omission in the corpus survey (row 6).

## Critic-side points to state more prominently

1. The population-level endpoint reverses the E result (about 600x rarer, 0.44% over 100 events), and the aa-birth endpoint is small next to the standing recessive load (row 1, 2).
2. E4's growth with N holds only if family size scales with N; for fixed family size Kingman is recovered and the excess shrinks (row 3).
3. E4 changes the time per fixation, never P_fix or the substitution rate, so it cannot support the rate arguments of the other branches (row 5).
4. Critic-side prior art exists for the sweepstakes chain (Keruru, 2026-08-26), with the same exact 1/(2N) and Ne reduction; Day's new content is limited to the t-based extension (row 6).
5. The LTEE mutator evidence is from asexual, very large populations; the corpus's critic (arctic review) argues recombination decouples mutator alleles and that the mutator averaging is artefactual (row 8).
6. The "known result relabelled" reading of E4 (multiple-merger coalescents) should be stated outright, with the caveat that the primary literature is still unretrieved (row 4).

## Where R4-E is not too generous (for balance)

- It already separates the aa-birth endpoint from the fitness endpoint, flags the 0.06 x 0.39 route as wrong in both factors, and exposes the hwe artefact; these are critic-favourable findings.
- The "abstract versus body" point on the 10% threshold is accurate and fair to both sides.
- The headline rate being higher than Day's (2.75% vs 2.33%) is a real finding in Day's favour and is correctly reported.
