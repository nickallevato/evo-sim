# R4 review: Day-side steelman (H, C2, B3b/B3c, C1/C6, B1c, B4a, F2, A-sim)

Reviewer role: argue Day's strongest honest reading of each claim and ask whether the check tests it. Read-only audit of `R4-H-C2.md`, `R4-B3b-C1.md`, `R4-B1c-B4a.md`, `R4-F2-A.md`, the claim files, `quotes-day.md`, `balance.md` and `parameters.yaml`. I did not re-run any script. Numbers marked "(derived here)" are my own back-of-envelope arithmetic from the audit's tables, not simulations. `sources/raw/day/` is not present in this checkout, so quote locators are the ones given in the claim files and `quotes-day.md` (Q-numbers).

Where Day is simply wrong, I say so. The weight below falls on what the checks leave untested and on where the suggested verdicts overstate.

## Cross-cutting findings

1. **Day's current position is narrower than several R4 verdicts assume.** The 2026-05-07 retraction (H1, Q65-Q69) concedes that cost-based limits bind only *adaptive* substitution, and that total k is governed by input flux and polymorphism throughput. Day's blog (B1d, 2026-10-01) concedes a *full* pipe at the split. MITTENS 3.0 drops d (A4d). The B1c, B4a and B3c checks refute the neutral-count form (empty pipe, k = 0.743 mu). The residual claim that still bites is "adaptive fixation is limited by cost of selection (hard selection) and by interference". That is branch H and F2, and those are exactly the checks with the largest unmodelled gaps. The R4 headlines "contradicted" and "refuted" are strong for neutral accounting, but they do not reach what Day currently defends.
2. **Soft selection only, in the checks that matter most.** F2, A-sim and the `soft` arm of H all remove the demographic cost by construction. H's hard arm is the only place cost appears, and it concludes that M and R decide the outcome and that human M is undetermined. So no check has tested Day's central residual claim at human parameters. That is an open question, not a Day loss.
3. **Scale gaps.** Every simulation runs at N = 500 to 2000 or at a scaled N. The human-relevant quantities (number of simultaneously active selected loci, M = 2Ku) are orders of magnitude beyond the validated range (see F2).
4. **Inputs chosen from the critics' side are not flagged as such.** Yoo's Ne,anc is paired with pedigree mu, the step contraction at the split is the most extreme, and the C1 designs and Ne are the audit's own. None is unreasonable, but each moves the result toward the critics, and the suggested verdicts do not mention it.

## B1c: ancestral pipeline state (Ne history)

1. **Day's strongest reading.** The claim is not "no standing variation". Day's formula E[count] = mu L (T - 4Ne) is stated for *new* fixations since the split (Z22903977 §3, Q35, "At generation zero, the substitution pipeline is empty"). Day then concedes the pipe was full at the split, "228,000 generations long" (B1d, 2026-10-01 ¶31, ¶36). In other words, **Day himself puts ancestral Ne,anc at about 57,000** (4Ne = 228,000), not 10^4. The claim to test is therefore "how much does the post-split fixation count fall short of mu L T *for post-split mutations*, and what covers the remainder?"
2. **Does the check test it?** Partly.
3. **What it legitimately shows.** Contraction from Yoo's 1.3-2.0e5 gives K/UT of 1.9-3.9 on every sourced history. The "new mutations" column (0.84 at Ne = 1e4) *reproduces Day's formula*: (T - 4Ne)/T = 0.84. The audit's reading section says so ("Day's formula is right for post-split mutations"). The excess comes from ancestral alleles fixing in the lineage, **and K counts alleles that fix in *both* lineages, which are not human-chimp differences**. The audit itself says "K is not the observable; B4a is". So B1c's excess is not a test of Day's claim about how many *new* fixations are required per lineage. It tests whether "empty start" is realistic, and Day has already abandoned that (B1d). The verdict wording "contradicted: ... an excess of 1.9-3.9x U T" overstates, because K-with-ancestral-alleles is not the quantity Day's formula bounds.
4. **Fairness issues.**
   - The human lineage is stepped to 1e4 *at the split*. That is the most contraction-heavy choice available and produces the largest excess. H3 and C2 are milder but still start from 1.3-2.0e5.
   - Takahata is cited at only 10x modern Ne for more than 4.6 My, a different divergence date from 6.3 My. PM2013 PSMC curves were not extracted, so the human histories are scenarios.
   - The controls sit 1.2-1.6% below 1 (2-3 SE), an unexplained bias. It is small beside the effects, but unexplained.
5. **Additional check for fairness.**
   - Report, per scenario, K *restricted to alleles that are not fixed in the other lineage* (a difference-restricted K). This is the quantity Day's "required fixations" counts. B4a's fixed differences are close to this but are not decomposed into ancestral-origin versus post-split-origin.
   - Add an Ne,anc of about 5.7e4 (Day's own B1d number). By interpolation d would be about 0.88% (2 mu T = 0.605% plus 4 Ne mu = 0.274%, derived here), about 71% of the observed 1.23%.

## B4a: pairwise divergence with ancestral polymorphism

1. **Day's strongest reading.** "Ancestral polymorphism is a rounding error" (Z22903977 §3, Q37; B6a) at Ne = 1e4 (0.35% of 410M). Day's *real* point is that the model E[d] = 2 mu T + 4 Ne,anc mu has three unknowns (T, mu, Ne,anc) and the single measured divergence (1.23%) cannot fix them. A different Ne,anc moves the "needed" T. Day also argues that Ne estimated by the coalescent depends on mu (B3h, Q-B2026-08-27).
2. **Does the check test it?** Yes for the arithmetic, partly for the claim's *external* assertion.
3. **What it legitimately shows.** The simulation reproduces 2 mu T + theta_anc to about 1%, agrees with msprime, and shows Day's "rounding error" holds only at Ne,anc = 1e4. **At Day's own Ne = 1e4 the model gives 0.65%, about half the observed 1.23%; matching it needs T of about 492,000 generations.** That is Day's direction: a 6.3 My / pedigree mu / Ne = 1e4 combination cannot produce the observed divergence. The reconciliation depends entirely on Ne,anc of 1.3-2.0e5.
4. **Where the verdict overstates.**
   - "Supported at Yoo's Ne,anc (1.32e5 reproduces 1.23%)" is a coincidence of three free parameters. The audit says so in prose, but the verdict table says "supported". At Yoo's HCB Ne (1.98e5) d is 25% too high, so the same Yoo Ne rejects the model as readily as supports it, depending on which ancestor is used.
   - **CSAC's measured 14-22% polymorphism share versus the model's 51-61% theta_anc share is left "unresolved".** Day can fairly say that if the consortium's share is taken at face value, T must be 400-440k generations (10-11 My) or mu about 1.6e-8, and the model does not reproduce observed divergence at 6.3 My. The audit notes the definitions may differ, which is plausible but untested. "Poly-but-diff" (8-27%) is the like-for-like column, and it is close to CSAC.
   - **Yoo's Ne,anc is scaled by its own mu and generation time.** The simulation multiplies it by pedigree mu = 1.2e-8. theta_anc = 4 Ne mu is invariant only if both use the same mu. The repo does not record which mu Yoo used. If Yoo used a different mu, theta_anc here is mis-scaled by mu_Yoo/1.2e-8. The audit's own caveat for PM2013 (Ne scales as 1/mu, about 2x under the pedigree rate) is exactly this issue, and it applies to Yoo too.
   - **Does Day dispute Yoo's Ne?** I found no passage where Day disputes Yoo's ancestral Ne. He uses Yoo as an authority for required substitution rates and divergence (k = 32.3 mu, Q43), he grants a full pipe at the split (B1d), and his only Ne objection is the circularity of Ne = 1e4 from theta = 4 Ne mu (B3h). The circularity charge is weak for a pedigree mu (the claim file itself says so, and Z22129121 says "theta = 4 Ne mu is untouched"). So Yoo's Ne is not a straw input, but the audit should state that it relies on Day not having engaged it, not on Day having conceded it.
5. **Additional check.** (i) Re-express the model in terms of theta_anc (observed ancestral diversity) rather than Ne,anc, to remove the mu mismatch; (ii) find Yoo's mu and g and rescale Ne,anc; (iii) run Ne,anc = 5.7e4 (Day's B1d); (iv) propagate the joint (T, mu, Ne,anc) uncertainty (T 5.5-8 My, mu 1.1-1.5e-8) instead of reporting point matches.

## F2: multi-locus interference (Bernoulli Barrier cap of about 230)

1. **Day's strongest reading.** Not "interference collapses throughput at 230 loci with free recombination" (the 230 is "working backward", no derivation, Gc). The strongest reading is: *the selection differential an organism can physically deliver is bounded* ("the differential available to any individual is bounded by what the organism can physically achieve", Z19984826 §3.3, Q in H8; s_max of order 1, §3.3.1 "none of them raises s_max above order unity"). That is a hard-selection statement. Hössjer, an ally, accepts the cost step, and Nunney confirms hard-selection cost exists for M < 1/2.
2. **Does the check test it?** No for the strongest reading. Partly for the Bernoulli-count reading.
3. **What it legitimately shows.** With free recombination, soft selection, N = 1000, s = 0.01, there is no throughput collapse up to about 270 active loci (R_int 0.97 +/- 0.004). This does falsify "a hard cap of about 230 simultaneous sweeps from variance compression with unlinked loci under soft selection". The linear scaling is fair and fwdpy11 agrees at three points. The clonal and tight-linkage results (R_int 0.09-0.2) support Day's *qualitative* claim that interference is real.
4. **Why it dodges Day's stronger form.**
   - Soft selection: N is fixed and every genotype is replaced one for one. There is no demographic cost, by construction. The audit admits "cost of selection (branch H) not tested". Day's Term 3 / Haldane position is precisely about hard selection. The F2 verdict "contradicted" should therefore carry the qualifier "under soft selection".
   - The human-relevant active-loci count is far beyond 270. By Little's law (derived here: n_active = rate x sweep duration), the 17.5M SNV differences over 252,000 generations is 69 per generation, times a 440-generation transit in the 0.1-0.9 zone (Gc) is about 3e4 active loci. With sweep duration (2/s) ln(2N) at s = 0.01, N = 1e4 (about 1,980 generations) it is about 1.4e5. That is 2-3 orders above F2's maximum. The pre-registered additional prediction itself says "R < 0.8 needs n_mid of order 10^2-10^3", so the test never reached its own breakpoint, only the top of its own predicted range. It also assumes *all* differences are adaptive, which Day has retracted for total k (H1), so it overstates the load Day still needs.
   - Per-Morgan density: 23 human chromosomes of about 1.5 Morgans each is free between chromosomes, but at 1e3-1e4 active loci per chromosome the linked-map results (0.58 at 208 loci on one 1.5 M group) become the relevant regime. Note the scaling direction: the sim at N = 1000, s = 0.01 corresponds to N = 1e4, s = 0.001 with Morgans scaled by 10, so a real 1.5 M chromosome is a *more* recombining sim, which favours the critics. The audit's "1.5 M is pessimistic" stands, but the density beyond 270 is untested.
   - Implied Haldane load (derived here, using the audit's own D = ln(1/p0) haploid, p0 = 1/2000, D = 7.6): at 2N U_b = 32 the rate of 0.615/gen needs about 4.7 units of selective death per generation. At 2N U_b = 2 it is about 0.29, which is already 3x Haldane's 10% budget (0.105). So the high-supply F2 cells, where the "no collapse" result is strongest, correspond to regimes impossible under hard selection with D = 7.6. This is the audit's own point ("H binds first"), but it is not carried into the F2 verdict.
5. **Additional checks.** (i) F2 with hard selection (fecundity R and carrying capacity, as in H Model 1) at n_mid up to 1e3-1e4 and a realistic map; (ii) N = 1e4 unscaled for at least one cell; (iii) with a DFE, since s = 0.01 uniform has no weak-effect load; (iv) measure per-locus P_fix directly at n_active about 230 (the Gc falsifier) rather than inferring it from throughput. The audit says R_int is "the same quantity averaged", which is true on average but hides locus-to-locus differences.

## A-sim: LTEE scaling and the "ceiling"

1. **Day's strongest reading.** (A5d, Z23003785 p.8) "Supermutation does not scale linearly with mutation rate. ... fixation is ... bottlenecked by the dynamics of sweeps." A 100x mutation increase gives 8.5-17x. (A2e) The LTEE is "the empirical ceiling" for what evolution can do under the LTEE's conditions, and the strictly weaker claim is that supply scaling by the factor 94,000 cannot be assumed linear (KITTENS's A5b assumption).
2. **Does the check test it?** Partly. The sublinear half yes; the ceiling half only by extrapolation.
3. **What it legitimately shows.** Asexual response to supply is sublinear (a = 0.24-0.35, even more than Day's measured 0.47-0.61), so **KITTENS's linear scaling is not supported for an asexual genome**. The audit says this ("partial credit to Day"). It also agrees with Day that the LTEE is interference-limited, i.e. is a *lower* bound of what recombination permits. That does not make "ceiling" right, but it shows Day's mechanism (sweep dynamics) is real in this regime.
4. **Where it is weaker or unfair.**
   - The "7x to 174x" free-recombination factor (A2e, A5f) is *not a simulation*: it is the F2 pattern (R_int of about 1) pushed to the LTEE-calibrated supply at Ne = 3.3e7, where 2N U_b spans 0.3 to 1e6 and n_mid is unknown. The audit labels it an extrapolation, but then uses it for the A2e verdict "not supported for recombining organisms".
   - The calibration is underdetermined by three orders of magnitude in U_b (6e-7 to 6e-10), Ne is from memory, there is a single s and no DFE. The 7x or 174x therefore reflects the calibration choice (1.6x at s = 0.03).
   - The simulated asexual response is *weaker* than Day's observed mutator response. The audit attributes this to the absent DFE, but it means the model does not reproduce the data it is used to assess. Day's measured number is empirical and stands.
   - The A5f verdict "Day's 'recombination won't speed up fixation' is contradicted ... Kimura-Ohta 1969 is single-locus" is fair as a reading of Day's citation, but the quote is Reddit's (RE-07) and Gariepy's; Day's own words are "double-edged sword" (blog 2026-10-01), which the sim does not address (recombination also breaks up favourable combinations; segregation costs).
5. **Additional check.** An unscaled or properly scaled recombining simulation at the calibrated supply (even Ne = 1e5 with c-scaling, which A-sim validated for clonal only) to see whether the free-recombination factor survives well beyond n_mid about 270; include a DFE and mild epistasis (Day cites diminishing-returns epistasis in LTEE, E7).

## H: cost of selection and Term 3

1. **Day's strongest reading.** (H, Z18168236 §2.3 and Abstract) "Haldane calculated that mammals could fix no more than approximately one beneficial substitution per 300 generations", "The 10% selective mortality is a total budget for the population, not a per-locus allocation." After the retraction, the claim is only about *selected* substitutions (H1).
2. **Does the check test it?** Partly. The arithmetic and the Nunney qualitative M-dependence, yes. The human regime, no.
3. **What it legitimately shows.** 300, 487 and 41,068 reproduce. In the audit's own hard-selection model, T50 is about 285 at R = 2.2 (a 10% budget), M = 0.1, n = 1, which matches Haldane. The limit moves from 300 to 30-80 at larger M or R. Human M is undetermined by the repo's sources. The audit makes this point itself and gives no verdict on the human regime. Keightley's U = 2.2 test shows hard selection needs about 18 offspring per female, and Day can use that: it says a hard-selection human population is near its limit, whichever way the deleterious-load debate goes.
4. **Where the audit is unfair or overstated.**
   - "I: fails as a comparison to the 20M total" is correct for total k but Day *already conceded* it (H1). The suggested verdict should read "scope concession applies; residual claim untested", not "fails", since the argument now concerns adaptive substitutions only. Whether H1 extends to H is flagged by the audit as undecided.
   - The soft arm is "global competition for K slots proportional to w". The audit notes Wallace local soft selection is not simulated. Day's reply in §3.3.1 (truncation, soft, composite fitness only redistribute the budget) is about exactly these forms, and the sim contradicts it only in the region where R is ample; at R <= 3 (human-like) soft selection performed *worse* than hard.
   - The reconstruction of Nunney's Gaussian model does not reproduce his absolute values (2 to 12x lower at R = 10), so the match at R = 2.2 may be partly luck. This weakens the claim that "the limit moves to 30-80 generations", which depends on the reconstructed model.
   - Term 3 correction "17.1 vs 7.7" is a fair correction of the R2 note. Day does not lose by it; it moves the discrepancy between his two cost figures.
   - Haldane's D = 30 is an input, and the audit finds D = 92-278 generations for standing variation (p0 = 1e-2 to 1e-6, diploid). That is a **real point against Day's 300** (it is the upper end). It is also **below** the 300 only for standing variation; for new mutations (p0 = 1/2N = 5e-5, D about 20 diploid) it gives about 200, the same order as Haldane. So Day's 300 is within a factor of 1.5 for the new-mutation case that his argument uses.
5. **Additional check.** Estimate human M = 2 K mu_b per beneficial target from a stated DFE of beneficial effects (say, from Zeng 2021 or an explicit assumption), then run the hard-selection model at that M with human-like R (about 2.2-3, with age structure). Without it, "contested" is the correct verdict, and no side is entitled to a win.

## C2 / C2a / A4: the turnover coefficient d

1. **Day's strongest reading.** d = (actual allele frequency change per generation) / (change predicted by the discrete-generation model) (Z18166234 §2.3, Q20); d = T x int(mu l v) / int(l v) (§3.3, Q21). In MITTENS 2025 it multiplies *the number of nominal generations* available (146,250 = 325,000 x 0.45). Day concedes researchers "appear to have been implicitly compensating" in their s (Discussion, per C2), which weakens the empirical use but also shows he knows s may already absorb d.
2. **Does the check test it?** Yes for the derivation; partly for the application.
3. **What it legitimately shows.** The integral equals the mean cumulative hazard at the age of mothers (0.789 vs 0.784). For selection acting as a fractional change of the hazard at all ages, d s is *exact* (V1, ratio 1.000), a point **in Day's favour**: his formula is the correct conversion for hazard-scale selection. For per-generation fitness (fecundity, pre-maturity survival) the factor is 1. Day's d s is 22% off for V3/V4 at e0 = 32. d is not identifiable apart from s, onset time, T and dominance (C2d).
4. **Is "d is a unit conversion" fair?** Mostly yes, and the audit's mathematics is right. But two qualifications:
   - Day's *definition* is a ratio of actual to discrete-model change. For hazard-scale s that ratio *is* d (V1), so the definition is satisfied in the V1 case. The audit's verdict "fails as stated" depends on Day claiming that d applies to *all* s. In the "demographic correction to the rate of selection" statement the audit attributes to Day, "for any s" is the audit's reading, not a quoted claim. Day's papers do not state which scale published s are on, so "unit conversion between two scales of s" (A4's phrasing) is closer to what d computes than "no meaning". A fair verdict is "valid for hazard-scale s; not shown to apply to the literature s values", not "fails".
   - Equivalence to the standard theory with generation length T/d (55.6 y for d = 0.45, T = 25) is the audit's own finding. Under that reading Day's claim is "effective generation length for selection per unit of hazard-scale s is T/d", an arithmetic restatement, not an error.
   - **Day-adverse points that hold:** d = -ln L for a semelparous annual organism (not 1 unless L = 1/e) contradicts "d = 1 for discrete generations" and "0 to 1"; published aDNA s are per-generation logistic slopes, so d = 1 for them; Table 1 d values (0.53 ... 0.015) were *not* reproduced (Siler gives 0.79/0.93 at e0 = 32), but Coale-Demeny West tables were not retrieved, so that is untested, not refuted. The ratio identity (C2c) is a trivial algebraic result, as the audit says.
5. **Additional check.** Retrieve Coale-Demeny West and Gurven-Kaplan life tables and reproduce Day's Table 1 d values, then check how each cited aDNA paper defines s (per generation, per year). If s is hazard-scale for any of them, d s holds for that locus.

## B3b / B3c: Balloux and Lehmann; RRME

1. **Day's strongest reading.** (Z22903977 §6, B3b) "k != mu under the joint conditions of fluctuating demography and overlapping generations." For RRME (Z18525262 eq. 1, 3; Q40): a cohort sampled in a fast-growing population yields k = 0.743 mu. Strongest reading of the latter: in growth, the number of *copies of the allele that can still fix* is dominated by the cohort sizes at birth.
2. **Does the check test it?** B3b yes. B3c yes.
3. **What it legitimately shows.** B&L's eq. (3) is reproduced by independent simulation (|z| < 1.6); k = mu without overlap holds (Kimura's identity stands); **k != mu with overlap plus fluctuation is real**, and in contrived two-state cycles the range is 0.59-1.70. So Day's *qualitative* B3b claim is right and well documented. For realistic expansion the effect is **upward** (+38% arrival rate, +70% in extreme cycles), consistent in sign with Day's later k > mu (Z18525547 dates "15-150x shorter") but not with his k = 0.743 mu in Z18525262, which is the opposite sign. This internal inconsistency in Day's own papers is the audit's finding, not Day's defence.
4. **Magnitude.** The audit finds the human-like effect to be -0.3% to -2% for symmetric cycles and +38% for one-way growth, so neither 0.743 nor 32.3 follows. The finding is robust for 0.743 (the mechanism test falsifies the 1/(2N_t) fixation probability). For 32.3, no derivation exists in the corpus; B&L cannot supply it.
5. **Caveats Day can legitimately raise.** (i) The per-mean-generation-time normalisation deviates 0.81-1.11 under strong fluctuation, and Lehmann 2014 was not retrieved, so the clock-relevant normalisation (per year) is not settled. Dating cares about per-year rates. (ii) The human-like cases are analytic (eq. 3), not simulated. (iii) The B&L model is haploid with uniform survival, no age structure. These do not rescue 0.743, but they leave Day's direction open.
6. **Additional check.** A simulation with age-dependent survival and a documented human population history (e.g., from a demographic reconstruction), reporting k per *year* against 2 mu T, in a model with overlap, to see whether a net k > mu factor of a few is attainable, as distinct from tens.

## C1 / C1a / C6: ascertainment and the "zero fixations"

1. **Day's strongest reading.** (C1a, Z18525185 §9.4, Q) "such bias would favor detecting recent fixations, not obscure them. The absence of recent fixations despite this bias strengthens our conclusion." The claim itself (C): no allele moved from below 50% (or below 10%) to fixation in seven millennia. The 21 post-7000 BP "first-passage" fixation events in time bins (C6), against 630 expected from k = mu, test whether new substitutions traverse the spectrum.
2. **Does the check test it?** C1a: yes in sign and magnitude, with the caveat of the audit's own design choices. C: yes for the neutral expectation. C6: **no** (binning not modelled).
3. **What it legitimately shows.** Under neutrality with Ne = 1e4, the expected number of events from a start below 50% is about zero, and from 50-90% is 0.04-0.11, because new-in-window mutations essentially cannot fix inside 280 generations (5e-151 per site). So the headline "0 from <50%" is the neutral prediction. **Day's test therefore does not discriminate.** That is a clear audit win, and it also fits Day's own §4.3 admission that the fixations are "completion events for alleles already near fixation".
4. **Where Day earns credit that the results understate.**
   - **C1a's sign is right for the bands Day's test uses.** The audit finds African-discovery designs enrich the 10-90% start bands (1.2-3.4x), the bands relevant to "from <50% / 50-90%", and suppress only the >=90% bands. Day's reply is about detecting fixations of the kind he counts, so it is correct where it applies. The suggested verdict "mixed: C1a right for 10-90% bands by factor 1-3, wrong for >=90%" is accurate but the >=90% suppression is irrelevant to Day's headline; it should say so. The effect on the headline is immaterial because both expectations are ~0, which the audit also says.
   - **The observed 1 and 3 events from 50-90% are, at Ne = 1e4, P(>=3 | 0.11) = 2e-4 anomalies**, in the direction of *more* change than neutral. They are attributed to admixture/error, which are not modelled. That attribution is an assumption; v66 total 3,469 is 3.8x *below* prediction and unexplained. The simulation does not explain the data it is set against.
   - **Ne sensitivity.** 50-90% completions equal expectation at Ne about 7,000 and reach 22 at Ne = 5,000. The tail is extremely sensitive to the Ne that Day calls circular (B3h/C5a). Ne = 1e4 is the audit's input (from parameters.yaml, "unverified"), so the audit is not entitled to call the observations a "mild anomaly" without the Ne caveat.
5. **Does C1 model Day's actual binning?** No. The model counts "newly 100% in the modern sample while not 100% in the Neolithic sample", Day's Zenodo definition (Q52, §2.4). Day's C6 counts *first-time-100% events in time bins* (22,428 alleles; 8,741 in 8000-10000 BP; 21 after 7000 BP) from a time series of unequal, small sample sizes. The audit says this itself ("Day's own definition (not modelled; early small bins register near-fixed alleles as 100% earlier)"). The gap is material: it is the number (21) the C6 critique and Day's "1,000x" claims rest on, and the suggested C6 verdict ("not like-for-like") leaves it unresolved. The fixation-rate comparators (630 vs the repo's own 5.2 vs the model's about 1,500) are all shown to be wrong; the comparison Day actually makes is not computed.
6. **Additional checks.** (i) Simulate the full time-series pipeline: sample sizes per bin from the AADR, first-passage to 100% per bin, neutral expectation with Ne in [5,000, 15,000], reporting the post-7000 BP count; (ii) include a two-source admixture model (the Neolithic replacement Day invokes) to see whether it explains the 8000-10000 BP burst and the 1/3 anomalies; (iii) repeat with the 1240k design as a union of the Human Origins and 610-Quad discovery panels rather than D1-D3.

## Where Day earns credit that the results understate (summary)

- **Post-split new-fixation deficit is exact** (B1c new-mutation column 0.84 = Day's (T - 4Ne)/T; B1 empty-start formula). Day's math is right; the dispute is the start state, which he concedes.
- **Pairwise divergence at Day's Ne = 1e4 is half the observed value.** The observed 1.23% needs Ne,anc of 1.3-2.0e5, or a longer T, or a higher mu. The B4a reconciliation is a three-parameter fit, CSAC's 14-22% polymorphism share is left unresolved, and Yoo's Ne is not rescaled to the pedigree mu used.
- **Sublinear response to mutation supply (A5d) and asexual interference are real and quantified**; KITTENS's linear scaling for the LTEE is rejected by A-sim.
- **B&L is a real effect; k != mu under overlap plus fluctuation is confirmed**, with the correct (upward) sign for realistic growth. Day's papers are inconsistent on sign.
- **d s is exact for hazard-scale selection.** The derivation is Day's, and the audit verifies it.
- **C1a's sign is right for the start bands Day tests.** The ascertainment bias does not hurt his headline; the headline is just not informative.
- **Haldane's limit is real in the regime of small M and a 10% budget** (T50 about 285 vs 300), and Keightley's U = 2.2 shows that hard selection is near its limit for realistic fecundity. No critic engaged Nunney.

## Where Day's side is simply wrong (not defended here)
