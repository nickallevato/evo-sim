## 1. Correctness
**Reproduction and arithmetic.** Output reproduces exactly. Kimura genic u(s,N) = (1-e^{-2s})/(1-e^{-4Ns}) is the right form for heterozygote effect s and N = N_e. Relative fixation 2N u(-s), cost 2s per fixation under Reading A, per-bin weighting by fixation probability: all correct. 84,035 = 1.2e-8 x 27,789,507.5 x 252,000 (checked). Both DFE rows sum to 1.000. Scoring table matches the raw band printout (DB1 hit, DB2 miss, DB3 miss, CB1-3 hit). Pre-registration was committed before the run and not edited; the DFE-share disclosure is stated in the docstring and the write-up.

**Probe 1: the s convention is resolvable from the saved text, and it is Reading A.**
- Boyko 2008 (the source of the first Table 3 row and of the method Kim reuses): "We assume s and 2 s are the selective disadvantage of heterozygotes and homozygous mutants and infer the DFE based on the scaled selection parameter, γ = 2 N e s" (Methods, saved text), and "our results should be interpreted as the distribution of heterozygote fitnesses".
- Kim 2017: "γ = 2 N A s, where N A is the ancestral population size" (Methods, Fit∂a∂i), "scaled by twice the ancestral population size", "like the method of Boyko et al. (2008), our method assumes additive selective effects and should be interpreted as averaging of selection over all heterozygotes and genetic backgrounds". Kim does not write "s is the heterozygote coefficient" in so many words (NOTE-1), but additive PRF with γ = 2N_A s, N_A diploid, is the Boyko parametrisation, and the Kim row for Boyko is a reanalysis of the same data.
- So s is the heterozygote coefficient, scaled to 2N_A (N_A diploids): Reading A. Reading B (|s| = homozygote effect) is not supported by either text. The ambiguity the write-up carries through the table, caveat 1 and "What it shows" is closed; the headline decline is 0.37-0.43 (N_e 1e4), not 0.37-0.43 vs "0.65-0.69" (those are fitness ratios, not declines, in the task framing; the write-up table itself is clear).
- **MAJOR-1 (resolvable ambiguity left open; Reading B rows should be dropped to a footnote; source misattribution of the N = 10,000 rescaling).** Caveat 1 says the full-text check is "open"; it was one grep. Separately, the write-up and docstring say Table 3 is "|s| rescaled to N = 10,000". The quoted sentence ("A population size of 10,000 diploids is used to rescale the distribution of 2Ns to s") is from the **Figure 1 legend and applies to Eyre-Walker et al. 2006 only**; the same legend says Boyko and Li are rescaled with 25,636 and 52,097. For Table 3 itself the Methods say s = γ/(2N_A) with N_A computed from θ_S = 4 N_A μ L_S "assuming the mutation rate to be μ = 1.8 x 10^-8" for the comparison rows (Boyko reanalysis: N = 7,778 diploids), and μ = 1.5e-8 for the other rows. Consequences: (a) the Table 3 s values are absolute, so running them at other N_e is not a double count: s stays fixed and only the fixation probability changes, which is what the script does; (b) but they are conditional on μ = 1.8e-8 and N_A about 8-10k, whereas the script uses μ = 1.2e-8; a DFE in γ-space re-expressed with a smaller μ (larger N_A) shifts mass toward smaller s, and the 1.5e-8 rows (0.237 / 0.127 / 0.192 / 0.266 / 0.178, same Table 3) have fewer strongly deleterious bins' weight than the rows used; (c) the N_e = 57,143 scenario applies a DFE whose s-scale was derived from a small N_A to a population defined by a different N_A, an inconsistency (a lineage with N_e 57k and the observed θ implies μ about 1.8e-8 x 8k/57k) that the write-up does not flag. Fix: replace "rescaled to N = 10,000" by the Methods statement (N_A from θ_S and μ = 1.8e-8; Boyko 7,778), resolve to Reading A, move B to one line, add the 1.5e-8 rows as a sensitivity.

**Probe 3: the neutral-equivalent base.**
- **MAJOR-2 (μ x T does not reproduce the observed divergence, so the absolute counts and the decline are tied to Day's T and a low μ, which the write-up presents next to an observation that implies about 2.8x more flux).** The write-up sets 84,035 beside "observed about 64,700". These are not on the same basis. Observed per-lineage synonymous differences (R4-L1 V0 ALL Sd = 141,853 / 2 = 70,926) against the neutral-expected 1.2e-8 x 8,311,550 x 252,000 = 25,134 gives a ratio of 2.82; the nonsynonymous counterpart is 64,740 observed against 84,035 x 0.21-0.27 predicted = 17.8k-21k (3.1-3.6x). The ratio dN/dS is scale-free, so CB1 is unaffected, but the headline counts (15-21k) and cost (0.37-0.43) scale with μ T and the write-up never says that the model's predicted total nonsynonymous divergence is 3x below the observed one. Part of the observed excess is ancestral polymorphism in the pS/pN (Nei-Gojobori, human-chimp, two lineages) and CpG hypermutability, so the full 2.8x is not the right factor; but the write-up should say that the pre-registered base is Day's T and a pedigree μ, report a divergence-calibrated sensitivity (scale μ T by 1.5x-2.8x), and note the consequence: 0.431 x 2.8 = 1.2, above ln 3 = 1.10 at the high end, so DB2 "Miss" is conditional on a base that undershoots observed divergence. This matters for the verdict wording "not a collapse" and for the HF-d replacement. The CB2 statement ("most of the nonsyn flux is purged") is also base-dependent.

## 2. Steelman-Day (is the check unfair to Day?)
- The check is fair in recording DB1 as a hit and crediting the premise. Where it under-credits: (i) it uses Day's own T = 252,000 and μ = 1.2e-8 but those under-predict observed divergence by about 2.8x (MAJOR-2); a Day-side reading calibrated to observed substitutions gets a coding decline of about 0.5-1.2 (A, N_e 1e4), at or above ln 3 in the 1000G row, which flips DB2. The write-up shows "Miss" only on the base where Day's own timescale is used against him. (ii) Kim's N_A about 8-10k is higher than the 7,778 of Boyko and an ancestral N_e below 1e4 (a bottleneck history) raises the decline; the write-up lists N_e 1e4 as the primary and 2e4 / 57k as lower-cost rows, but no N_e < 1e4 row, which is the direction most favourable to Day. (iii) Hard selection: 0.37-0.43 is a 31-35% fall in absolute fitness if selection is hard; the critic's "compensation and soft selection" is untested here, and the write-up says so in Who-this-helps but its headline "not a collapse" is only about ln 3, an arbitrary threshold from HF-d (R = 3). (iv) The noncoding target is 6x the coding one and the write-up's own bracket gives 0.38-2.26; "could equal or exceed" is fair, but nobody has ruled out Day's premise by it. A fair Day-side summary: premise supported on coding at the lower base, plausibly above ln 3 on a divergence-calibrated base plus any noncoding contribution; the check cannot settle that and says so only partly.
- The check also correctly shows that only about 100 fixations have |s| >= 1e-4; Day-side counter: the cost per fixation is tiny but the count is large, which the check already reflects in the cost. No further objection.

## 3. Steelman-critic (is the check too generous to Day?)
- The decline is dominated by the least-constrained part of the DFE. 93% of the cost and 99% of the count sit at |s| < 1e-4, |N_e s| below 1; for these, Kim says the DFE is less accurate, the within-bin shape is the audit's assumption, and such mutations are subject to linked selection, compensatory mutation and (at that size) near-certain reversion in a finite genome. A critic would say "decline 0.37" is an upper bound for a model that excludes every mechanism a real genome has, and the check labels it so (caveat 3) but the "What it shows 3" headline ("35% lower absolute fitness ... noncoding could equal or exceed it") reads as a finding. The noncoding bracket is clearly labelled unsourced in the Results section, caveat 6 and Who-this-helps; it is kept out of the scoring table and the verdict moves (good), but it re-enters "What it shows 3" and the Day paragraph as "could add as much again" with no sourcing; MINOR-8: move that sentence under the bracket and state "q is unmeasured" there, or cut.
- The DFE is estimated from polymorphism of extant humans under a demographic model and assumes additive effects and no dominance variation; applying it to fixation over 252,000 generations with a constant N_e is stationary-process extrapolation. Known critic point, listed in the write-up (caveat 3).
- The nearly-neutral class is exactly where the critic says Kimura 1983 already predicted: some drift fixation, and a population-genetic equilibrium (mutation-selection-drift balance with back and compensatory mutation) that returns fitness to a stationary level instead of a monotone decline. The check cannot test it and says so (caveat 3, Who-this-helps Critics). One more critic point not in the write-up: a monotone decline of 0.37 over 252k generations at constant N_e is not an equilibrium prediction; at equilibrium the deleterious fixation rate is balanced by beneficial/compensatory ones, so the absolute number of net "deleterious" substitutions is not the same as net fitness change (MINOR-9: add a sentence to caveat 3 or to Critics).
- The per-generation decline 3e-7 to 1.7e-6 is correctly framed as slow; the critic framing "3 orders below Day's rates" needs the per-claim correction in MINOR-2.
- The result is not circular for the critic: the DFE is fitted to polymorphism and the dN/dS prediction is not fitted to divergence (a real point for the critic), but see MINOR-4 on the band.

## Verdict
Arithmetic sound and reproducible; script unchanged since pre-registration; pre-registered bands scored as written; the noncoding bracket is clearly labelled and kept out of verdict moves except for the loose sentence in What it shows 3. No BLOCKER. Two MAJOR: (1) Reading A/B is resolvable from the saved text and the N = 10,000 rescaling claim is a misattribution (Figure 1 legend / Eyre-Walker; Table 3 uses N_A from θ_S and μ = 1.8e-8); (2) μ T does not reproduce observed divergence (about 2.8x on synonymous sites), so the fixation count and the cost are not calibrated to the observed lineage, and DB2 "Miss" depends on that base.

Recommended changes before integration: (a) resolve to Reading A with the Boyko and Kim quotes; drop B from the headline and fix the "rescaled to N = 10,000" wording; add the 1.5e-8 and ESP rows as sensitivity; (b) add a divergence-calibrated sensitivity (mu T x 1.5-2.8) and state that DB2 depends on it, and an N_e < 1e4 row; (c) headline decline range 0.29-0.43 (bin-0 shape), "31-35%", correct the "3 orders" claim; (d) mark the HF-d replacement as a proposal beyond the pre-registered mapping and "supplements", and keep GAP-05 / B9 / H5 / V20 moves as written; (e) soften "independently disfavours the large-N_e reading"; (f) move the noncoding "could equal or exceed" language under the bracket.
