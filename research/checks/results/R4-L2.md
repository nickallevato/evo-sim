# R4 L2: a sourced hardness h for humans (two routes)

Status: **written up 2026-10-13 (EVO-44); reviews pending** (load-bearing tier: correctness, steelman-day, steelman-critic).
Brief: `research/checks/briefs/L2-analyst.md`. Targets H, H5, H8 (all `load_bearing: true`). Thresholds: `R4-HF.md`.

**Result in one line.** Both routes are sourced and **disagree**: route (a), one population-regulation estimate
(Madsen, Robertson and Ye 2019, working paper), gives **h in [0.125, 0.256]**; route (b), ten Crow-index and
episode records from pre-transition populations, gives **h in [0.284, 0.641]**. The intervals do not overlap
(DISJOINT). The governing hull [0.125, 0.641] lands in **B-MH**, a band that **moves no verdict value** on H, H5 or
H8. No pair of readings (registered sensitivity) reaches B-HH or B-LL, the only two bands that move a verdict value.
The audit's prior (0.1 to 0.3, `QUEUE.md` L2) is met by route (a) and missed on the high side by route (b).

## Post hoc and descriptive (computed for this write-up beyond the printed outputs; govern nothing)

- **P1. Route (a) depends on the specification.** The registration admits "the one its authors call main or
  preferred". Column 2 is the full-period SUR estimate the authors lead with (L2Q-20); they call columns 3-7 checks
  that show "the results are robust to variations in estimation periods and country sample" (C1440 text after
  Table 3). Through the same formula and G in [25, 30], post hoc: column 4 (1600-1870, gamma -0.032) gives h in
  [0.040, 0.068]; columns 5 and 6 (-0.029) [0.130, 0.183]; column 7 (Italy, Netherlands, UK, 930-1870, -0.021)
  [0.370, 0.437]; column 3 (-0.034) gives 1 + gamma T < 0, no half-life (already noted in the screening log);
  OLS column 1 (-0.002; authors: "biased towards zero") about 0.94-0.95. The text also prints 40-year interval
  estimates: -0.024 (full period) gives [0.089, 0.134]; -0.023 (1600-1870) [0.150, 0.206]; -0.026 (1480-1600) no
  half-life; and 20-year estimates "range from -0.042 to -0.024": [0.064, 0.101] to [0.375, 0.442]. **Across the
  authors' own SUR robustness set, route (a) spans roughly 0.04 to 0.44**, which crosses both cut points. The
  registered primary (column 2) is narrow only because its standard error is small.
- **P2. DISJOINT rests on a gap of 0.028 and on the R-E2 line.** Santa Rosa (same source and survey as Chañarcito and
  Olacapato) gives 0.205; it was set E2 false because its women's mean live births are 5.79 against the run-time
  threshold of 6 (Chañarcito 6.44, Olacapato 6.87; none stated to be completed families, L2Q-16). Admitting it would
  make route (b) [0.205, 0.641], overlapping route (a); DISJOINT would be False, PA-3 still a hit, the band still
  B-MH. Other E2-false records below 0.284 whose transition status is arguable: Koraga 0.223 (3.97 births), Kodava
  0.256 (4.54), Amma Kodava 0.185 ("transitional" per source), South Altai 0.256, Yakut 0.215, Koscierzyna 0.282.
  Admitting Amma Kodava or Aguada Guzmán (0.174; the source reports fertility control, so false is well grounded)
  would turn PA-3 into a miss. **No E2 decision can move the band**: below 0.1, only post-transition records
  (Lucknow 0.040-0.098, Ingush 0.035, Zapotec 2000 0.070) and the excluded C0142 lie there.
- **P3. Courtiol 2013 (C0142, rural Gambia) was excluded on E3/stat grounds**: it reports annual-fitness variances
  (child survival 5.20e-2, fertility 5.57), not lifetime episode opportunities. The run task flagged that admitting
  them as episodes "would give h about 0.01". Had it been admitted, route (b) would reach about 0.01 and the band would
  be B-LH (still no move). The exclusion follows the registered stat list; the reviews should rule on it.
- **P4. Weak forms by route.** PD-w (lo >= 0.1) holds on each route separately (route (a) 0.125, route (b) 0.284).
  PC-w (hi < 0.3) holds on route (a) alone and fails on route (b), where 8 of 10 eligible records are >= 0.3.
- **P5. Slovincian g1 (the route (b) maximum).** The source prints Ps 0.66 while 1 - Pd = 0.69, and its total index is
  printed as Im + If (L2Q-08 note). With Ps = 0.69, h = 0.651 instead of 0.641; the record rests on N = 32 women.
- **P6. Route (a) point value.** gamma = -0.028 through the source's formula gives a half-life of 11.3 years
  (printed: 11). At T = 11, h = 0.151 (G = 30) to 0.207 (G = 25). The point estimate is not used by the mapping.

## Who this helps

**Day side**
- **PD-w hit.** On the sourced data no eligible pre-transition record, on either route, puts human h below 0.1. The
  route (a) lower end is 0.125 (0.176 at G = 25) and the route (b) lower end 0.284 (P4). In the model, a cost exists
  at every h > 0 (H5 note), so the critic-side premise that the cost of selection does not apply to humans because
  selection is soft (GG-26, "only applicable for hard selection models") is not borne out as an all-or-nothing claim:
  on the primary readings at least an eighth of selection is hard.
- **Route (b) is mostly above 0.3.** 8 of 10 pre-transition Crow and episode records give a viability share of 0.3
  or more (up to 0.641). Where pre-reproductive deaths are taken as hard (M-b1), Term 3 holds within R1 in-model at
  that end (H8 placement), and that matches Nunney's "hard selection will dominate the adaptive process" for the
  viability channel.
- The most likely bias in route (a) runs Day's way (Open 3): a dynamic panel with country fixed effects tends to
  overstate convergence speed, which would understate h.

**Critic side**
- **PD miss.** B-HH is not reached and cannot be reached under any reading pair. The one population-regulation
  estimate says a deviation in population is about 84% compensated within 30 years (1 + gamma T = 0.16), so route (a)
  lies wholly below 0.3 and the sentence "the soft-selection escape does not apply" cannot be written for humans.
  H8 stays contested; it is not supported.
- **Readings that favour softness move the band down, never up.** A1 x M-b0 and every A-down pair give B-LH; modern
  records give B-LH. Route (a)'s robustness columns reach 0.040-0.068 (P1, column 4) and the 20- and 40-year estimates
  reach below 0.1. The excluded Gambia data would give about 0.01 (P3). The Utah late cohorts give 1/7 (0.143) as the
  transition proceeds.
- Route (b)'s mapping counts every pre-reproductive death as hard (M-b1). The registration itself names the
  alternative (M-b0: replacement births, positive check density-dependent), and route (a) is direct evidence that
  population losses are largely compensated within a generation.

**Both / the audit's own errors**
- **PA-4 missed**: the audit expected route (a) to straddle 0.3; it lies wholly below.
- **PA-2's hit is vacuous**: Byars 2010 was not retrieved, so the E3 expectation (adult-only sample) was not tested.
- **Neither named design was used.** The brief named Stearns 2010 and Byars 2010 for route (b); both were unavailable
  and the route rests on Crow's index, a variance share, not a decomposition of selection on traits. All seven S1
  candidates were unavailable; 66 ids are on the owner list.
- **Route (a) is one record**, a working paper, whose published version (C1433) was not fetched; its narrow
  interval is the standard error of one column, not the spread of the authors' own specifications (P1).
- **Run-time rules after some values were seen** (R-E2 and four others), disclosed in the screening-log header. R-E2's
  threshold of 6 births decides the DISJOINT flag (P2).
- **The registered "no uncertainty" flag is not printed.** The docstring says a point value is "flagged 'no
  uncertainty'"; `report()` prints only the clipping flags, so every route (b) row shows "-". The inputs file carries
  the flag in an `uncertainty` field, so nothing is lost, but the printed table understates how thin route (b) is.
- **The audit's prior (0.1 to 0.3) was too low for route (b)** and too wide for route (a) (column 2).
- h has no field estimator (R4-HF, Critic-4). Both mappings are the audit's own, fixed before the numbers; they are
  where this result is decided, and the reviews should test them, not only the arithmetic.

## Things that looked wrong (for the reviews to rule on)

- **W1. Utah episodes record (b-utah-early).** Moorad's "proportional contributions of age-specific survival" cover
  survival at all ages, not only pre-reproductive, in a data set of "females known to have reproduced" weighted for
  nulliparity (L2Q-19). It was entered with I_mate = 0, so its M-b2 reading is a degenerate [1.000, 1.000]. It does
  not set either end of the route (b) hull, so the band is unaffected.
- **W2. Albeza 2002 E2 threshold.** The 6-birth line is applied to women's mean live births not stated to be
  completed families (L2Q-16), so 5.79 vs 6.44 may partly reflect age structure. This decides Santa Rosa and the
  DISJOINT flag (P2).
- **W3. Route (a) "preferred" specification.** Column 2 is the authors' lead estimate, but they do not call it
  preferred over columns 4-7; "preferred SUR" is the run task's wording. Under the registration's fallback ("if none is
  designated, all of them (union)"), the SUR columns with a defined half-life give route (a) about [0.040, 0.437] at
  G in [25, 30] (P1; adding OLS column 1 would widen it to about 0.95); the governing hull would be [0.040, 0.641],
  band B-LH, PC-w still a miss, PD-w a miss, PA-4 a hit, DISJOINT False, and still no value move. A reviewer should
  rule which reading of the eligibility paragraph's specification clause applies.
- **W4. Slovincian Ps inconsistency** (P5), and that source's non-standard total (I = Im + If). Small effect (0.641 vs
  0.651) but it is the route (b) maximum.
- **W5. The `report()` omission** of the registered "no uncertainty" flag (above).
- **W6. Mapping scale.** The model's h is a share of the log-load; M-b1 uses a share of Crow's variance index. The
  registration accepts this as its stated mapping; the critic-side reviewer may argue it is not the same quantity.
