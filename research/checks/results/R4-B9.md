## Readings
- **A (charitable; "rendered infertile" = mutation load).** Selection is fully relaxed (premise 1 taken as the state of the world), and new deleterious mutations accumulate. The model is W_t = (1 - d)^t, with d = U x f x s per generation and 25 y per generation.
  - Lynch's inputs: "∼100×0.01×1%=1% per generation"; "as high as 5% per generation"; for mice, "Extrapolating by dividing by 6.7 suggests a human decline rate of ∼0.5%"; for Drosophila, "0.2–2% decline in fitness per generation"; deleterious class "1–10% of all human mutations", with effects "on the order of 1% per mutation".
  - Day's premise 2 sets f = 0.75 (3:1).
- **B (literal "substitutions").** Harmful mutations that behave neutrally take ~4Ne generations to fix (Kimura & Ohta 1969: "takes about 4Ne generations until fixation"). Ne is 1.0e4 (Takahata 1995) or 1.31-1.62e4 (Prado-Martinez 2013), from `parameters.yaml`.

## Who this helps
- **Day side.** Taken charitably, "within centuries" is the right order for fully relaxed selection on Lynch's upper value (338 y to halve) and on Day's own premise 2 (75 of ~77-100 new mutations harmful; s assumed at 0.1-1%, giving ~12-290 y to halve). On Lynch's central and mouse values it is millennia (1,724-11,484 y), stated plainly here. The worry behind it, deterioration under relaxed selection, is mainstream: Lynch 2016 projects about a 1% fitness decline per generation, up to 5%. His best remaining point is GAP-05: slightly deleterious mutations do drift to fixation, and no critic has quantified how many.
- **Critic side.**
  - Premise 1 is false. Drift on neutral sites and purging of harmful mutations run at the same time, so the conditional's antecedent never obtains, and the speed of a hypothetical collapse is moot.
  - Premise 2 conflates mutations with substitutions. "3x more harmful" may describe new mutations; it cannot describe substitutions, because purging removes most harmful mutations before they fix.
  - R4-L1's human-chimp dN/dS of ~0.21-0.27 implies that roughly three quarters or more of nonsynonymous changes were purged. That is direct evidence that selection operated alongside the substitutions that did fix.
  - Day's 3:1 ratio is also far from Lynch's 1-10% deleterious share. On Lynch's numbers the timescale is millennia, not centuries.
- **Both.** Reading B (literal fixation of harmful mutations) gives ~1e6 y. That is the source of the earlier non-sequitur verdict, and it is about 1,000x "centuries". The verdict turns on rule C choosing reading A.

## Caveats
- The model assumes multiplicative, independent effects and no epistasis. Synergistic epistasis would shorten the times; dominance and the slow approach to homozygosity would lengthen them.
- The W = 0.5 and W = 0.1 thresholds are arbitrary stand-ins for "infertile". Extinction depends on absolute fertility, and the model ignores the reproductive-excess reserve (W is relative to an ancestor). Neither threshold is a demographic extinction model.
- Lynch says his estimate is indirect: "Owing to the indirect way in which this estimate of the recurrent human mutation load has been obtained, skepticism is to be expected".
- At d of 30% or more (Day's premise 2 with s >= 0.5%), the per-generation compounding is crude, with fewer than 5 generations to threshold.
- Reading B uses the mean conditional fixation time. It is not a steady-state rate, since under neutrality fixations arrive at k = mu once the pipeline is full.
