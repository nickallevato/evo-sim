Questions asked: does the check test "protein-guided alignment" as practised, or a stand-in that could not have disagreed with its own input? "Not compared" means CDS length / 3, less one for the terminal stop, minus the codons U0 counts ((S + N) / 3): 130,047 codons in the 19,310 genes (1.17%), which agrees with L1b's 129,092 (1.16%) to within the fractional site counts. They are offered for the fix pass to check.

Overall. The write-up is candid about much of this: it says the pipeline is not his, shows the registered `supported`, gives him the transfer hit and lists the aligner's limits. What his defender would still press is one structural point with four faces. **Every arm starts from the codons Cactus paired.** The protein arms re-pair that material; the synthetic table hands every arm a perfect pairing; nothing in the check aligns a chimp coding sequence that Cactus did not already place. So the check measures what a second aligner does after a first one has chosen the material, and Q186 names the first kind of step as much as the second: "It is a methodological artifact of using a conservation-dependent alignment to measure conservation."

What this side concedes at the start, so that the findings are read at their true size: nothing below recovers 0.80. For the codons outside every arm to lift the pooled value to 0.80 they would need a pN of about 0.67 (reviewer; 0.60 before the terminal stops are taken out, as in correctness review note 9), which is unrelated sequence. The findings bear on the words "cannot", "contradicted", "realistic" and "stress", on the band margin, and on what Reading b may be said to rest on.

## Findings

### 1. MAJOR. The protein-guided arms are conditional on Cactus, so their agreement with U0 is near automatic; CRITIC licenses a narrower sentence than the write-up uses
- Evidence, docstring amendment 1: PA "builds the chimp protein in the Cactus codon frame (cactus_frame): clean codons as they are, in-frame indel runs translated as the chimp has them, frameshift runs as X (not compared), uncovered codons omitted; then the same Gotoh alignment." The chimp "protein" given to the aligner is the Cactus pairing with the gaps closed. Where Cactus has no indel and no hole the two inputs are the same string in the same register, and a global aligner with a gap cost of 10 + k returns the diagonal.
- Evidence, data: in the 16,545 indel-free genes PA differs from U0 in 3 genes (correctness review, checks that pass). So the critic-side hits "PA within ±0.01 of U0; agreement >= 99.5%" (0.006; 99.95%) could hardly have failed on 85.7% of genes. The pre-run review said this of the registered P (MAJOR-5: "arm P cannot show his mechanism by construction"); the resolution added trimming rules on the same path, which inherit the same input.
- A protein-guided pipeline as practised starts from each species' own annotated CDS, translates, aligns and back-translates (the docstring's own verification note: PAL2NAL takes "a protein alignment plus the corresponding DNA (CDS) sequences", so "a per-species in-frame CDS is its starting point"). No chimp annotation is used here (caveat 5). The check therefore never runs the method Q180 names; it runs a re-pairing of a genome alignment's output. The registered arms that did start from an ungapped chimp string were invalid, and what replaced them is closer to U0 by design.
- What CRITIC licenses under that limit: "on the codons Cactus pairs, re-pairing with an in-script BLOSUM62 Gotoh aligner and five trimming rules changes the pooled value by 2% to 6%." It does not license Interpretation 1 ("on these data the protein-guided step cannot be what produces it") as a statement about protein-guided pipelines, nor the verdict-table phrase "now also on data" without the same three qualifiers.
- Fix: (a) mark the two PA agreement predictions "near automatic by construction on indel-free genes", as the write-up already does for A5's second clause; (b) reword Interpretation 1 and 2 and the DN6 row to carry "on the Cactus-paired codons, with the audit's in-script aligner"; (c) change the queue item from "not needed for the verdict" (docstring) to a stated condition: no strengthening of DN6 Reading b beyond `contested` until one external codon-aware pipeline has been run from an independent chimp CDS annotation on at least the 2,765 genes with indels or holes.

### 2. MAJOR. The input is itself a conservation-dependent alignment, the synthetic table has no such step, and the reading under which that matters is not in the Readings table (rule RD)
- The quote. Q181: "Regions too divergent to score well get gapped out or are left unaligned, so they aren't counted." Q186: "a conservation-dependent alignment". Neither sentence is limited to protein scoring. A nucleotide genome aligner also leaves low-scoring regions unaligned. DN6 has Readings a, b and c; none is "the genome alignment that feeds U0 is such a step". RD: "Record every plausible reading with its verdict." This is a reading of the quoted words, not an extension to a different object.
- Size of what no arm sees (reviewer): about 156,600 of 11,149,017 annotated sense codons (1.40%), of which 130,047 lie in the 19,310 genes and the rest in the 97 genes with no U0 codon. The write-up's "about 1.6%" (and the correctness review's 1.58%) counts some 19,400 terminal stop codons as left out; the needed pN moves from 0.60 to 0.67 when they are removed. The left-out codons are concentrated: 351 genes with 20% or more of their codons not compared hold 78,954 of them (61% of the in-gene total).
- The missing material sits in fast genes, and by the audit's own discriminator those genes are not noisy (reviewer; U0, genes binned by the share of their codons not compared; second line restricted to the dS-clean set DSC):

| under 1% (13,307 genes with none: 0.209) | 17,874 | 0.223 | 0.0158 | 0.430 | 17,413 | 0.206 | 0.0146 |
| 1% to 5% | 549 | 0.401 | 0.0280 | 0.577 | 443 | 0.309 | 0.0182 |

  On dS-clean genes the paired codons read 0.21, 0.31, 0.42, 0.47 as more of the gene goes missing, at a pS between 0.015 and 0.022, all under the audit's own 0.023 gate. Amendment 4 says what that pattern is: "a fast-evolving protein has ordinary pS and high pN". So the more Cactus leaves out of a gene, the faster the part it kept is evolving, at ordinary synonymous divergence. That is the direction Q182 states, seen at the gene level on the input alignment, before any protein arm.
- What the left-out codons would need (reviewer; they are given U0's N:S split; pS as stated):

  If the left-out codons merely read like the paired codons of the 351 worst-covered genes (pN 0.053, pS 0.082), U0 ALL becomes 0.294 at kappa 1 and about 0.41 on the kappa-5 line (0.294 times the write-up's factor 1.40; approximate). So 0.80 is out of reach, and this side says so; the kappa-hat margin of 0.015 is not.
- The transfer table cannot speak to this. Its generator hands U0 the true pairing; no synthetic sequence passes through a nucleotide genome aligner, and no cell has a hole. "In that cell U0 itself returns the high truth (0.68)" is true by construction, and the argument built on it ("the combination Day's reading needs ... is not what the real run looks like": U0 reads 0.271) assumes the real U0 is as complete as the synthetic one.
- Fix: (a) add a Reading d to DN6 ("the genome alignment is the conservation-dependent step") with the two tables above and its bounded result: cannot carry 0.80 or 0.65; not excluded for a rise of the order of 10% (0.271 to 0.29) or for crossing 0.40 on transition-weighted sites; (b) move the last Day bullet of "Who this helps" (the 1.6% bound) into Results with the gradient table; (c) state in the transfer section that U0 is exact by construction there; (d) queue: pairwise alignment of the 351 genes from an independent chimp annotation.

### 3. MAJOR. "Realistic" and "stress" are the audit's labels, with nothing run between them and no arm tied to a named pipeline's rule
- The rules table printed by the run (`raw/l1c.out` l.330) shows how far apart the two trims are. For one replacement between unchanged neighbours, PF1 / PAF1 drops the centre only "if_score_below" -11 (neighbours with self-score 4) or lower. BLOSUM62 has no such score (the script's `_B62` table; the standard matrix minimum is -4), so the lenient rule can never remove an isolated replacement; it needs a run of bad positions. PF2 / PAF2 drops the centre and both neighbours when the replacement scores below 1, -1 or -3 for neighbours with self-scores 4, 5 or 6. One rule cannot touch a single radical replacement; the other removes any replacement scoring 0 or less beside the commonest residues, with its neighbours. The check reports those two points and calls one realistic and the other adversarial. Where a real divergence filter falls between them is not measured.
- PAF2 is not a noise remover on clean genes (reviewer, extending correctness finding 4): on DSC it lowers the count of nonsynonymous differences by 26.8% (net of codons gained) with 0.54% fewer codons, and its dropped codons have x = 2.0; on HQs 24.8% with 0.47%, x = 1.6; on the 12,724 genes of the real list 33.2% with 0.60% (x = 5.8 there, the list not being dS-filtered). On dS-clean sets it meets all three conditions of the amended Reading A (`small` or larger flag, nonsynonymous-enriched, x <= 3). The `contested` then rests on the label alone, and the label was set after the selftest miniature had shown the arm reading low (caveat 4).
- Which published pipeline each arm stands for: PGB follows the Gblocks documentation for two sequences, not validated against the binary; PL38 / PL16 are "this audit's rule, not a named pipeline's", with X-drop values "from memory, NOT verified"; PAF1 and PAF2 are the audit's windows; PA is a global alignment on the Cactus frame. So one arm of seven is anchored to a document, none to a pipeline of the literature Day criticises, and the amended docstring withdrew its own sentence that PF2 bounds "any plausible threshold". Pipelines that add identity thresholds, stop-codon filters and length filters remove whole genes or segments, a step this check has only at the gene-set level.
- Fix: (a) a labelled post hoc dose-response on the PA path (windows 3 and 5, mean-score thresholds from 1 to 3 in steps), reporting drop, share of Nd removed and x on ALL, DSC and CSAC23, so that the reader sees where between -5.8% and -34.8% a given rule lands; (b) a table "arm, rule, documented source or none"; (c) until then write "stress (audit's label)" and carry both Reading A values into the DN6 Readings table, with the fact that on dS-clean genes PAF2's dropped codons are dS-normal.

### 4. MAJOR. The real genome is not shown to be outside the clustering regime in the genes where the mechanism can act; the indel-free bound is drawn on genes selected for conservation
- The correctness review (finding 2) bounds the regime on the 16,545 indel-free genes. That subset is chosen on an outcome: genes with no length difference and no hole. It reads 0.2045 on U0; the other 2,765 genes read 0.4247 and hold 47.5% of all nonsynonymous differences (reviewer). A bound on dense replacement runs drawn from the genes least likely to have them does not transfer to the genes most likely to. Fast-evolving regions are where indels are (the correctness review's own open point).
- In those 2,765 genes the realistic arms are not negligible (reviewer): U0 0.4247; PA 0.4043 (-4.8%), PL38 0.4007 (-5.7%), PGB 0.3920 (-7.7%), PAF1 0.3801 (-10.5%). The lenient trim passes the 10% relative line there. And the one observable that was to say whether what it removes is misalignment or dense real replacement, x, is shown by correctness finding 1 not to separate the two (x = 9.6 in the saturated synthetic cell, 7.6 to 15.2 on real data).
- So the accurate statement is two-sided: on 85.7% of genes the mechanism has nothing to act on; on the 14.3% that hold nearly half the nonsynonymous differences it removes 5% to 10%, and the check cannot say whether that is error removed or signal removed. The write-up's "nothing in it fixes the regime on its own" is right for this second group and should stay for it.
- Fix: when the correctness fix is applied, keep the split in both halves, give the 2,765-gene row beside the indel-free row, and word the bound as "in genes with no length difference". Do not let Interpretation 3 ("looks like misalignment") stand as the reading of the 2,765 genes until the queued statistic (replacement density per window against chance) has been run on them.

### 8. MINOR. Fidelity: the CSAC text refutes the label "protein-guided", not the wider words of Q186
- Held text (`quotes-literature.md`, CSAC2005): the 16,045 genes "were indirectly aligned to the chimpanzee sequence via the genome alignment"; the 13,454 came "After removing low-quality sequences and likely alignment artefacts"; they are genes "for which it was possible to generate high-quality sequence alignments covering virtually the entire coding region". The first sentence contradicts "protein-guided" (Q180). The other two describe selection on alignment quality, which is what Q186 calls "a conservation-dependent alignment" if that phrase is read to include the artefact filter. The criteria are "in the SI, not held" (docstring).
- The held quote for the 7,043-gene subset says "realigned using Clustal W"; it does not say whether proteins or nucleotides were aligned. The docstring's "protein-aware realignment (Clustal W)" has no quote under it.
- Fix: two fidelity readings in DN6. Strict (a protein-guided realignment produced the 0.23): `misread`, as now. Broad (an alignment-quality selection step stands behind the 0.23): `partial` or `unverifiable` until the SI criteria are held; the real list does read lower than all genes here (0.219 against 0.271; NOT23 0.344). Remove "protein-aware" from the Clustal W gloss unless a quote supports it.
