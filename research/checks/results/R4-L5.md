## Result in brief

1. **At Day's required rate (15,556 completions in the window, taken at face value, no panel scaling) the expected count of his zero-reported statistics is effectively 0 for every s <= 0.02**, in both modes (at most 1e-6 in any 25-y cell; at most 0.044 in the 20-y cells; the second panel design, D3, is a partial sensitivity only, section 11 item 3). This includes s = 1e-3, the beneficial s Day uses in another post (Q44; the post that carries the comparator states no s): median 0 in both modes. No simulated sweep registers at s <= 0.01: the largest printed value is 5.9e-16 (threshold for the critic band: 1). That figure is not a margin. The simulation resolves 24 to 184 events per cell at 95%; **E <= 1 at s <= 0.01 rests on sweep-time arithmetic** (section 4), corroborated post hoc by a direct trajectory bound.
2. **At s >= 0.05 the same simulated panel registers hundreds to thousands of events** (median 520 and 3,924 for new-mutation sweeps; 286 and 1,237 for standing-variant sweeps). The positive control passes: the model can see a sweep. **At s >= 0.05 Day's zero excludes his required rate**, and so excludes any rescue of that rate by strong sweeps (P(0) below 1e-69; his non-zero statistic M10 expects 32 to 408 at s = 0.03 against his 1 and 3). No critic claim in the corpus invokes such a rescue, and no source of Day's gives the 15,556 an s that high (section 9, R-d). Magnitudes at s >= 0.05 treat 15,556 sweeps as independent, which they could not be (section 11 item 7).
3. **s = 0.03 is the edge.** Median 0.099 (new mutations) and 3.08 (standing); at Day's 20-year generation 9.9 and 54. The crossover s\* (smallest grid s with median E >= 3; new mutations / standing) depends on the statistic: **B10 (blog, Q55) 0.05 / 0.03; Z50 (paper, Q53) 0.05 / 0.05; Z10 (paper, Q53) 0.1 / 0.1**. The band does not depend on that choice (section 4).
4. **The mainstream model predicts about zero** (at most 4.7e-31 at 25 y; 4.75e-18 at 100 times the rate and 20 y).
5. **Why the power is zero at low s: the window, not the panel.** No simulated window completion at s <= 0.02 started below 50% at 7,500 BP (0 of 3,007 to 6,380 pooled per row). The panel does not hide strong sweeps: linked panel SNPs carry them (new mutations), and for standing-variant sweeps the swept site itself is on the panel in 8.8% to 24.7% of completions. Two registered predictions about ascertainment (P4, P5) missed in that direction. This bears on C1 and corrects two figures in the audit's own claim text (section 6). It does not test the sign Day asserts in C1a (section 6; an earlier version of this write-up said it did).
6. Of the eight registered predictions, **two hit in full (P2, P8), six missed in part or in whole** (P1, P3, P4, P5, P6, P7). The band clause of P7 was expected to miss (pre-run note); the others were not.

Values such as 1e-47 are not estimates of a rate and not margins. Only the modern side of each probability is exact; the Neolithic side is 32 Monte Carlo draws per SNP (`R_MC`), and SNPs whose modern-side probabilities are all below 1e-7 are skipped (`live`). A printed 0 means "below those cuts"; a printed 1e-47 is a modern-sample probability, such as 0.92^1,315, times a coarse Neolithic fraction. They show how far the nearest simulated sweep is from registering. Read them as "no simulated sweep registers".

**What "E <= 1 at s <= 0.01" rests on.** Each cell simulated 253 to 3,008 window completions against F = 15,556, so one fully registering sweep would add F / n_wc = 8 to 61 to a cell's E, and by the rule of three the simulation alone resolves **24 to 184 events per cell** at 95% (s <= 0.01, 25 y; 25 to 117 in the 20-y cells). Monte Carlo alone therefore cannot show E <= 1 in any cell.
