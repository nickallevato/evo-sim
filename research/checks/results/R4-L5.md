## Result in brief

1. **At the required rate Day attributes to the Modern Synthesis (Q275: "The Modern Synthesis requires ..."; 15,556 completions in the window, taken at face value, no panel scaling; "the required rate" from here on; it is not a rate Day's own model predicts) the expected count of his zero-reported statistics is effectively 0 for every s <= 0.02**, in both modes (at most 1e-6 in any 25-y cell; at most 0.044 in the 20-y cells; the second panel design, D3, is a partial sensitivity only, section 11 item 3). This includes s = 1e-3, the beneficial s Day uses in another post (Q44; the post that carries the comparator states no s): median 0 in both modes. No simulated sweep registers at s <= 0.01: the largest printed value is 5.9e-16 (threshold for the critic band: 1). That figure is not a margin. The simulation resolves 24 to 184 events per cell at 95%; **E <= 1 at s <= 0.01 rests on sweep-time arithmetic** (section 4), corroborated post hoc by a direct trajectory bound.
2. **At s >= 0.05 the same simulated panel registers hundreds to thousands of events** (median 520 and 3,924 for new-mutation sweeps; 286 and 1,237 for standing-variant sweeps). The positive control passes: the model can see a sweep. **At s >= 0.05 Day's zero excludes his required rate**, and so excludes any rescue of that rate by strong sweeps (P(0) below 1e-69; his non-zero statistic M10 expects 32 to 408 at s = 0.03 against his 1 and 3). No critic claim in the corpus invokes such a rescue, and no source of Day's gives the 15,556 an s that high (section 9, R-d). Magnitudes at s >= 0.05 treat 15,556 sweeps as independent, which they could not be (section 11 item 7).
3. **s = 0.03 is the edge for the zero-reported statistics.** Median 0.099 (new mutations) and 3.08 (standing); at 20 y (Day 2019; his current value is 25 y) 9.9 and 54. The crossover s\* (smallest grid s with median E >= 3; new mutations / standing) depends on the statistic and is a grid **ceiling**: **B10 (blog, Q55) s\* <= 0.05 (between 0.03 and 0.05 by the log-odds arithmetic, about 0.031) / 0.03 to 0.05; Z50 (paper, Q53) <= 0.05 / <= 0.05; Z10 (paper, Q53) <= 0.1 / <= 0.1**; the grid and the V3 understatement both push the printed values up, against Day. The band does not depend on that choice (section 4). **Day's non-zero statistic M10 crosses one grid step lower** (post hoc, descriptive; section 7): on the validated design v62 and the reference cell (const 1e4) it excludes the required rate at s = 0.02 standing and 0.03 hard at 25 y, and at s = 0.01 standing and 0.02 hard at 20 y.
4. **The mainstream model predicts about zero** (at most 4.7e-31 at 25 y; 4.75e-18 at 100 times the rate and 20 y; both under the ASSUMED mapping of Uricchio's classes to s = 1e-3 / 1e-2, so the x100 figure is not a robustness result: section 9, R-e row).
5. **Why the power is zero at low s: the window, not the panel.** No simulated window completion at s <= 0.02 started below 50% at 7,500 BP (0 of 3,007 to 6,380 pooled per row). The panel does not hide strong sweeps: linked panel SNPs carry them (new mutations), and for standing-variant sweeps the swept site itself is on the panel in 8.8% to 24.7% of completions. Two registered predictions about ascertainment (P4, P5) missed in that direction. This bears on C1 and corrects two figures in the audit's own claim text (section 6). Values such as 1e-47 are not estimates of a rate and not margins. Only the modern side of each probability is exact; the Neolithic side is 32 Monte Carlo draws per SNP (`R_MC`), and SNPs whose modern-side probabilities are all below 1e-7 are skipped (`live`). A printed 0 means "below those cuts"; a printed 1e-47 is a modern-sample probability, such as 0.92^1,315, times a coarse Neolithic fraction. They show how far the nearest simulated sweep is from registering. Read them as "no simulated sweep registers".

**What "E <= 1 at s <= 0.01" rests on.** Each cell simulated 253 to 3,008 window completions against F = 15,556, so one fully registering sweep would add F / n_wc = 8 to 61 to a cell's E, and by the rule of three the simulation alone resolves **24 to 184 events per cell** at 95% (s <= 0.01, 25 y; 25 to 117 in the 20-y cells). Monte Carlo alone therefore cannot show E <= 1 in any cell.
