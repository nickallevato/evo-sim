# R4 L5: ancient-DNA zero-fixation power analysis (how many of Day's "fixations" would a selection-driven model show on the 1240k panel, at his sample sizes, in his window?)

**Status: run, written up, NOT yet reviewed, not integrated.** Band by the script's registered rule: **CRITIC**. The verdict moves in section 8 are proposals for the reviews; no claim file has been touched. Everything here is dialectic (rule RH): a model-conditional simulation, not a measurement of the real past.

Targets: claims C, C1, C1a (C5 and C6 noted). Quotes: Q53 "Across 1.14 million autosomal loci and 7,000 years of European prehistory, zero alleles moved from below 50% starting frequency to regional fixation, and zero moved from below 10% to fixation, in either run." (Z23046531, p.6, 2026-09-29); Q55 "The observed fixation count was zero. Not a single allele in 1.2 million crossed from rare (<10% frequency) to fixed (>90% frequency) in seven thousand years." (blog 2026-01-14, para 5); C1a "such bias would favor detecting recent fixations, not obscure them" (Z18525185, s9.4, 2026-02-08).

## Result in brief

1. **At Day's required rate (15,556 completions in the window, taken at face value, no panel scaling) the expected count of his zero-reported statistics is effectively 0 for every s <= 0.02**, in both modes and both panel designs (at most 1e-6 in any 25-y cell; at most 0.044 in the 20-y cells). This includes Day's own beneficial s = 1e-3 (median 0 in both modes). The largest value anywhere at s <= 0.01 is 5.9e-16 (threshold for the critic band: 1).
2. **At s >= 0.05 the same simulated panel registers hundreds to thousands of events** (median 520 and 3,924 for new-mutation sweeps; 286 and 1,237 for standing-variant sweeps). The positive control passes: the model can see a sweep, and at strong selection Day's zero would exclude his required rate.
3. **s = 0.03 is the edge.** Median 0.099 (new mutations) and 3.08 (standing); at Day's 20-year generation 9.9 and 54.
4. **The mainstream model predicts about zero** (at most 4.7e-31 at 25 y; 4.7e-18 at 100 times the rate and 20 y).
5. **Why the power is zero at low s: the window, not the panel.** No simulated window completion at s <= 0.02 started below 50% at 7,500 BP (0 of 3,007 to 6,380 pooled per row). The panel does not hide strong sweeps: linked panel SNPs carry them (new mutations), and for standing-variant sweeps the swept site itself is on the panel 240 to 670 times more often than a random site. Two registered predictions about ascertainment (P4, P5) missed in that direction. This bears on C1 and C1a and is on Day's side (sections 6, 9).
6. Of the eight registered predictions, **two hit in full (P2, P8), six missed in part or in whole** (P1, P3, P4, P5, P6, P7). The band clause of P7 was expected to miss (pre-run note); the others were not.

Reading. For post-split European new mutations at s >= 0.02 the swept site is never on the panel: the mechanical exclusion of C1 point 1 is exact in the model. It does not hide the sweep, because linked panel SNPs supply hundreds to thousands of events at s >= 0.05. For standing-variant sweeps the swept site is an old variant, common in Africa, and the panel is strongly enriched for it; at s = 0.03 the focal site supplies nearly every event.

So within this model the panel's ascertainment raises the visibility of selection-driven completions in three of the four regimes and leaves it to hitchhikers in the fourth. The sign Day asserts in C1a ("such bias would favor detecting recent fixations, not obscure them") is what the run shows for standing-variant sweeps, though by a different route than the one he gives (he supposes enrichment for sites polymorphic in modern Europeans; the model's panel is discovered in one African individual). The audit predicted the opposite in P4 (standing) and partly in P5, and missed.

Beside these, as the pre-run note requires:

1. **CRITIC was the arithmetically expected band.** P2 and P3a were registered with their arithmetic (about 1,000 generations for a sweep at s <= 0.01 against a 280-generation window), and together they are the CRITIC condition. A CRITIC outcome is therefore a **weak test** of the move on C external. What the run adds beyond the arithmetic is the size of the margin, the positive control, and the location of the crossover.
2. **Half of the DAY band was unreachable.** It needed median E >= 3 at s = 0.003 and 0.01, which the audit's own registered arithmetic excluded. "Not DAY" carries no information. The informative contrast was CRITIC against MIDDLE. This is a limit of the test, on Day's side.
3. **The registered wording on C1 and C1a does not fit what the run shows about mechanism** (section 6). The zero power at low s comes from the window length, not from the panel; and for standing-variant sweeps the panel favours detection, as C1a says. The run gives no ground to move C1a toward `contradicted`, and reviewers should consider whether "wrong for selection-driven completions" can stand as the comment, and whether "the power result added" to C1 should say that ascertainment is not what removes the power. These are observations for the reviews, not moves.
4. The MIDDLE band's registered comment ("zero excludes the required rate only if its sweeps had s >= s\*") is also true of this result, with s\* = 0.05 (new mutations) and 0.03 (standing, by 3%). The difference between CRITIC and MIDDLE in the rule is whether the mainstream model and the s <= 0.01 cells are at zero, which they are.

## 9. Rule RD: what "zero fixations refutes selection" depends on

The observation (Q53, Q55: zero) is not in question here. The inference from it to "the selection-driven rate is refuted" depends on which s the required-rate sweeps are taken to have, and on which of Day's statistics is meant. Day's sources give the comparator (15,556) and, elsewhere, a beneficial s of 1e-3; they do not state an s for the 15,556. Readings and the verdict on C external under each (not chosen here):

| Reading | What it takes Day's model to imply | Expected count at 15,556 (median, 25 y) | Verdict on C external under this reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| R-a | The sweeps have Day's own beneficial s = 1e-3 | 0 in both modes, every statistic | `contradicted` (scoped): the zero is what the model predicts, so it refutes nothing |
| R-b | Any s up to 0.02 (weak to moderately strong selection) | at most 2e-9 (largest 25-y cell 1e-6; largest 20-y cell 0.044) | `contradicted` (scoped), as R-a |
| R-c | s unspecified; the claim is read as "zero excludes the required rate for some s" | crossover s\* = 0.05 / 0.03 (B10), 0.05 (Z50), 0.1 (Z10) | `contested`, with the comment "only if its sweeps had s >= s\*" (the registered MIDDLE wording) |
| R-d | Strong selection, s >= 0.05 | 286 to 3,924 (B10); 8.6 to 1,160 (Z50) | `supported` (scoped): zero excludes 15,556 such sweeps, P(0) < 1e-69 |
| R-e | The 15,556 are the neutral-clock substitutions of the modern synthesis (20,000,000 / 9,000,000 y), not sweeps | neutral model: 0 | unchanged; this is the existing internal `non-sequitur` |

By statistic: under R-c and R-d the blog statistic (Q55, B10) is the stronger test; the paper's "below 10% to fixation" (Q53, Z10) has power only at s = 0.1 (median 6.8 hard, 76 standing), and at s = 0.05 expects 1e-11 to 0.04.

The registered CRITIC move corresponds to R-a and R-b. Under rule RD the claim file records every reading; which value the verdict field takes ("the reading under which the quoted statement is most defensible") is for the reviews and integration, and the count of reading-dependent verdicts rises by one if this table is adopted.

## 10. Who this helps

**Day**
- **The panel registers strong sweeps.** At s >= 0.05 his statistics would show hundreds to thousands of events at his required rate. His premise that ascertainment does not hide completed sweeps holds for strong selection, in both modes and both designs.
- **C1a's sign is borne out for standing-variant sweeps:** the swept site is on the panel 240 to 670 times more often than a random site. The audit's P4 and P5 predicted otherwise and missed.
- **The zero power at low s is not an ascertainment effect.** C1's "mechanical exclusion" is exact for European new mutations and yet costs nothing at s >= 0.05.
- At s = 0.03 some cells do give E >= 3: standing const 3.6 and Gazave 4.9 (D3: three of four, 3.7 to 7.3); **at his own 20-year generation all four cells** (10.1, 9.7, 57, 51), where s\* is 0.03 in both modes.
- M10 excludes his required rate one step earlier (s >= 0.03) than the zero statistics.
- **Half of the DAY band was unreachable** by the audit's own arithmetic, and CRITIC was the expected band: the move against him was weakly tested.
- The V3 bias runs toward his band, so it cannot have produced the result against him; conversely the true crossover may be higher than reported.
- The 15,556 is his figure taken at face value: genome-wide, no panel scaling, every completion a sweep.

**Critics**
- **The zero carries no information for s <= 0.02**, which includes Day's own beneficial s = 1e-3: expected count 0 at his required rate, margin more than 15 orders of magnitude at s <= 0.01, in every cell and design.
- **The mainstream model predicts about zero**, even at 100 times the central rate. keruru's "zero is the expectation" (C5) holds under neutrality, under the mainstream selection model, and under Day's rate with weak selection.
- No simulated completion at s <= 0.02 began below 50% at 7,500 BP: "7,000 years is too short" is the operative mechanism.
- "Below 10% to fixation" (Q53) has no power below s = 0.1.
- For European new mutations at s >= 0.02 the swept site is never on the panel (C1 point 1, exact).

**Both (the audit's own errors)**
- Two launch bugs (an unpicklable task tuple; `sel` rounding above 1), both fixed post hoc before any task output.
- P7's band clause contradicted the coded rule and P2 + P3 (ST-09): a registration inconsistency, caught by the criticality pass before launch, not by the author of the check.
- V3 missed at tight linkage and the run went ahead on a reading fixed in advance; the tolerance was not changed.
- Six of eight predictions missed at least in part. The audit's model of how the panel treats sweeps (P4, P5) was wrong in Day's favour; its guess at the hard-mode crossover (P3b, P7b) was wrong in the critics' favour.
- The run location departed from the pre-run note (builder had no scipy), recorded only in `l5.host`.

## 11. Open (for the reviews)

1. **Sampling resolution against F.** Each cell simulates 253 to 3,008 window completions; F is 15,556. A class of trajectories rarer than about 1 in 1,000 completions is absent from the sample but could contribute at F. The pooled counts "0 of 3,007 to 6,380 from below 50%" bound that class at about 7 to 16 completions per 15,556 (rule of three; post hoc, descriptive), not at 1e-27. The claim at s <= 0.02 therefore rests on the simulation **and** the deterministic sweep-time arithmetic (section 4), not on the printed exponents. Reviewers: is that enough for "E <= 1 in every cell"? (Day-side question.)
2. **V3** at r = 1e-4: does the factor 2.2 in non-founder share qualify the hard-mode Z50 values at s >= 0.05 (monomorphy in about 1,300 chromosomes is sensitive to a 1% share)? The band does not depend on them.
3. **D3 is a partial sensitivity.** In `sweep_events` the hitchhiking SNPs are drawn from the D2 panel sample for both labels; D3 only reweights them (hard mode) or leaves them unweighted (standing). Hence D3 = D2 exactly in hard mode wherever the sweep is absent from Africa. D3 differs only through the focal site and the neutral background. "D3 does not change the band" is true but weaker than an independent D3 panel would make it.
4. **Normalisation by exact population fixation.** E counts events from every sweep at 80% or more today, per sweep that reached exact fixation in the window. In the growth demographies fixation of the last copies is slow, so `n_wc` is small relative to sweeps in progress (hard s = 0.05, Nelson: E = 757 with 0 of 696 completions from below 50%). This raises E at s >= 0.03 (Day-favourable) and may explain the ordering Nelson > const.
5. **Standing s\* = 0.03 is a knife edge**: median 3.08 against 3, two of four demographies below 3, no Monte Carlo error available (the json holds sums only). On Z50 alone the standing crossover is 0.05.
6. **Mainstream s mapping** (72% at 1e-3, 28% at 1e-2) is ASSUMED; a mainstream model with a few strong Holocene sweeps (lactase-class, s of a few percent) was not run. At F = 4.49 and E per completion of 0.011 to 0.049 (s = 0.05, 25 y) it would still expect 0.05 to 0.22, but that is arithmetic from this table, not a registered result.
7. Known limits from the docstring stand: no admixture, migration, background selection, genotype error or capture heterogeneity; single-African-individual discovery; generation time 25 y primary; panel model validated on v62 at constant 1e4 only (v66 N100 2.9 times over).
8. The second timestamp in `l5.host` is unlabelled.
9. P6's single miss (standing, s = 0.1, const) is unexplained; at that s nearly every sweep registers in either clock, so the 12% difference may be Monte Carlo noise.
