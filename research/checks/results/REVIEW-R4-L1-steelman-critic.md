# Review of R4-L1 (steelman, critics' side)

Reviewer role: argue as the most capable and honest critic of Day's dN/dS claims would (a population geneticist familiar with the Consortium's methods). Questions asked: does L1 state the strongest critic-side case? Is it robust to the T2T objection? Does the write-up give too much ground through the "real gene-set effect" credit? Is DN4 (dN/dS read as a verdict on adaptive evolution) handled as firmly as the audit's own rules allow?

Reviewed (2026-10-11, head ef5fb49): the same files as the correctness and Day-side reviews (`R4-L1.md`, the three L1 scripts' docstrings, the raw outputs, Day's saved post ¶6-¶23, claim files DN1-DN5, R4-X1). The literature is cited below from reviewer memory only and is marked so. Under hard rule 2, none of it may be recorded as a number in the repo until the text has been fetched.

Overall: L1 is a fair, well-designed replication. The critic-side predictions are scored honestly, including one marginal miss (ALL/O121 1.309 > 1.3). Its strongest critic-side result, though, is buried under a post hoc mechanism story. That result comes from Day's own numbers and is estimator-free. It is also robust to T2T. DN4's non-sequitur is decidable from Day's own text now, and the write-up defers it.

## Verdicts (critics' view)
- **DN1:** internal `pending` (unreleased method). External **`contradicted`**, on the estimator-free count discrepancy (finding 1) plus the hg38 replication. L1b is confirmatory.
