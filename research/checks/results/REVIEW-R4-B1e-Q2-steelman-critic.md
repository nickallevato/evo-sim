## Findings

### SC1. MAJOR (moves a field-setting value; lead ruling): R-A's external `contested` rests on a "true half" that the run's own P3 and P4 refute
- **Evidence.** The R-A row (l.81) says: "`contested`: true that (7) on the open interval fixes no split (P5); false that what fixes it is a Wright-Fisher import. It is the classical solution's own boundary-flux bookkeeping (P4)". The second clause already contradicts the first: if the classical solution's own boundary fluxes fix the split, then (7) on the open interval does fix it. My flux computation above makes this concrete. The probability that leaves the interior through x = 1 over all time, computed from r alone, is ∫x p^I (4/9 for the registered start; x0 for a delta start). That is P_fix = p0, and for one copy it is the "1/(2N)". P5's three alternative splits (θ = 0, 0.5, 1) satisfy constraint 1 only by booking mass that leaves at x = 1 as loss at x = 0, or the reverse. That is non-local, and no model of fixation allows it. So, under R-A's own gloss of "the mathematics" (PDE (7) on the open interval), the second sentence of QB ("It is not a result produced by the mathematics") is false. The first sentence ("an assumption imported from the Wright-Fisher model", glossed as "enters from the discrete process") is false in substance too. What enters from outside the PDE is only that mass leaving at x = i stays at x = i. That is the meaning of the state x = 1, not a premise about reproduction, and the amount is computed by the PDE.
- **Best defence of `contested`, recorded.** Identifying "exit at x = 1" with "fixation" is a modelling act from outside (7). Chalub himself frames the laws as additional ("two additional conservation laws had to be introduced into the model", §1, l.44-45). Neither carries the number: the identification fixes which boundary is called fixation, and (7) fixes how much arrives there. Chalub's framing bears on fidelity, not on the mathematics.
- **Against correctness C4's `supported` option.** C4 reads a law "introduced into the model" as close to an "assumption". The flux result shows the law adds no number that (7) does not already give, so `supported` is not open on the mathematics. I agree with C4's part (1): drop the P6b clause from R-A's rationale. P6b answers WF-specificity, which R-A's gloss has set aside.
- **Fix.** Restate R-A external as: "false in both halves under R-A's gloss. The classical solution's integrated outflux at x = 1 equals ∫x p^I (P3 with P4; reviewer check SC1). P5's free split needs non-local booking." Propose R-A external **`contradicted`** (from `contested`). This challenges the registered mapping, as C4 does in the other direction, so it is a D-24 ruling. Because it moves the governing value of a field, a fresh reviewer checks it before integration.

### SC3. MAJOR: fidelity evidence that needs no simulation is cut off. Chalub's second law is the conservation of ϕ, the diffusion's own fixation-probability function, for any selection ψ
- **Evidence.** The write-up quotes C1 as "two conservation laws derived directly from the discrete process". The full sentence (§2.2, l.147-150) continues: "are satisfied: ∂t ∫₀¹ p(x, t)dx = ∂t ∫₀¹ ϕ(x)p(x, t)dx = 0, where ϕ is the solution of ϕ″ + ψϕ′ = 0, ϕ(0) = 0, ϕ(1) = 1. See [8] for further discussions. In the neutral case ψ = 0, and therefore, the two conservation laws are" (9). Three facts follow from Chalub's own text:
  - (i) The second law is not "the average allele frequency never changes" (B3c). It is the conservation of ϕ for arbitrary ψ, under which mean frequency does change. Mean frequency is ϕ only in the neutral case.
  - (ii) The laws come from the "large population, small time-step limit" of "the Moran and the Wright-Fisher processes" in the generalised, frequency-dependent model ([7], [8]; §1, l.38-44). They are not Wright-Fisher-specific.
  - (iii) ϕ″ + ψϕ′ = 0 is the stationary backward equation of (7) itself. The adjoint of (7) with κ = 1 is x(1−x)(ϕ″ + ψϕ′). Its boundary values 0 and 1 are the meaning of loss and fixation. The value the law carries, ϕ(x0), is therefore fixed by an ODE built from the diffusion's own coefficients, with no Wright-Fisher input (reviewer check for constant ψ: the classical (1 − e^{−sx})/(1 − e^{−s})).
  Correctness C6 found the same paragraph's companion passage (l.46-49). Under every reading, (i)-(iii) bear on "imported from the Wright-Fisher model" and on "not a result produced by the mathematics".
- **What it does not move (against critics' interest).** Under R-A's gloss, Chalub's text does not contradict "enters from the discrete process" or "introduced into the model". So R-A fidelity `partial` stands on the rule's definition (l.84: `misread` needs the source "in hand and contradicts the reading"). The finding strengthens R-B's and R-A0's `misread` and the external case of SC1.
- **Fix.** Quote the full C1 sentence in "Quotes for integration" and in the fidelity basis of R-A, R-B and the R-A0 row C4 proposes. Add to the critic bullets: Chalub's second law is the conservation of the diffusion's own ϕ, valid with selection, and reduces to mean frequency only at ψ = 0. Slip-ledger entry for B3c's description (context, S2).
