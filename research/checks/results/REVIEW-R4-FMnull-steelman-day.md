# Review of R4-FMnull (steelman, Day's side)

Reviewer role: argue as Vox Day's most capable and honest defender would. Questions asked: under the reading of Q164 that is most defensible in his own words, where does 80 land, and is that said? They are for the fix pass to check, not results.

Overall. From Day's side the write-up is careful where it matters most: it proposes no verdict move, it reports both branches of the registered rule (with and without the gate), it leads with ST-10, it records the P8 (i) miss as his, and it lists the gorilla failure and the alignment-term dependence without softening. What is left falls in five places:
- His sentence is true of the held data under its most natural reading, and the write-up never says so in those words.
- "No measured cell is DAY" is reported as a finding, though the same would be reported if a block mechanism of the size the check itself planted were real.
- The counts of cells "inside" include cells that are inside only through an unsourced artefact term or through the variants that the data left standing because they are wide.
- The gate measures closeness to an SD-ratio reading in variance units, and "the data" in the gate are the audit's alignable subset.
- The lineage-specific signal exceeds the registered neutral bound at the three finest scales, which the write-up does not report.

The honest cost to Day is stated first, so that nothing below is read past it. Nothing in FMnull supports FM10. On repeat-masked single-copy sequence the measured indices sit near the central neutral scenario, not near its upper edge (H, F2, 100 kb: 14.3 at exposure 0.58, about 24 at exposure 1, against 11.6 and 32.0 in the central PREREG and SOURCED simulations). No measured cell is above three times any upper bound, with or without the alignment term. At the windows where his sentence is true of these data (1 Mb and above for single lineages) the pedigree-calibrated null predicts as much dispersion or more. His number can be granted and his conclusion still does not follow from it.

## Answers to the seven questions put to this review
1. **Most defensible reading.** R1. Under it Q164 is true of the held data at 1 Mb and above and is not anomalous there. The write-up gives the reading its due on anomaly and not on truth (finding 1). R2 is DAY as a class and not reproduced for single lineages; "not reproduced" is fair for them, not for DHC in the reading's own units, and it is not a critic point (finding 4).
2. **Small windows.** Charity grants the window; it does not grant the conclusion. If W <= 10 kb he has a number this null cannot reach and the audit cannot find on alignable sequence. Both halves belong in the text (finding 5).
3. **Elastic null.** DAY needs 7 to 34 times the central neutral index at 100 kb; the variants that survive the low edge are the wide ones; 10 to 23 of 35 unmasked cells are inside only through the alignment term. The write-up says parts of this and counts as if it had not (finding 3).
4. **φ_HC.** Above the registered bound at 1, 3 and 10 kb on single-copy sequence; absorbed by a lineage-specific share of about 0.05. No Day-side criterion could fire, so "unmet" carries no weight (finding 6).
5. **ST-10.** Severe for DAY, weak for its absence. The absence is what was observed; three sentences read the other way (finding 2).
6. **Gate.** The write-up does not lean on the flag. It leans on the W restriction to remove the small-W DAY cells and on a variance-unit convention to remove R2 (findings 4, 5).
7. **Tone and labels.** Findings 7 to 12. No conclusion in section 7 is stronger than the registered rule allows; several sentences in section 8 are.
