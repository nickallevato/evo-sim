## Q1. B-R32's basis: does it rest on registered items?

**The derivation stands without any reviewer post hoc computation.** It needs two stated identities, which the ruling should name (RC-3). From the registered items:
- P3 registers "(1/2) sum d_n = 4/9" and "Chalub's printed a(t), b(t) equal ... int x p^I - int x r", all to 1e-25 (docstring l.142-145; HIT, worst 1.38e-40, `b_inf 0.444444444444`, raw l.18). Chalub's printed form is b(t) = ½ Σ d_n (1 − e^{−λ_n t}), so b(0) = 0 is an identity of the printed formula, not a printed item.
- P4 registers b′(t) = −J(1), J = ∂x[x(1−x) r], "the outward flux of the classical solution at x = 1", for four delta starts **and the Beta(4,5) start** at t ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.5, 2} (docstring l.146-151; raw l.20-39; HIT, worst_rel 4.98e-64). The Beta(4,5) rows (raw l.36-39) are the same start as P3, so the two items can be combined without any extension over starts.
- To integrate over t ∈ [0, ∞) one needs b′ = −J(1) at **every** t, and P4 printed four. The step is the mode-wise identity −∂x[x(1−x)C_n^{3/2}(2x−1)] at x = 1 = C_n^{3/2}(1) = λ_n/2 (correctness C3, checked there for n < 30). It is an analytic identity and needs no new run. With it: ∫₀^∞ −J(1) dt = Σ d_n C_n^{3/2}(1)/λ_n = ½ Σ d_n = 4/9 = b(∞) − b(0). Every term on the left is a function of r alone, so the ruling's claim ("computed from r, with no constraint and no point mass entering") is right.
- "For a one-copy start that number *is* the 1/(2N)": this is Chalub's general b(∞) = ∫x p^I applied to p^I = δ_{x0}. It is a stated identity, not a printed item (P3 printed the Beta start only). P7's large-t item (b ≈ x0 − 3x0(1 − x0)e^{−2t}, 3.43e-9 at t = 5) is consistent with it. Acceptable as worded.

**The P5 half survives only under non-local booking: correct.** P5 (docstring l.152 ff.) splits total absorbed mass by a fixed ratio θ, not by where it leaves. The fix pass conceded this to SC2, and the registration's own P4 consequence says the same ("the split is fixed by the classical solution's own boundary fluxes", l.150-151).

**P6b is correctly out of the basis.** R-A glosses "imported from the Wright-Fisher model" as "enters from the discrete process" (l.195-197). P6b tests Wright-Fisher exclusivity, which is R-A0. All three reviews agree, and so do I.

**But the basis does not reach `contradicted` from (1) and (2).** (1) and (2) establish that the *number* is the classical solution's own outflux and that the free split needs non-local booking. They do not exclude non-local booking under R-A, because R-A's "the mathematics" is PDE (7) **on the open interval**, and (7) on the open interval says nothing about where mass that has left it is booked. Ruling out non-local booking needs basis (3), the absorbing-boundary semantics. That is where the ruling changes reading (RC-1).

## Q2. `contradicted`, `contested` or `supported`?

### RC-1. MAJOR: B-R32 reaches `contradicted` under R-A by importing R-B's gloss; under R-A as B-R31 keeps it, QB's external value is `contested`
- **Evidence.** (a) Basis (3) says "the most defensible reading of 'the mathematics' cannot be narrower than a diffusion whose boundaries are absorbing". R-B, as registered, is exactly that: "'the mathematics' = the neutral diffusion model with its boundary behaviour" (docstring l.198). R-A is "PDE (7) on the open interval" (l.196). So basis (3) does not score R-A. It replaces R-A's gloss with R-B's while keeping R-A's name. In B-R31 the lead rules that R-A (open interval) governs and that R-B is "not field-setting". B-R32 then scores the field on R-B's content. That is a re-ranking of readings, which B-R31 and RD forbid.
  (b) Sentence 1 is not shown false under R-A. The ruling calls it false because "the input the diffusion needs is its own boundary semantics, not anything taken from the discrete process". Its support is D1's appended derivation (the weak, generator-dual form on [0, 1] yields both laws) and SC3 (iii) (ϕ solves the diffusion's own stationary backward equation). The write-up names that exact contrast as the R-A/R-B hinge: "Strong form of (7) on (0, 1) ... against the weak (generator-dual) form on [0, 1] ... Under the second, 'The diffusion cannot produce it' (B6) is false" (R4-B1e-Q2.md l.90). R-A is the strong form, and that is Chalub's own set-up (C2, C4). Under the strong form the laws are not supplied by (7), and Chalub says they are "derived directly from the discrete process" (C1, C13). Both can hold at once: a law can follow from the generator on [0, 1] *and* be derived from the discrete chain as its limit. "Derived from the discrete process" is therefore not false. The ruling's own fidelity basis accepts it as a fair rendering of Chalub (B3e). Under R-A, sentence 1 is defensible as glossed, apart from the Wright-Fisher narrowing, which is fidelity, not external.
  (c) Sentence 2 is half true under R-A. Read as "the *value* p0 is not produced by (7)", it is false: P3 with P4 give it as the classical solution's own integrated outflux (Q1 above). Read as "(7) on the open interval with probability conservation alone does not fix which boundary's account the mass goes to", it is true, and that reading is the one P5 hit (with SC2's booking qualifier). RD sets the field by the reading under which the quoted statement is most defensible (rule file l.198). Basis (3) moves in the other direction: it uses a different source to make QB less defensible.
  (d) This is the shape the audit already files as `contested`. RD l.198: "A claim whose formal statement fixes the reading ... is `SCOPED`, not reading-dependent; other scopes are recorded as contested." The Zenodo conjunct in the same claim is `contested` "on the start state only (true from an empty start, the paper's premise; from a stationary start the flux is exactly μ)". It is literally false under one premise and true under another, and it was not filed `contradicted`. A3a external is `contested` with "contradicted if 205M is read as an event count" and untested under Day's stated reading. QB under R-A is the same: false on the number, true on the input that (7) on (0, 1) does not supply, with the split between the two set by a modelling choice (strong vs weak form) that the quote does not fix. Filing it `contradicted` would treat Day's quote more strictly than the audit treated the Zenodo sentence in the same file.
  (e) The conjunction argument ("on every plausible reading at least one conjunct is false and under the governing reading both are") does not survive (b): under R-A neither conjunct is shown false as a whole. The ruling's "narrower still" reading, in which "even local booking counts as an outside import", is R-A itself. On it the ruling concedes that sentence 2 "could be true" and holds sentence 1 false only by the weak-form argument of (b).
- **Steelman for the ruling, recorded.** Local booking is close to analytic: frequency 1 *is* fixation, and a jump of mass from x = 1 to x = 0 is not a model of anything. On that view the only input outside (7) is a definition. A definition is not "an assumption imported from the Wright-Fisher model", and the 1/(2N) is a result. This is SC1's case and it is strong. It is an argument about whether a boundary convention counts as "the mathematics", and that is exactly the open point between R-A and R-B. It belongs in the scope line as the false half and the lean, not in a value that settles it.
- **Fix (value and rationale).** QB external under R-A: **`contested`** (the registered value; no field move). Rationale, restated in R-A's own terms and post hoc against the registered wording (P6b dropped, booking qualifier added): *true* that (7) on the open interval with probability conservation alone leaves the split free unless mass leaving at one end may be booked at the other (P5), and that Chalub adds the laws from the discrete process (C1, C2, C13); *false* that the value is an input: with local booking it is the classical solution's own integrated outflux at x = 1, ∫x p^I (P3 with P4), and what enters from outside (7) is the meaning of the boundary states, which carries no number. R-A0 and R-B stay `contradicted`, not field-setting. Because this keeps the registered value, it needs no further fresh-reviewer check under D-24. If the lead keeps `contradicted`, the ruling must first say why basis (3) does not adopt R-B's gloss, and B-R31 then has to be re-argued (RC-8).
