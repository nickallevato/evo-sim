### Hössjer's own tables at d_max = 1 *[post hoc, PH-G; verified against the source]*
Verified by `pdftotext` of the local copy `sources/raw/d15-hossjer2021/hossjer2021.pdf` (Hössjer, Bechly & Gauger 2021, J. Theor. Biol. 524:110657), grep only:
- Table 5 (neutral), W = 6, d_max = 1: E(T_1) = 4.5271e4, E(T_2) = 9.0758e4 generations (1.1 and 2.3 My at 25 y), against 5.3813e7 and 2.0518e8 at d_max = 0. The stored `hossjer_ET` reproduces all four within 0.2% (4.527e4, 9.077e4, 5.381e7, 2.055e8; C = 2).
- Table 9, K = 2, W = 6, d_max = 1: 7.10e2 generations (targets differing in 1 position) and 2.24e2 (differing in W positions).
- So **his own model at his own published parameter values gives sub-9-My times** for mismatch-tolerant sites, and HO-12 does not say which W, d_max or K the prediction assumes (critic-steelman M2).
- The Day-side reading, computed here (PH-E, PH-G) from his H0 formula (App. D: H0 ∼ Po(L0 K 4^-W Σ_d C(W,d) 3^d)) with L = 1000: at W = 6, d_max = 1, E0 = 4.62 chance matches per regulatory region, so a matching site is **already present at t = 0 with probability 0.99**. The 4.5e4 mean is driven by that: from an all-absent start ("new binding sites appear", review p.7), the same chain gives E[T_1] = 4.57e6 generations (114 My at 25 y; P(T ≤ 9 My) = 0.076) and E[T_2] = 6.88e6 (172 My; P = 0.006). His paper states this effect in words only for d_max = 2: "if dmax = 2 and W = 6, with very high probability, the target is reached already at time t = 0" (App. B.1.1, discussion of Table 5). I found no such sentence for d_max = 1.
- **Sweep relabel (correctness m5).** kmult = 15 is exactly the d_max = 1 *forward* rate (k10 = 15.0 × UF0) from an all-absent start; it keeps the sweep's back rate 5.3e-8 rather than his 2.2e-9 and his stationary start. The sweep's neutral kmult = 15 m = 1 (p9 0.08, mean 4.26e6 at N_e = 1e4) agrees with the absent-start chain above (0.076, 4.57e6). Read: "a *new* mismatch-tolerant W = 6 site exceeds 9 My on average (≈ 12x) but is reached within 9 My in about 8% of histories; a pre-existing one is not a new feature."

### Which genes: any m of M *[post hoc, PH-F; verified against the source; analytic, not simulated]*
The sweep fixes in advance which m genes must change; kmult varies only redundancy *within* a gene (critic-steelman M1). Hössjer's 2021 Appendix D models the alternative. Verbatim (App. D and Table 16 caption): Table 16 gives "Expected waiting time E(T_Mm) until the first m of M possible targets have appeared, when any subset of m genes (among all (M choose m) possible subsets) constitutes a valid target of m = 5 coordinated mutations" (L = 1000, K = 3, W = 10, d_max = 0, M = 5 to 25). His conclusion: "for this particular example, … although multiple targets decrease the expected waiting time a lot, it is not sufficient to bring it down to small numbers." Table 16's c = 0 entries run 7.85e8 to 8.32e9 generations (the c = 1 entries are larger as rendered; `pdftotext` drops some minus signs in this paper, so I do not quote them). He adds: "one may argue that for biological reasons, (D.2) is unnecessarily restrictive. Indeed, it is often the case that only a few multiple targets (and only a few pathways to each one of them) are possible (Weinreich et al., 2006)." He gives no value at W = 6 or at M in the thousands.

- **Critic reading.** If the human/chimp expression differences could have been produced by "any of many" gene sets, the prediction fails by orders of magnitude at M ≥ ~1,000; this moves the answer more than kmult or s.
- **Day reading.** At W = 6 the stationary P(site present) is 0.216 per gene, so about 4,300 of 20,000 genes already carry such a site at t = 0 and the "any m of M" target is met before evolution starts: it is not a new feature. Hössjer's own text argues that only a few multiple targets are biologically possible. The disjoint-subset reading (his D.2) stays far above 9 My for m ≥ 4.

### N_e and the fixation sojourn *[post hoc, PH-C; replaces old disclosure 3]*
The fixed-state chain counts only the waiting time to the origination of the successful mutant. A neutral allele that fixes takes about 4N_e generations to do so: 4e4 at N_e = 1e4 and **4e5 at N_e = 1e5, which is longer than the whole window** (10 My at 25 y, 8 My at 20 y). In the stored data, sim mean − chain averages 5.7e5 generations over the 19 complete N_e = 1e5 neutral cells (every cell within 1.3 SE of 4N_e, including the flagged kmult=100 m=1 cell, excess 4.66e5, ratio 1.68), and 2.7e4 over the 22 complete N_e = 1e4 cells (every cell within 2 SE of 4e4). The earlier attribution to the f = 10 scaling is withdrawn; the monomorphic jump is exact up to a negligible multi-hit approximation (correctness N1). **This is a Day-favourable latency floor**: for neutral routes a larger N_e adds ≈ 4N_e generations the chain omits (the "latency is not throughput" point Day makes in F1a, here at the regulatory-site level).

## Proposed verdicts *[revised post hoc]*

## Who this helps
- **Hössjer / the Day side.** The core arithmetic is confirmed independently: in all 96 in-scope cells (specific W=6 site, m ≥ 2, neutral or valley intermediates) none approaches 9 My (p9 = 0, p90 ≤ 0.017 at both N_e, with and without recombination, at 20 y and 25 y). Valley intermediates of 1e-2 to 1e-3 never complete at kmult ≤ 15, and only a few replicates do at kmult 30-100. Even at kmult 3-10 the neutral and valley cells "exceed" 9 My, though four low-m cells at kmult ≤ 3 do not "far exceed". Requiring several coordinated changes (m>=3) makes the waiting time longer in every family; recombination, even at its upper bound, changes little. The Fin family "exceeds" for m ≥ 3, kmult ≤ 10. *[post hoc]* For neutral routes, a larger N_e adds ≈ 4N_e generations of fixation time (≈ 10 My at N_e = 1e5), a floor the fixed-state chain omits. His Table 5 d_max = 1 rows are fast only because the site is already there; a new such site takes ≈ 4.6e6 generations. At N_e = 1e4 the no-tunnelling chain is accurate for the Fin cells.
- **Critics (Durrett and Schmidt; the Panda's Thumb line).** The result also confirms their central point: the prediction is conditional. With +1% per step intermediates (S2), the 9 My target is reached in 57-77% of replicates at kmult=1, N_e=1e4 (100% at kmult >= 3) and 100% at N_e=1e5, for a specific site and any m from 2 to 4; final-only benefit reaches it at kmult >= 30 for m=2. *[post hoc, corrected]* At N_e = 1e5 the no-tunnelling chain overstates Fin times by about 1.4-5.4x (MLE), most at m = 2. Hössjer's own model gives sub-9-My times at d_max = 1 (Table 5) and in an any-m-of-M reading at W = 6 with M ≳ 1,000 genes, and his review does not say which W, d_max or gene-set specificity his prediction assumes. Per the critic review (N2, citing B2; not re-checked here), Durrett & Schmidt's own specific-pair number (162-216 My) agrees with Hössjer on the specific case; their critical point was about non-specific targets, which is the gene-set question (critic-steelman N2).
- **Both.** The dispute reduces to three empirical inputs, none of which **any source in this audit's corpus** has supplied: (1) how many equivalent sites a human regulatory change can use (kmult, d_max); (2) whether the intermediates were beneficial; (3) which genes: whether the observed expression differences required a particular gene set or could have come from many (G3, G3b).
