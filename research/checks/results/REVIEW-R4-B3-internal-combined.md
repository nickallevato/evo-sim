## 3. Critic-side steelman

**K-1. MAJOR. For a choice between joinings, "most defensible" always picks the weakest joining, so D-15's guard has nothing to act on.** A disjunction ("some route follows") is always at least as defensible as a conjunction. Ranking joinings by defensibility therefore always gives the reading that is best for the speaker. That is exactly the outcome D-15 was written to keep separate from the criterion ("not the reading that gives the speaker the best verdict", DECISIONS l.31). Once applied here, every umbrella with one quoted child that holds would hold, so the umbrella value becomes the best of its quoted children. That is a general aggregation rule with no owner decision behind it (OPERATING-PROCESS, "Don't open new process machinery"). The B umbrella's RD precedent ranked readings of one quote ("nearly empty"), not joinings (B claim l.12, l.87). *Fix:* same as C-1 (counted once in the totals).
