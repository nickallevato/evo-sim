# Review of R4-FM: Day-side steelman

Reviewer role: argue for Day as strongly as honesty allows, then judge fairness. Date: 2026-10-10. Targets: `results/R4-FM.md` (87ace0b), `research/checks/fm_mesa_replication.py` (d723064, unchanged), `raw/fm_main.out`, `raw/fm_analyse.json`, ROOT-FM claim file, `quotes-day.md` Q162-Q171, `refresh-2026-10-10.md`, `R4-X1-verdict-rule.md` (rules S, C, N, F, RH). The numbers not in R4 come from `fm_main.out` and the JSON mesa block (see `REVIEW-R4-FM-correctness.md`). No repo run.

## Verdict in brief
The check is a fair replication attempt. It takes Day's invitation (FM10, Q171) at face value, uses the data he named, freezes a grid before seeing the data, and credits his three descriptive statements. It is unfair to him in three places. It proposes a non-sequitur verdict on FM5 for an inference FM5 does not contain (rule S). It omits the Day-favourable cells that the raw output contains: chrX F1, the human mesa count lead in every cell, and the lowest null coverage. It calls FM4 "supported" without testing the part of FM4 that is most specific to Day's hypothesis, the "sharp boundaries". The critics' side still wins every registered discriminating cell. The question is how widely that result can be stated.

## The strongest honest Day-side case
1. **His descriptive claims replicate on his data.** φ exceeds 80 against a uniform rate at 1 Mb (H 136), and for DHC already at 100 kb (210). Clustering is strong (lag-1 0.45 at 10 kb). There are contiguous high-density blocks, and many have excess above both 191 and 840. Under rule C (most charitable window), FM3, FM4 (clustering) and FM5 as literally worded hold.
2. **The human lineage leads where nothing is masked, and on chrX after the dup mask.** Autosomal F0: φ_res H/C 16.2/8.2 (100 kb) and 34.3/18.8 (1 Mb); φ_HC 8.5-24. In scale-free CV² the F0 H/C ratio is about 1.6. chrX F1 100 kb: φ_res 18.2/4.8 = **3.8**, which is above the > 3 cut the audit itself pre-registered as Day's prediction (P3), though for autosomes. An analysis like Day's, unmasked and perhaps including chrX, would see a real human-heavy signal in these data.
3. **The human lineage has the most mesas in every cell, and the shared null explains them least.** Mesa counts H vs C: 794/601, 33/31, 193/167, 18/17. M-null coverage (M/obs) for H is 0.50, 0.64, 0.56 and 0.69, against C at 0.69, 0.58, 0.77 and 0.85. At F2 10 kb that leaves about 85 human mesas unexplained by the null against about 39 for chimp. This is not Day's pre-registered ratio (> 3), but its direction is consistent.
4. **"Some of these mesa regions" (FM7) asks only for some.** Human-specific mesas exist: 35 (F0 10 kb), 1 (F0 100 kb), 2 (F2 10 kb). Sigillation as worded is a hypothesis about *some* mesas in "one dominate lineage". It is not a claim that the pattern as a whole is human-specific.
5. **The invitation to replicate was honest and workable.** One public file, 70 minutes on one core. Day deserves explicit credit for making FM10 checkable, which most of his earlier claims were not.

## Fairness judgement
The pre-registration gave Day clear thresholds and recorded where the audit's own expectations missed (P1 at 1 Mb, P7). The critic-side results on autosomal single-copy sequence are robust: φ_HC 1.8, symmetric φ_res, 0/0 specific mesas, a 1-2% DHC share. Findings 1 and 4 concern verdict labels, not data. Finding 2 is a reporting balance issue. Finding 3 is the one substantive gap on Day's side: sigillation's distinctive prediction (discrete, sharp-edged, single-lineage blocks) was only half tested. With these fixed I would support: FM3 `holds` / `supported` (description); FM4 clustering `supported`, boundaries `pending`; FM5 `holds` / `supported`, non-discriminating; FM8b `contradicted` (SNV divergence); FM10 internal `non-sequitur` (N2d) if Day's text supports it, otherwise external `contradicted` for the mesa route on single-copy autosomes, with the dup and chrX cells open.
