**The single-SNV share is estimator-free (reviews: correctness #1, steelman-day #5, steelman-critic #1).** Day's 71.6% nonsynonymous among single-SNV codons is a count ratio of 2.52 nonsynonymous per synonymous. No site convention, multiple-hit correction, codon model or aggregation enters it. Every correct-frame gene set here gives 0.67-1.35 (ALL 0.85, one2one 0.67). Unconstrained sequence gives about 3 (the correctness reviewer's simulations: share 0.75 for neutral point mutation and for unrelated random sequence). Day reported this diagnostic, and it excludes estimator explanations on both sides at once: Day's own (the 0.23 is a method artefact) and the critics' easy guesses (he forgot site normalisation, he averaged ratios). What it shows is that the codon pairs Day counted behave as if about 80% of them were unconstrained. It does not say why.

Reading under rule C. The most charitable reading is Day's own: the 0.80 is a real property of the full gene set, so the 0.23 is a curation artefact. On this pipeline that reading fails. All genes give 0.27, and the non-one2one genes would need a pN/pS of 2.4 to lift the total to 0.80. Day's count ratio (2.52) is about three times that of correctly aligned human-chimp coding sequence (0.85). The audit's inference, after the fix pass, is a class statement. **An unidentified extraction or alignment problem pairs human codons with chimp sequence that is not their aligned ortholog, in most genes.** Day's own near-empty stop-filtered stack points the same way. Two refinements from the per-gene run:
- The specific gene-level frame-error mixture proposed in the first version is **withdrawn**. Day's own Q175 contradicts it: such a mixture leaves thousands of intact genes, and filters pull the number down to 0.24-0.25.
- The behaviour Day reports (filter-robust 0.80, a 72% share, a near-empty strict stack) is what a contamination *inside* nearly every gene produces. The one tested instance that fits is chimp sequence out of register with human downstream of a point in each gene (W2), and it needs a terminal-stop filter convention to reach a near-empty stack. Other mechanisms in the same class were not tested and could fit as well: a chimp-only indel the extraction does not handle, partial non-orthologous projection, or misassembled chimp sequence. This is not a finding about Day's code, which is unreleased. His per-gene table (promised, Q176) would show it directly: under this class, per-gene ratios would be high in nearly every gene, rather than very high in a subset.

## Interpretation
- **Day's own counts are the decisive datum (critic-side review #1).** His single-SNV codons give 2.52 nonsynonymous per synonymous, against 0.85 on correctly aligned human-chimp CDS and about 3 for unconstrained sequence. A raw count needs no estimator, and a better assembly cannot raise it: hg38 and T2T-CHM13 coding sequences are nearly identical, and a better chimp assembly removes indel and frameshift errors, which only push the count ratio up.
- The Consortium's 0.23 is reproduced in kind on an independent pipeline: 0.207 on Ensembl one2one genes and 0.200 with CSAC-like quality filters. Its fidelity against the 2005 paper is still unchecked (the paper was not read).
- **Gene-set effect: real, material by R1 (+31%), but a fraction of what Day's 0.80 needs.** All genes give 0.271, against 0.207 for one2one genes, and the non-one2one genes run at 0.45 (0.452 for the 5,935 mapped ones, 0.382 for the 482 unmapped transcripts). Day is right that the curated set gives a lower number than all genes, and by the audit's own 25% line the difference is material. Two qualifications. First, this is a pipeline-level upper bound: part of the non-one2one value is expected to be paralog misalignment, which is the documented reason for orthology filtering, so the exclusion is not evidence of selection to the answer. Second, the effect is +0.06; 0.80 needs +0.57.
- **What "all genes" means here (steelman-day #2).** ALL is all knownCanonical genes *with a syntenic-net multiz alignment*. UCSC builds the panTro5 row from chained and netted pairwise alignments, which restricts it to syntenic chains (reviewer description of the UCSC pipeline; the multiz30way README was not fetched for this pass). So orthology was filtered at the alignment level for every gene, and that step was not varied. L1 is decisive against the *gene-list* version of Q176. Day's *complete-extraction* route, with no alignment-level orthology filter, is untested (L1b).
- **The T2T route is bounded (correctness #12; reviewer arithmetic, post hoc).** Day's 20,400 genes are fewer than ALL's 23,183, so T2T cannot reach 0.80 by adding genes. It would have to change the values of genes already present. Even 1,000 extra genes (about 4% of sites) would need a pN/pS of about 13 to lift 0.271 to 0.80, and unrelated sequence gives about 0.95. A different chimp assembly, alone, cannot close the gap. A different *alignment* that pairs genes with non-orthologous sequence can, but that is an error route, not a curation route.
- Day's filter-insensitivity reproduces in kind (0.23-0.28 across all 32 stacks), but at 0.25, not 0.65.
- Q177's step from dN/dS to "natural selection is not the primary mechanism of adaptive evolution" is scored from Day's own text (DN4, rule N): ¶6 and ¶20 define the ratio as a measure of *purifying* removal, and ¶21 concludes about *adaptive* evolution.
- Literature on nearly-neutral theory and on the time-dependence of dN/dS between close species is qualitatively on Day's side about weaker purifying selection in hominids (both reviewers, from memory). It is not cited here because it has not been fetched, and it does not support 0.80.

## Caveats for the reviewer
1. Not T2T and not GENCODE v46. Genes in newly assembled T2T segmental duplications are absent here, and a whole-genome extraction can project paralogs. That route is not tested (L1b); its arithmetic bound is in the Interpretation.
2. Ensembl one2one (release 116) is not the 2005 list, and 16,766 is not 13,454.
3. Day's filter thresholds are not stated. The audit's thresholds (5% gaps, 5 differences, 300 bp, dN/dS <= 2) are its own.
4. NG convention: changes to stop codons count as nonsynonymous sites (N = 3 - S). Excluding them would lower N/S slightly.
5. The mixture analysis is post hoc and its f is fitted to 0.80; the share follows from the fit. The per-gene run is post hoc but pre-registered (c04a1f8); the W2 + S' row is exploratory and was not pre-registered.
6. The per-gene TSV stores values to four significant digits; a recomputation from it reproduces the JSON (V0 full stack 13,610 genes at 0.247; W2 full stack 1,737 vs 1,736, a rounding effect).
7. `required_non121` targets a JC ratio (0.80) with a p-ratio; the difference is < 1% at these p.
