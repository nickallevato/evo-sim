# Review of R4-E from Day's side (steelman)

Scope: `research/checks/results/R4-E.md`, `e_founder_hazard.py`, `e4_relictation_chain.py`, claim files `docs/research/claims/E*.md`, checked against Day's own text (`sources/raw/day/zenodo-23020792.txt`, `-23034852.txt`, `-23188201.txt`, read only) and the Tenaillon 2016 local copy. I did not re-run the scripts or recompute any numbers; the points below come from reading the report, the scripts' docstrings and Day's text.

Overall: the arithmetic and reproduction work is careful and several Day-favourable results are reported (literal model 2.75% > 2.3%, G-insensitivity, per-gene split harmless, E4 reproduced to 0.0004, "keeps growing" confirmed). The weak spots are in how the verdicts are worded and scoped, not in the numbers.

## Where the audit is right (Day's best case does not rescue these)

- The "minimum of two carriers" mechanism is not how most of the modelled hazard arises (68% from one founder copy), and 39% is too high for P(>= 1).
- A hit is not a population-level failure under the audit's metrics, and E3's cumulative-risk rhetoric ("approaches certainty") does not carry that over.
- The q = 0.0018 pooled figure is a modern human value; E3 §3.6 itself disclaims ancestral representativeness.
- The pooled-gene treatment overstates CMMRD incidence (3x), though the hazard barely changes (0.92-0.98x), which also undercuts Day's claim that omitting multiple loci is "conservative".

## Day-side points to state more prominently

1. Day scopes paper E himself: "per-event risk", consequence needs "formal demographic modeling". The 2.3% is not used in §7. Present as a model result with an open bridge.
2. Estimates 2.0 / 2.3 / 2.38 / 2.75% cluster within +/-20%. The literal model is higher than Day's figure. Lead with this.
3. `hwe` is a standard formulation and reproduces all of Day's tables within about 2 MC SE. This is an independent reproduction of E3.
4. Direction and robustness hold: rises with N and q, front-loaded, G-insensitive beyond about 200, insensitive to the per-gene split. 100-event cumulative risk is 93.9% for >= 1 affected birth.
5. Conditional on a hit there are about 2.5 affected births (0.068/0.0275), and the expected-count burden under drift may well exceed Day's 0.5. State this beside "600x".
6. The consequence endpoints are the audit's own (thresholds, direct viability only) and exclude Day's E3 §4 pathways (heterozygote mutation load, meltdown). Mark them as one test, not the test.
7. Mayr symmetry: the consequence finding is symmetric and leaves Day's central argument unexamined.
8. E4: the full reproduction (table within 0.0004, large-N 1.363 and 1.737, eigenvalue ratios, P_fix exact) and the "keeps growing" confirmation (the opposing pre-registration failed) belong in the verdict headline. Day's body already discloses N-dependent thresholds and a fixed-family return to drift, and the Λ-coalescent framing is his own.
9. The diploid pair-family model is one unvalidated construction with a random re-pairing assumption that biases toward a smaller effect. It tests the F-to-f mapping, not Day's mathematics.
10. Day withdrew the sexual-vertebrate hitchhiking pathway in E3 and says the hazard is the "secondary" finding. Credit this.
