# Review of R4-H3 (steelman, Day's side)

Reviewer role: argue as Vox Day's, and Hössjer's, most capable and honest defender would. Question asked: does H3 test their claims as stated, and is it fair to them?

Reviewed:
- `research/checks/results/R4-H3-human.md`;
- `research/checks/h3_human_scale.py` (docstring and model description), `h3_tables.py` (header);
- claim files H, H1, H5, ROOT-no-mechanism-suffices, A5a; `opponents/ola-hossjer.md`, `opponents/keruru.md`; `ledgers/gaps.md` GAP-01;
- Day sources read-only: `sources/raw/day/zenodo-18168236.txt` (§2.3, §4.4, §5.1), `blog-2026-05-07-a-retraction-and-a-revision.txt`, `blog-2026-09-28-mittens-3-0.txt`.

I ran no code and checked no raw output.

Overall: the pre-registration, the scaling check (P-V) and the D3 rule ("Day-favourable outcomes reported regardless") are sound. Many Day-favourable results are in the text. The weaknesses are of three kinds:
- Three swing assumptions are not shown together: soft versus hard load, R, and the length of the window.
- Two of Day's claims are operationalised more strongly than he stated them.
- The H1 concession is read as wider than the text supports.

## Honest concessions: points against Day that stand

These do not need changes, except that they should be stated alongside the findings above.

1. A hard-selection budget of 10% is R ≈ 1.11, and the sustainable rate rises with ln R (λ50 2.2× at R = 1.2, 19× at R = 3). As a general bound, "10% total budget" is Haldane's parameter, not a law. The H8 form (s_max ≈ 1) fits only if R is about 2.
2. No cost-of-selection model can pay 20M, 17.5M or 205M selected fixations. Whatever Day now thinks of the A/B chain, H as applied to those totals fails.
3. Hard U = 2.2 is incompatible with R ≤ 9 (Keightley's own statement). This is a point for the load being soft, and the report is right to rely on it.
4. Scaling was validated (P-V), and the pre-registration was honoured. Failures of the author's own predictions are reported, and D3 commits to reporting Day-favourable results.
5. The coding-only K_a (GAP-01) is payable at R ≳ 1.2–1.9 at central values and D = 20. If the mean-field φ = 1 holds, even at R ≈ 1.4. Day's cost argument is not decisive without an adaptive noncoding share of at least about 0.1%.
6. Day's §5.1 "Second" argument (neutral fixation takes 4Ne generations, so it is slower) conflates a conditional fixation time with a rate. H3 does not need to repeat it, but it should not be read as part of the case that survives H1.

## What the questions asked come to

- **Adaptive-count framing (GAP-01).** Fair to the cost-of-selection claim after H1, not fair to the A/B argument. It does not present Day's model as his own (finding 7), and it states what would make it right only indirectly (finding 7).
- **Treadmill.** Fair to Haldane's setting and Day-favourable on the cost side only (finding 9).
- **R ≈ 1.2–3 realism.** Not shown (finding 2). The "load is soft" proviso carries the result (finding 1).
- **"1/300 reproduced".** Reported as a headline in §6 but not in a summary; the window caveat is understated (findings 3, 7).
- **Buried Day-favourable items.** Findings 3, 7 and 10 (long-window failure, background selection, φ < 1). The a_nc point is stated (§6 Day 5) but its resolution limit is not (finding 6).
- **"Day's targets fail".** True and uninformative about A/B (finding 5).
- **Credit to critics not argued.** Findings 6 and 8.
