- **MAJOR-1.** The "0 of 12" HMM result is presented as an empirical finding that "fixation calls never decline", but the script reads only line 4 (#fixed) of Good's well-mixed file. In that model fixed and extinct are, as far as the file layout shows, absorbing states, in which case the count cannot fall by construction. The write-up must state whether the count is monotone by design (check `parse_file.py` / the Good 2017 methods) and, if so, say that "0 of 12" is a property of the model, not evidence that nothing was undone. The conclusion "contradicted for fixations as Good defines them" then rests on a definition, which is a legitimate A2c/E6 point but a weaker one than the table suggests. The HMM and snapshot counts also diverge by large factors in places (Ara-5 at 10K-30K: HMM 1, 3, 5 vs snapshot 9, 16, 36; Ara+5 38 vs 13), so the two columns are not the same quantity and should not be differenced by eye.
- **MAJOR-2.** Robustness of "10 of 12" is not shown. Five of the ten qualifying declines are 1-4 mutations (Ara+1 -1, Ara-6 -2, Ara+3 -4, Ara-5's second -3, Ara+5 -3 and -4) at depth/coverage thresholds that move counts by a few. From the table, requiring a net fall of >= 5 leaves 7 of 12 (Ara-1, Ara-2, Ara-4, Ara-5, Ara+4, Ara+5, Ara+6); the Ara+4 decline also leans on the recount's 0 at 50K, though Day's own 28 still gives -10. Report the count under a threshold of 5 and 10, and with Ara+4 50K replaced by Day's value. The audit's own "9-12" range was pre-registered, so this does not change the score, but the reader needs to know the 10 is threshold-sensitive. Day's sentence says "faster", which is sign-only; he is entitled to that reading, and the reproduction under it is exact.
- **MINOR-1.** The critic prediction "most R3 cases were never fixed in Good's sense" is scored a hit on "96% dip or hover; HMM never loses a fixed call". "Hover" (ended 0.5-0.95) and "dip" show the mutation stayed high, not that it was never HMM-fixed, and 'dip' returning to >= 95% fits Day's "undone then redone". Rescore as "partly supported: 4.6% lost, 96% did not end low".
- **MINOR-2.** "Dip" is defined by return to >= 95% at a later snapshot, with no minimum depth margin, so threshold noise around 0.95 is counted as a dynamic. Say so.
- **MINOR-3.** Data provenance is thin in the repo: sha256 list and data live on builder only; the host record gives one truncated hash (m1_annotated 6b08ec9f...). Commit the full hash list (hashes are allowed in git, data is not).
- **MINOR-4.** Caveat 3 (clade-level states FM/Fm, which can be lost) should move from the caveats into the Interpretation, since it is exactly the case in which Good-style calls can fall.
## 2. Day-side steelman
- Credit that reproduces: his count of 10 of 12, five tabulated rows (28 of 29 cells), and the Ara-4 -86 and Ara+6 -39 losses. The internal verdict `holds` on his stated rule is right and R4-X1 S/C are applied properly (verbatim Statement quote, charitable reading, rule inferred and marked as inferred).
- **MINOR-5.** Day can say that 45 mutations at >= 95% ended below 0.5, and that mutator populations show net losses of 39-86, which no one disputes; "undone" is a fair description of what happens to near-fixed alleles in clonal interference, and the 62% "hover" group are mutations that fell off the fixation threshold and stayed off. The write-up credits this; add that 'hover' is not 'noise' by default.
- The HMM comparison is a critic's definition, not Day's. His s4.1 defines the statistic as "mutations at >= 95% population frequency", and his s5.3 uses "fixations" for it. Under R4-X1 F/S this is a wording issue in a rhetorical paragraph (the post tags the line dialectic, so a wording verdict is fair); a `partial` fidelity is defensible, but it should read "partial (term used for a different statistic)", not "contradicted".
## 3. Critic-side steelman
- Strongest point, genuinely supported: Day's own source (Good 2017) publishes fixation calls that do not fall, and 96% of the departures are dips or hovers; "fixations undone" therefore overstates what the trajectories show. The pre-registered critic prediction on R2 was exact.
- **MINOR-6.** Critics would add that the quoted s4.1 table has a 0 for Ara+5 at 60K (no sample) and that a 28 at Ara+4 50K versus the recount's 0 shows the statistic is sensitive to low-coverage timepoints; the write-up mentions both but not that Day's own table has an implausible 0, which his own decline count could inherit. Check whether Day's 10 depends on it (Ara+5 has other declines, so no).
- **MINOR-7.** The mutators/non-mutators split matters: the largest declines (Ara-2 -84, Ara-4 -86, Ara+6 -39, Ara-5 -29, Ara+4 -38) are in a handful of populations; "ten of twelve" averages over many one-to-four-mutation dips. Report the median size of the 10 populations' largest decline.
