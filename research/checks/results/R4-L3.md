## 6. Proposed effect on verdicts (proposals only; integration decides)

were adaptive and does not split them by effect size. So L3 speaks to the **any-effect-size per-generation count
reading**, the reading of A2e's formal statement. That is the reading the superseded `contradicted` value was
scoped to. Per the docstring, L3 licenses **no move** on the strong-effect-only or fitness-weighted readings,
in any band. It licenses no direct move on A or F2 and has no bearing on RG-03 (it is coding-only). A2e internal
(`non-sequitur`) and fidelity are untouched. *Post hoc (D3):* strong-effect adaptive substitutions are a subset
of all adaptive ones, so L3's low point is consistent with the strong-effect-only reading too. This moves nothing
(the strong-only value is already `contested`) and is weakened by HM7's underestimation.

(quotes-literature l.651). On the same 661 samples and divergence basis, ABC-MK gives 0.135 (0.096-0.17) against
aMK 0.076: "our method nearly doubles the estimated human adaptation rate (to α = 0.135)" (R4-A2e-alpha l.137).

## 7. Post hoc, descriptive observations (no band, no verdict)

1. **The correction is large and the corrected value is about zero.** The classic MK over all bins is -0.8423
   (bootstrap [-0.8836, -0.8030]). The window-only value is -0.0789 ([-0.1155, -0.0444]). The asymptote is
   -0.0200. Most of the correction comes from the low-frequency bins (bin 1 alpha -0.996). Above x ≈ 0.3 the
   binned alpha(x) sits at or slightly below zero (12 window bins, mean -0.024, 8 below zero; section 2) and shows
   no rise toward x = 1. *Post hoc (reviewer, D7; rechecked):* the point is not a fit artefact. Pooled window bins
   give alpha -0.023 above x = 0.3 (Pn 5,044, Ps 6,762), -0.018 above 0.5, -0.019 above 0.7; the linear fallback
   on GOV gives 0.0161 (AIC prefers the exponential by 2.87, correctness NOTE-10, AIC not rechecked); S2 (linear by AIC) gives 0.0016.
   All eight point estimates on the record (GOV, S1-S6, GOV linear) are ≤ h.
2. **Uricchio's aMK baseline is not reproduced as a point, but it is not excluded.** The docstring describes
   Uricchio's 0.076 as computed on "the same 661 African samples and the same hg19/panTro4/ponAbe2 parsimony
   divergence". L3's point is 0.096 below it. Yet 0.076 lies inside both of L3's intervals (bootstrap upper
   0.0921; MC upper 48.9101).
3. **The interval reaches far above the flip.** The bootstrap upper bound 0.0921 is 5.3x h. Uricchio's ABC-MK
   lower HPD (0.096, the superseded basis) sits just above the bootstrap upper bound and inside the MC interval.
   *Post hoc (R2):* 0.096 lies 0.004 above the gene-bootstrap upper bound and inside the MC envelope only through
   its tail above alpha = 1; the comparison is across estimators, and HM7 predicts aMK below ABC-MK, so it is weak
   evidence against ABC-MK.
4. **Demography contrast (S5, EUR).** The EUR point is 0.0175 below AFR (Q6 hit). The EUR classic MK is more
   negative (-1.0062 vs -0.8423), as a bottleneck would make it. The asymptote moves much less than the classic
   value does.
5. **Width of the bootstrap interval alone** is 0.1664. That is also over Q5's 0.15, so Q5 would miss even
   without the MC upper tail.
6. **What the precision allowed (*post hoc*, reviewers D1 and SC1; rechecked from `l3_result.json`).** If the
   gene-bootstrap interval shifts with the point (an assumption), B6 needed P ≤ l - (U_boot - P) = -0.107, below
   any possible true value; B2 needed P > h + (P - L) = 0.072, which Uricchio's aMK 0.076 would have met; B1 needed
   P ≥ 3h + (P - L) = 0.107. On the MC envelope B6 was out of reach. The alpha* band (h - l = 0.0119) is 13.9x
   narrower than the bootstrap interval (0.1664). B5 is the strongest result the design could realistically give
   the Day side; the critic side had a realistic path to `contradicted`. The registration is not at fault: the
   width comes from the data, and the B6 → lean asymmetry (HM7) was stated in advance.
