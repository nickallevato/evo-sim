## Point 3: is X1 = 0.853 an honest result of the arm as registered?

**Yes, as far as the committed outputs can show. No bug in the X1 arm was found.** The evidence:

1. **Code against the registered definition.** `assemble` builds `naive` from `rows[0][3].replace("-", "")` per segment, where `rows[0][3]` is the first chimp row over the columns from the first to the last CDS base of that segment. Chimp bases in reference-gap columns (chimp-only insertions) are therefore kept and chimp gaps dropped, as registered. The concatenation is reverse-complemented for '-' transcripts before padding (`x1 = (x1 + "-" * L)[:L]`), so the comparison starts at the transcript's first codon on both strands. `gene_stats` upper-cases, skips codons containing '-' (the padding) and codons that are not sense codons in either species (internal stops, N). This is the arm as registered.
2. **Internal positive control.** In 16,673 genes the X1 difference counts equal the V0 counts exactly (pooled 0.2054 on both). X1 and V0 reach the same per-gene counts by different code paths (position by position on the concatenation, against column-indexed placement), so a frame, strand or ordering fault in the X1 path would show in these genes. It does not.
3. **Strand.** Reviewer's computation from the gene table: '+' genes 9,776, affected 13.8%, X1 pooled 0.848, share 59.8%; '-' genes 9,534, affected 13.5%, X1 pooled 0.858, share 60.4%. A minus-strand fault would separate these. It does not.
4. **The value in the affected genes is what mispaired codons give.** Affected genes read 0.960 pooled. The L1 correctness review measured pN/pS 0.95 for two unrelated random CDS under the same estimator.
5. **The write-up's reason for the A4 miss is right in substance.** It says: "any unhandled length difference, in frame or not, pairs every downstream codon with a non-homologous one, and a pooled ratio weights genes by their differences." Both halves check. (a) Weighting: 13.7% of genes carry 94.1% of X1's differences. (b) In frame or not: reviewer's computation, 444 affected genes have no internal stop in either species under X1, and they read X1 pooled **1.004** on 337,887 differences, 760 per gene (V0 on the same genes: 0.365 on 11,186). Long mispaired runs with no stop cannot be out of frame, so these are whole-codon shifts, and they read as high as the rest (2,190 affected genes with an X1 chimp stop: 0.952). A4's premise (frameshifting indels are rare) held and did not bear on the outcome, as the write-up says.

Two limits on this judgement are findings 1 and 2: the composition of the "length differences" is not known, and one assumption of the X1 code path could not be checked without the segment dump.

## Summary
The numbers are right: every table cell, every filter value and every post hoc figure the write-up derives from the gene table was reproduced, with one misattributed pair of coverage figures (finding 7) and a two-count rounding difference (finding 12). The band is CRITIC by the registered rule, LOCATED did not fire for the stated reason, and the hit and miss calls follow the registered wording. The run read the whole MAF (block count identical to GAP-07t's), the hashes match, and the script is the registered one. The proposed moves are exactly the registered ones and change no verdict value; the DN3 credit needs the two-pipeline wording of finding 5.

X1's 0.853 is an honest result of the arm as registered. No bug was found, an exact internal control (X1 = V0 in 86.3% of genes) and the strand split argue against one, and the write-up's explanation of the A4 miss is confirmed (whole-codon shifts read 1.00; 13.7% of genes carry 94% of the differences). What is not established is what the misregistration consists of: 40.7% of X1's differences sit in genes missing first-row chimp bases over more than 1% of their CDS, and the ordering assumption of the X1 path was not checkable here. The post hoc X1 wording proposed for the DN1 and DN2 comments should wait for the breakdown from the segment dump (finding 1), and "frame-preserving" should be replaced (finding 3). The registered result does not wait on either.
