## Honest concessions (points against the critics that stand)
- **Unscaled supply: Day holds** (F <= 0.104 in every mode at kappa = 1). Hössjer's mutation-rate-only scaling (125) leaves F < 1 at s >= 0.01.
- **The critics' registered threshold was wrong**: "F > 1 whenever humans recombine and kappa >= 125" fails at s >= 0.01.
- Recombination alone (kappa = 1) does not break the ceiling. A5f's direction holds, but its magnitude needs supply scaling.
- **Adaptive-only, the verdict turns on an unmeasured ratio.** A 430x lower beneficial fraction or effect-size factor at central (7x at one corner) restores the ceiling.
- Capped by Day's cost figures, the per-generation margin shrinks to 2-7x.

## Verdict view (critic steelman)
- Per generation, adaptive-only model: `contested`, leaning against A2e. The flip (central 217) is far below the supply ratio derived from Day's factors (93,659), it does not depend on the recombination mode, and humans exceed it even when treated as clonal. The remaining uncertainty is reducible by data (C3).
- Per generation, on Day's stated total-throughput basis: F = 50,765. That is `contradicted`, conditional on the human neutral rate k = μ (A5c vs Day's s8.2).
- Per year: no A2e verdict; this supports A2i.
- Internal: non-sequitur stands, strengthened by C5.
