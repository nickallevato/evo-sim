# R4-FM: Day's "mesas" (The Fourth Mechanism) replicated on the T2T ape alignment against a heterogeneous-μ null

Status: **three reviews done (1796620); fix pass below (Review resolution); mirror test pre-registered (737648e) and running** (load-bearing tier). Reviews: `REVIEW-R4-FM-correctness.md`, `-steelman-day.md`, `-steelman-critic.md`. Script `research/checks/fm_mesa_replication.py`; pre-registered d723064 (docstring P1-P9, grid, mesa rule and verdict mapping fixed before any data analysis). Run on na-workhorse 2026-10-10: extract 4,144 s (5,366,631 MAF blocks, 24 chromosomes), then analyse. Raw: `raw/fm_main.out`, `raw/fm_analyse.json`, `raw/fm_main.host`, `raw/fm_maf.md5`. The per-1-kb bin file `fm_bins.npz` (74 MB) stays on workhorse, not committed. No post hoc changes to the code or the scores; the fix pass changes wording and reports cells already in the raw output; new tests are in `fm_mirror.py` (post hoc, pre-registered 737648e).

Data: the T2T Primate Consortium 8-way Progressive Cactus alignment exported as an hs1 (T2T-CHM13)-referenced MAF (`8-t2t-apes-2023v2.hs1.maf.gz`, cgl.gi.ucsc.edu; md5 df51e5c3…53b, matches the published .md5). These are the data Day names (Q171). Lineages are counted by parsimony on CHM13 coordinates, with gorilla and Sumatran orangutan as outgroups and hs1 = hg38 required: H (human since the human-chimp ancestor), C (chimp), B (bonobo), G (gorilla terminal), and DHC (unpolarised human-chimp differences). Filters: F0 = all; F1 = no paralogous rows; F2 = F1 without soft-masked repeats; F3 = F2 without CpG. Autosomes unless stated.

**Scope (all verdicts below).** Only hg38- and orangutan-alignable CHM13 sequence is counted: a core column needs an A/C/G/T in hs1, hg38, chimp, bonobo, gorilla and Sumatran orangutan. About 86% of autosomal 1-kb bins pass the coverage rule at F0 (k = 2,501,091); centromeric satellites, rDNA arrays and other CHM13-only sequence are absent from every cell. A window is used if ≥ 50% of it is core columns under the filter, so F2 keeps the repeat-poor third of the genome (100 kb: k = 26,386 F0, 24,687 F1, 9,520 F2, 8,439 F3). The F1 symmetry is the wider evidence (94% of F0 windows); F2 is stricter but narrower. F1 removes real human-specific SD evolution along with paralog artefacts, so the F1 symmetry is conditional on single-copy sequence.

## Reading (revised after the reviews; proposed verdicts for integration)
All verdicts are scoped to hg38- and orangutan-alignable sequence (see Scope). Rule RH: FM3, FM4, FM5 and FM10 are tagged dialectic, FM6 rhetoric, FM8 mixed (refresh 2026-10-10); only dialectic statements are scored.
- **FM3 (overdispersion "more than 80 times").** Internal **`holds`** only in the weak sense that > 80 is reachable with φ at W ≥ 1 Mb (≥ 100 kb for DHC), for every lineage. Fidelity **`unverifiable`** (rule F): the window and the statistic are unreleased, and φ grows roughly linearly with W (H at F0: 3.3, 10.4, 36.9, 136 at 1 kb to 1 Mb), so the same data give 3x or 800x. External **`supported` as a description**: the magnitude is set by the window, and against a null with shared regional rate variation it shrinks to about 2-6 (F2 100 kb: 2.7 H, 2.4 C).
- **FM4 (clustering, "mesas", "sharp boundaries").** Clustering **supported** and shared: 32-53% of human-mesa windows lie inside chimp mesas against a 0.56-1.0% background (32-82x enrichment); the shared-rate null reproduces 64-69% of human mesas at 100 kb; H density correlates with C at 0.84 and with G at 0.66. **"Sharp boundaries" not supported** (post hoc E1, below): human-mesa edges are no sharper than chimp-mesa edges (S_H/S_C 0.35-1.10) or than a 1-kb null at 10 kb, and at 100 kb they are soft (the step at the edge is 0.06-0.22 of the mesa excess). Proposed FM4: clustering `supported` as a description; "sharp boundaries" `contested` (half-steps at 10 kb, S about 0.5, alike for H, C and the null; soft at 100 kb; never sharper for H than for C).
- **FM5 (one mesa exceeds MITTENS and Haldane).** Rule S: Q166 states only that one mesa's human-lineage excess exceeds both ceilings; it draws no inference there. Internal **`holds`** (literal). Fidelity **`unverifiable`** for Day's own Haldane and MITTENS figures (rule F: not given with the mesa), `accurate` on the audit's 840 / 191 reading. External **`supported` as a description, non-discriminating**: 32 human, 29 chimp, 31 bonobo and 19 gorilla mesas exceed 840 (F0 100 kb). "Human-lineage" is not "human-specific", so the earlier "contradicted for any human-specific reading" is withdrawn.
- **FM8b ("dominant source of genomic divergence").** **`contradicted` for single-nucleotide divergence on alignable sequence**: human mesas hold 1.25-2.5% of human-chimp differences (denominators: the cell's used windows). Structural, indel and SD divergence (Yoo 2025's SDRs average 327 Mb per lineage) are not measured; on total divergence this check is silent.
- **FM10 ("neither selection nor drift viable").** The rule-N analysis moves here from FM5: the step from FM3-FM5 to "neither … drift" needs the premise "drift implies a uniform rate". If Day's own material uses lineage- or time-varying rates (MITTENS, relictation), the step contradicts it (N2b); otherwise the conclusion is stronger than its premises (N2d). The integration pass greps the Day corpus for this; internal stays `pending` until then. External: every pre-registered discriminating cell (P3, P4, P5, P7) falls on the critics' side. **The pattern is shared, so it is not evidence for a human-lineage mechanism. A residual φ_res of 2.3-2.7 remains in every lineage (G 4.4) and is unattributed; the shared-rate null borrows whatever the other lineages show (μ, ancestral coalescence depth, alignability) and is not a mechanistic neutral model.** Critic-side, that residual is an upper bound under a better-specified null (lineage differences in CV², gBGC and PRDM9 turnover are neutral and unmodelled); Day-side, a shared-rate null does not show that drift with variable μ produces the mesas. Proposed external: **`contested`, leaning critic**, with the open cells named (autosomal F0 duplicated sequence; chrX F1). If the mirror test scores M1 and M3 as projection artefacts, the proposal becomes `contradicted` for the mesa route on alignable sequence; if H stays heavier on the chimp reference, `contested` stays with a Day-side cell.
- **Day-side point, reworded.** In unmasked data (F0) there is a human-heavy excess **in duplicated sequence as projected onto the human reference**: φ_res H/C 16.2 / 8.2 (100 kb), 34.3 / 18.8 (1 Mb); CV² ratio about 1.6; φ_HC 8.5-24. It vanishes once paralogous rows are removed (F1: 4.48 / 4.08). Two explanations predict it: real human-specific SD divergence (real human-lineage change, which F1 also removes) and an hs1-reference projection artefact (a human-specific duplication puts two reference positions against one ape copy, so paralog differences at the second copy count as H). H lag-1 is lower than C's in F0 (0.47 vs 0.59 at 100 kb) but not in F1, which suggests isolated high windows rather than mesas. The earlier wording ("would see a real human-heavy signal") is **withdrawn pending the mirror test**, which separates the two. Even in F0, chimp-specific mesas outnumber human-specific ones (8 vs 1 at 100 kb; 70 vs 35 at 10 kb).

## Caveats (pre-registered or noted now)
- Parsimony with two outgroups; no ancestral reconstruction from the HAL. CHM13 is one haplotype; hg38 agreement removes only part of human polymorphism.
- No recombination or replication-timing map. The covariates are GC, CpG, discordance and hs1≠hg38.
- The mesa rule and the windows are this audit's. Day's are unreleased, so a different rule could give different counts. It would not change the per-lineage symmetry, which holds in every cell of the grid.
- The heterogeneous-μ null borrows the rate from the other lineages, so it assumes the variation is shared. That is the hypothesis under test, and it is the right null for "human-specific". It is not an independent mechanistic model of μ.
- The F0 human-heavy residual and chrX F1 are followed up in the mirror test (pre-registered 737648e): chimp-referenced re-run of the frozen pipeline, plus SD overlap on hs1. A CHM13-only split is moot: CHM13-only sequence is already excluded from every cell by the hg38 core requirement.
- "Heterogeneous-μ null" is renamed **shared-rate null** in this write-up: it borrows rate variation from μ, ancestral coalescence depth and alignability alike.
- No check that a CHM13 position is the reference row of only one block (uint16 counts); the mirror run asserts the maximum core columns per 1-kb bin (C11).

**Reading of stage B.**
- **F0 autosomal excess (S1):** most of it (60-70% of the H-minus-C residual) sits in the ~6-10% of windows with SD coverage; removing them lowers R from 1.97 to 1.52 (100 kb) and from 1.83 to 1.32 (1 Mb). The rest is spread outside annotated SDs and vanishes in F1. Localisation in SDs does not say whether it is artefact or real human SD evolution; the mirror (M1) decides that.
- **chrX F1 (S2):** not an SD effect. It is one 100-kb window at 57.3 Mb, 98% repeat-masked, with a human residual of 132 SD; without it the chrX ratio is about 1.15. The registered S2 score is a Day hit, and it is reported as such; the substance is a single repeat-dense window, not a chromosome-wide human excess. Whether that window is real human-lineage change or a projection or assembly effect is for M3 (and an annotation check of the window, not registered).
- **Sharp boundaries (E1):** no cell shows human-mesa edges sharper than chimp-mesa edges (S_H/S_C 0.35-1.10), and at 10 kb they are no sharper than the 1-kb null (1.08, 0.99, where that comparator is biased toward Day). At 100 kb the edges are soft: the density 5 kb inside a mesa edge is only 0.06-0.22 of the mesa excess above the density 5 kb outside. Neither side's full criterion is met because the shared-edge fraction (0.20-0.32 at 10 kb) falls between the bounds. FM4's "sharp boundaries" is **not supported**: the measured edges are not step-like at 5 kb resolution, and are no sharper for H than for C.
- **Lineage-specific mesas (E2):** both lineages have more lineage-specific mesas than a shared-rate null gives, chimp more than human (F0 10 kb: H 35 vs null p95 27; C 70 vs 24). That fits lineage-specific regional rate variation (gBGC, PRDM9 turnover, assembly differences) on every branch. It is the registered Day hit, and it does not point to a human-specific mechanism, since the chimp excess is larger.
- **Null calibration (C3):** the shared-rate null procedure is calibrated on synthetic data (M/syn 0.94-1.13), so the correctness C3 concern (noise-inflated null counts) is not borne out; G's high M/obs (1.55-2.16) is a property of the gorilla data (cause not tested), not of the procedure. P5's 10-kb ratios are therefore usable too (H 0.50 and 0.56).
- **K3, K4:** a β-fitted null does not shrink the human residual; the F1/F2 symmetry stands. No mesa rule could make "mesas" hold most of human-chimp divergence: half of DHC needs the densest 36-44% of the genome.
