Status: **three reviews done (1796620); fix pass below (Review resolution); post hoc mirror test and follow-ups pre-registered (737648e), run and scored; combined re-review 43dca91; second fix pass with post hoc `fm_fix2.py` (pre-registered 6aa9bb6), run and scored (last sections)** (load-bearing tier). Run on na-workhorse 2026-10-10: extract 4,144 s (5,366,631 MAF blocks, 24 chromosomes), then analyse. Raw: `raw/fm_main.out`, `raw/fm_analyse.json`, `raw/fm_main.host`, `raw/fm_maf.md5`. The per-1-kb bin file `fm_bins.npz` (74 MB) stays on workhorse, not committed. No post hoc changes to the code or the scores; the fix pass changes wording and reports cells already in the raw output; new tests are in `fm_mirror.py` (post hoc, pre-registered 737648e).

Data: the T2T Primate Consortium 8-way Progressive Cactus alignment exported as an hs1 (T2T-CHM13)-referenced MAF (`8-t2t-apes-2023v2.hs1.maf.gz`, cgl.gi.ucsc.edu; md5 df51e5c3…53b, matches the published .md5). These are the data Day names (Q171). Lineages are counted by parsimony on CHM13 coordinates, with gorilla and Sumatran orangutan as outgroups and hs1 = hg38 required: H (human since the human-chimp ancestor), C (chimp), B (bonobo), G (gorilla terminal), and DHC (unpolarised human-chimp differences). Filters: F0 = all; F1 = no paralogous rows; F2 = F1 without soft-masked repeats; F3 = F2 without CpG. Autosomes unless stated.

**Scope (all verdicts below).** Only hg38- and orangutan-alignable CHM13 sequence is counted: a core column needs an A/C/G/T in hs1, hg38, chimp, bonobo, gorilla and Sumatran orangutan. About 86% of autosomal 1-kb bins pass the coverage rule at F0 (k = 2,501,091); centromeric satellites, rDNA arrays and other CHM13-only sequence are absent from every cell. A window is used if ≥ 50% of it is core columns under the filter, so F2 keeps the repeat-poor third of the genome (100 kb: k = 26,386 F0, 24,687 F1, 9,520 F2, 8,439 F3). The F1 symmetry is the wider evidence (94% of F0 windows); F2 is stricter but narrower. F1 removes real human-specific SD evolution along with paralog artefacts, so the F1 symmetry is conditional on single-copy sequence.

## Reading (revised after the reviews; proposed verdicts for integration)
All verdicts are scoped to hg38- and orangutan-alignable sequence (see Scope). Rule RH: FM3, FM4, FM5 and FM10 are tagged dialectic, FM6 rhetoric, FM8 mixed (refresh 2026-10-10); only dialectic statements are scored.
- **FM3 (overdispersion "more than 80 times").** Internal **`holds`** only in the weak sense that > 80 is reachable with φ at W ≥ 1 Mb (≥ 100 kb for DHC), for every lineage. Fidelity **`unverifiable`** (rule F): the window and the statistic are unreleased, and φ grows roughly linearly with W (H at F0: 3.3, 10.4, 36.9, 136 at 1 kb to 1 Mb), so the same data give 3x or 800x. External **`supported` as a description**: the magnitude is set by the window, and against a null with shared regional rate variation it shrinks to about 2-6 (F2 100 kb: 2.7 H, 2.4 C).
- **FM4 (clustering, "mesas", "sharp boundaries").** Clustering **supported** and shared: 32-53% of human-mesa windows lie inside chimp mesas against a 0.56-1.0% background (32-82x enrichment); the shared-rate null reproduces 64-69% of human mesas at 100 kb; H density correlates with C at 0.84 and with G at 0.66. **"Sharp boundaries": relative reading only.** Post hoc E1: human-mesa edges are no sharper than chimp-mesa edges in the three cells with ≥ 50 edges (S_H/S_C 0.35-1.10; F2 100 kb, 16 edges, is uninformative). Absolute sharpness, calibrated post hoc (T1, fix pass 2) against a perfect step placed at a random position relative to the window grid and run through the same mesa caller and estimator: at 10 kb the observed S is 0.92 (F0) and 0.88 (F2) of the perfect-step value, q = 0.76 and 0.63 on the scale from a 40-kb ramp (0) to a step (1). The registered Day bound (q ≥ 0.75 in both 10-kb cells) is met in F0 and missed in F2; the critic bound (q ≤ 0.25) is missed in both: **indeterminate**. The estimator barely separates a step from a 10-kb ramp (S 0.48-0.51 vs 0.55-0.58), and at 100 kb it cannot separate anything (a perfect step gives S ≈ 0), so the earlier "soft at 100 kb" and "half-steps" wording is **withdrawn** as a resolution artefact (review D-1). Proposed FM4: clustering `supported` as a description; "sharp boundaries" **`pending`** (consistent with steps or ramps up to about 10 kb at 10-kb resolution; graded 40-kb transitions disfavoured in F0 only); H edges no sharper than C (relative, non-discriminating).
- **FM5 (one mesa exceeds MITTENS and Haldane).** Rule S: Q166 states only that one mesa's human-lineage excess exceeds both ceilings; it draws no inference there. Internal **`holds`** (literal). Fidelity **`unverifiable`** for Day's own Haldane and MITTENS figures (rule F: not given with the mesa), `accurate` on the audit's 840 / 191 reading. External **`supported` as a description, non-discriminating**: 32 human, 29 chimp, 31 bonobo and 19 gorilla mesas exceed 840 (F0 100 kb). "Human-lineage" is not "human-specific", so the earlier "contradicted for any human-specific reading" is withdrawn.
- **FM8b ("dominant source of genomic divergence").** **`contradicted` for single-nucleotide divergence on alignable sequence**: human mesas hold 1.25-2.5% of human-chimp differences (denominators: the cell's used windows). Structural, indel and SD divergence (Yoo 2025's SDRs average 327 Mb per lineage) are not measured; on total divergence this check is silent.
- **FM10 ("neither selection nor drift viable").** The rule-N analysis moves here from FM5: the step from FM3-FM5 to "neither … drift" needs the premise "drift implies a uniform rate". If Day's own material uses lineage- or time-varying rates (MITTENS, relictation), the step contradicts it (N2b); otherwise the conclusion is stronger than its premises (N2d). The integration pass greps the Day corpus for this; internal stays `pending` until then. External: every pre-registered discriminating cell (P3, P4, P5, P7) falls on the critics' side. **The pattern is shared, so it is not evidence for a human-lineage mechanism. A residual φ_res of 2.3-2.7 remains in every lineage (G 4.4) and is unattributed; the shared-rate null borrows whatever the other lineages show (μ, ancestral coalescence depth, alignability) and is not a mechanistic neutral model.** Critic-side, that residual is an upper bound under a better-specified null (lineage differences in CV², gBGC and PRDM9 turnover are neutral and unmodelled); Day-side, a shared-rate null does not show that drift with variable μ produces the mesas. Proposed external after the mirror test: **`contested`, leaning critic**. A human-specific autosomal mesa signal is contradicted on alignable sequence (P3, P4, P7, M1, M5; M1: the F0 excess follows the reference); that is a premise of the human-lineage reading, not FM10's conclusion, and it says nothing new on whether drift with variable μ produces the mesas (the D4 sentence above stands). The one Day-side cell (chrX F1 100 kb, M3 R 3.39 on the chimp reference; not at 10 kb, 1.51, or 1 Mb, 1.24) is one orthologous window, CHM13 chrX 57.3 Mb = chimp chrX 58.1 Mb, in the chrX centromeric transition region (fix pass 2, post hoc T2-T4).
- **FM7 (lineage-specific mesas; E2, post hoc).** Registered E2 result: Day hit (human-specific mesas exceed a shared-rate null's 95th percentile in 2 of 4 cells). Non-discriminating: the paired null has no lineage-specific neutral rate variation, which the critics' model includes (gBGC, PRDM9 turnover, assembly differences), and chimp-specific mesas exceed the same null by more (F0 10 kb: H 35 vs p95 27; C 70 vs 24). Proposed: lineage-specific regional excess exists on both branches, larger on chimp, so it does not support a human-lineage mechanism; FM7 external `contested`, leaning critic, unless the integration finds FM7 worded otherwise.
- **Day-side point, reworded.** In unmasked data (F0) there is a human-heavy excess **in duplicated sequence as projected onto the human reference**: φ_res H/C 16.2 / 8.2 (100 kb), 34.3 / 18.8 (1 Mb); CV² ratio about 1.6; φ_HC 8.5-24. It vanishes once paralogous rows are removed (F1: 4.48 / 4.08). Two explanations predict it: real human-specific SD divergence (real human-lineage change, which F1 also removes) and an hs1-reference projection artefact (a human-specific duplication puts two reference positions against one ape copy, so paralog differences at the second copy count as H). H lag-1 is lower than C's in F0 (0.47 vs 0.59 at 100 kb) but not in F1, which suggests isolated high windows rather than mesas. The earlier wording ("would see a real human-heavy signal") is **withdrawn**. The mirror test (M1) found the excess follows the reference: on the chimp-referenced MAF the chimp lineage is heavier (R 0.25 at 100 kb, 0.19 at 1 Mb). Even in F0, chimp-specific mesas outnumber human-specific ones (8 vs 1 at 100 kb; 70 vs 35 at 10 kb).

## Caveats (pre-registered or noted now)
- Parsimony with two outgroups; no ancestral reconstruction from the HAL. CHM13 is one haplotype; hg38 agreement removes only part of human polymorphism.
- No recombination or replication-timing map. The covariates are GC, CpG, discordance and hs1≠hg38.
- The mesa rule and the windows are this audit's. Day's are unreleased, so a different rule could give different counts. It would not change the per-lineage symmetry, which holds in every cell of the grid.
- The heterogeneous-μ null borrows the rate from the other lineages, so it assumes the variation is shared. That is the hypothesis under test, and it is the right null for "human-specific". It is not an independent mechanistic model of μ.
- The F0 human-heavy residual and chrX F1 are followed up in the mirror test (pre-registered 737648e): chimp-referenced re-run of the frozen pipeline, plus SD overlap on hs1. A CHM13-only split is moot: CHM13-only sequence is already excluded from every cell by the hg38 core requirement.
- "Heterogeneous-μ null" is renamed **shared-rate null** in this write-up: it borrows rate variation from μ, ancestral coalescence depth and alignability alike.
- No check that a CHM13 position is the reference row of only one block (uint16 counts); the mirror run asserts the maximum core columns per 1-kb bin (C11).
