## 1. GAP-04: Weissman & Barton finite-map limit

### 1.1 Headline (a bracket, not a single cap)
**The quantities.** W&B give:
- an additive-approximation asymptote of R/2 per generation for fixed s (Eq. 7);
- R/4 for an exponential DFE (Eq. 13);
- simulations that exceed both, "remaining <3" × R even at Λ₀/R = 10³ (Fig. 4).

At R = 35–37.9 M these are 8.75–9.5 (R/4), 17.5–19 (R/2) and ≈ 105–114 per generation (3R).

**Day's stated model** needs 61.5–79 adaptive-or-hitchhiked substitutions per generation (17.5–20M per lineage):
- 6.5–9.1× above R/4;
- 3.3–4.5× above R/2;
- 0.54–0.76 of the simulated maximum.

So it lies above both analytic asymptotes but inside W&B's simulated range. Reaching that range needs a large baseline supply (Λ₀/R ≈ 30–300; §1.6).

**The critics' model.** The asymptotes are crossed only if more than **≈ 13–14% (R/4) to 25–27% (R/2)** of the 17.5M differences are adaptive. The ranges are 16–18% and 33–35% at T = 325,000. The simulated maximum is never crossed (> 150%). Below the crossing, the interference cost is:
- at the top GAP-01 row (10⁶): 17–19% (Eq. 7) to 30–31% (Eq. 13), with fixed supply;
- at ≤ 10⁵: ≤ 4.3%.

Day's own fallback scenario, "Even if 99% of divergence is neutral, 200,000 fixations remain required" (Z18165980 l.66), sits 22–31× below R/2 and 11–15× below R/4.

**What this check covers.** It is about the **interference leg only**. Branch H's hard-selection cap, which is a separate constraint, is 1,200–8,700 adaptive substitutions per lineage (`gaps.md` GAP-01, from R4-H2-hard). K_a ≥ 10⁴ exceeds that cap by 1–830×, and Day's 200,000 exceeds it by 23–167×.

### 1.3 Source (read in full)
Weissman DB, Barton NH (2012), *PLoS Genet* 8(6):e1002740. The PDF is from journals.plos.org; the Europe PMC XML renders equations only as images. All locators were verified by the correctness reviewer.
- **Model** (Table 1, p.2): N haploid individuals; U is the genomic beneficial rate; R is the "Total genetic map length". Λ₀ = 2NU⟨s⟩.
- **Eq. (1)** (p.3), unlinked loci, polygamous Wright–Fisher: Δz̄ = v = ¼ W(4v₀).
- **Eq. (6)–(7)** (p.7): P̄ ≈ 2s(1 − 2ZΛ/R), Z = 1.05 taken as 1; **Λ ≈ Λ₀/(1 + 2Λ₀/R)**.
- **Eq. (8)** (p.7): v/v₀ ≈ (1 − 2v/(sR)) e^{−4v}, with v = Λs.
- **Eq. (13)** (p.12), exponential DFE: Λ ≈ Λ₀/(1 + 4Λ₀/R), "twice" the interference of fixed s. Its large-supply asymptote is R/4.
- **Asymptote** (p.8): "In the limit of a very large density of incoming mutations, Λ₀/R ≫ 1, Eqs. (7) and (8) imply that Λ tends to an 'upper limit' of R/2 … independent of both population size and of the strength of selection."
- **Validity** (p.8): Eq. 7 agrees with simulation "for large populations (N > 10³) up to Λ₀/R ~ 1".
- **Fig. 3 caption** (p.6): at R/s = 10, Eq. (8) "slightly overestimates the amount of interference" (boundary effects).
- **Fig. 4 caption** (p.7): simulated Λ/R "continue to increase above Eq. (8)'s 'upper limit' of 0.5 … remaining <3 even for Λ₀/R = 10³". This was run at s = 0.05, R = 1, N = 10²–10⁶.
- **Growth above R/2** (p.8): "Λ/R should grow at least as fast as ~log(Λ₀/R)/log(Ns), although this remains to be carefully investigated."
- **Fig. 5 caption** (p.9): interference raises the mean sojourn time at intermediate frequencies "by no more than a factor of two". This was simulated at s = 0.1, R = 1, Λ₀/R ≈ 14.5, N = 10⁴–10⁵.

**Correction to the brief.** The cap is not "R × a log factor in N·U_b and s". R/2 (and R/4) has no dependence on N, U or s; the log appears only in the heuristic lower bound on growth above R/2.

**Two definitions of "interference loss" (resolves an apparent conflict with gaps.md).**
- **(a) Fixed supply.** The baseline Λ₀ is set equal to the required rate; the loss is 1 − 1/(1 + cΛ₀/R). This is the table's column.
- **(b) Rate to be realised.** The extra supply needed is captured by the loss cΛ/R. This is what the first pass and pre-registered P7 used: 21–23% (Eq. 7) and 42–45% (Eq. 13) at 10⁶.

`gaps.md`'s "≈ 4–16%" at 0.7–3.4 per generation is **correct under (a)** (3.8–16.3%). The first-pass "should read 4–19%" applied definition (b). It is **retracted**.

### 1.9 Who it helps (credit only for arguments actually made)
**Day, on his stated model (node A).**
- The interference he names exists, and in sexual genomes it is set by map length (Hill–Robertson; "linkage limits parallelism").
- His 17.5–20M requirement is 3.3–4.5× above R/2 and 6.5–9.1× above R/4.
- It is within the simulated range only with a beneficial supply that is implausibly large in most N_e and s cells (§1.6).
- On that model W&B-type theory does not rescue the requirement.

**Against Day, on the same model.**
- The magnitude of "linkage limits parallelism" in W&B is 1,800–25,000× more substitutions than MITTENS allows.
- His own fallback adaptive scenario (200,000) is 22–31× below R/2.
- His block arithmetic undercuts the hitchhiking premise his model rests on (§0; KITTENS).

**Critics** (DarwinZDF42, KITTENS: recombination lets beneficial mutations fix together).
- Supported **on the interference leg, for adaptive shares below ≈ 13–27%**: interference costs ≤ 4.3% at K_a ≤ 10⁵ and ≤ 31% at 10⁶, and the supply needed is ≤ 0.4% of new mutations.
- Not supported: the stronger "removes the limit". A finite, map-length-set asymptote exists.
- The hard-selection leg (branch H) is untested at these rates and already exceeds the audit's H2-hard cap for K_a ≥ 10⁴.

**Day's μ/r "channel capacity" (Z18637297).** The binding quantity in W&B is total map length in Morgans, not per-site μ/r. This bounds that argument; it does not refute it.

### 1.10 Caveats
- W&B is a haploid, new-mutation, constant-s, beneficial-only model, which the paper calls a "best-case scenario for adaptation". It leaves out three things:
  - **Deleterious mutations and background selection** would add interference (helps Day; not quantified).
  - **Standing variation, soft sweeps and polygenic adaptation**, which needs no fixation, reduce the relevance of any fixation-rate cap (helps the critics; not quantified).
  - **Hard selection and the cost of selection** (branch H) are absent.
- Diploid human → 2N_e copies is a mapping, not something W&B derive.
- The map lengths come from secondary reproductions (`gaps.md`). N_e = 10⁴ is the audit's working value; 3.3×10⁴ (Day) and 10⁵–2×10⁵ are shown as sensitivities.
- The Fig. 4 reading and the e^{4Λs} extrapolation are discussed in §1.6.
- The fwdpy11 run with a 35 M map (`gaps.md` proposed check 3) was not done.
- F2 is N = 1000, single s, soft selection, 4 replicates.

## 3. GAP-02: sweep-scan detection window

### 3.1 Headline
**What the window removes.** Expected detectable completed sweeps (E_detect) are computed **at detection power 1**, so every figure is an **upper bound**.
- Day's stated 3,200 sweeps (325,000 generations; the low end of his own block range, Z18452504 l.318) give ≈ **98** within the sourced ~10,000-generation window. That is "dozens to hundreds", which is what he says scans show.
- So the *saturation-by-count* inference does not follow at his stated number.
- At the top of his range (32,000), or with his upper N_e (33,000, giving a ~33,000-generation window), the upper bound is ~1,000–3,250. There the inference is not excluded by arithmetic. It would still need a power-calibrated comparator, which the cited outlier lists are not.

**What the window does not remove.**
- Classic sweeps are rare: Hernandez 2011 finds no excess diversity trough around human-specific amino-acid substitutions.
- If sweeps carried most fixations (Day's hitchhiking-block reading, and some critics' "sweeps fix many neutral loci"), Hernandez (and Murphy 2023, as recorded in `gaps.md`) would be a real constraint on that account.

### 3.7 Answer to the brief: is "absent signatures" evidence against the adaptive count?
- **Against Day's saturation inference.** At his stated 3,200 and the sourced window, the upper bound is ~98: no saturation is expected. At 32,000 or N_e = 33,000 the upper bound reaches ~1,000–3,250. Whether that would "saturate" anything depends on detection power, and on comparing with a list that is not threshold-limited. The cited scans are top-1% lists of mostly incomplete sweeps, so they cannot saturate by construction.
- **Against a coding-scale adaptive count (10³–10⁴).** Not evidence. Against the scan lists it is consistent at any power. Against Yoo's SFS counts:
  - 10³ is consistent;
  - 10⁴ is 5–18× above the ape median at power 1, so it is consistent only if power for completed sweeps is ≲ 0.2–0.5. Given Akey's 4N_e·s ≈ 400 cut and Uricchio's 72% weak share, that is plausible, but it is not shown.
- **Against large strong-sweep counts (≥ 10⁵, high noncoding share).** The constraint is Hernandez's trough test, not scan counts. Coverage gives a softer signal: 13–100% of the genome at power 1, against Hernandez's cited ~10%.
- **What neutral theory with the window predicts.** Most differences fixed by drift leave no sweep signature. A few hundred or fewer detectable completed sweeps is expected, so absence of thousands is predicted anyway.
- **Day's strongest variant.** If sweeps carry most fixations (his §8.2 "the remainder are hitchhikers", or DarwinZDF42's "sweeps … fix many loci at once, most of them neutral"), the diversity data are a genuine problem for that account. That problem lands on any critic who leans on hitchhiking as well as on Day's own model.

### 3.9 Caveats
- E_detect is an upper bound (power 1). Power depends on s (only 4N_e·s ≳ 400 is detected well), the statistic, sample size and demography.
- **Demographic confounding.** Bottlenecks and expansions distort the SFS and haplotype nulls in both directions, so "Tajima's D close to zero genome-wide" is not by itself a test of sweeps.
- **Background selection** both mimics sweeps and lowers power (Hernandez attributes the exon troughs largely to it).
- **Polygenic adaptation** needs no sweep.
- **Inputs:** the window is a single sourced number; 4,000 and 20,000 are unsourced; q_soft is assumed; the Hernandez extrapolation assumes a constant rate; scan lists are FDR-uncontrolled.
- **Untested:** Day's bullets on diversity uniformity, Tajima's D and bullet (6) (clustering of fixed differences, which does not decay with time).
